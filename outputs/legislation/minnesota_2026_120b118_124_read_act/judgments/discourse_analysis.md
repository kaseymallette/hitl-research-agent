## B2. Silencing

**Two bounded absence claims confirmed; zero not confirmed.** Counting is by substantive claim/observation, not by every noun in a list. Counter-examples are reported separately and are not counted as claimed absences.

1. **FOS 4: explicit background-knowledge building as a basis for comprehension — confirmed at the stated scope.** The comprehension definition specifies cognitive strategies, intentional thinking, and text–reader interaction, not building topic/background knowledge. The rest of the document does not expressly supply that formulation. There are related provisions: content mastery/access in 120B.12, subd. 4(b)(5); knowledge gaps in personal learning plans in subd. 3(b); vocabulary growth in 120B.119, subd. 17; and school-library use in 120B.12, subd. 4a(a)(14). These limit any stronger inference of substantive exclusion. The observation already disavows document-wide exclusion and recognizes content mastery.
2. **FOS 7: poverty, class size, racial segregation, and unequal school funding as named causes or conditions to change — confirmed at the stated scope.** These do not appear as explanations of reading outcomes or specified structural changes in the intervention menu or elsewhere in the supplied document. Funding does appear in 120B.12, subd. 4a(a)(11)–(12), and 120B.121 concerns Head Start, at-risk children, achievement gaps, community participation, and supplemental nonstate funds. These provisions prevent treating resources, disadvantage, or community conditions as entirely absent. They do not explicitly identify the named structural conditions as causes. The analysis's qualified, nonexclusive-menu reading is supported; an instruction-only account of the entire document would not be.

**Counter-examples confirmed:** FOS 4 for comprehension; FOS 5 for multilingual needs; FOS 6 for oral language and writing (three observations with counter-example components). Further corroboration includes 120B.121(a)(3)–(4), (8), which requires writing-rich environments, home-language support, and information for English-learner parents, and 120B.12, subd. 6, which gives immersion-program communities an advisory role. No claimed absence is contradicted at the limited scope actually asserted.

The observation itself recognizes that limitation. Retain **PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap (qualified)** as its better code, with the supplied alternative explanation of common professional standards; drop PRE-2. This is a coding overreach rather than a false statutory statement.

The following are opportunities from **my own reading**, not mandatory omissions or evidence that the analysis's chosen observations are false:

- **120B.121 (pp. 9–10)** receives no dedicated observation despite lying within the declared scope. Its explicit premise of active parents, staff development, and early-literacy standards would strengthen PRE; “at-risk children,” achievement-gap language, community stakeholders, home-language support, and writing-rich classrooms would extend SUB and FOS.
- **120B.12, subd. 6 (p. 9)** makes families, native speakers, educators, and special-education expertise participants in an immersion advisory committee. This adds collective voice to SUB's narrower parent opt-out account and institutional evidence to FOS's multilingual counter-example.
- **120B.119, subd. 2a (p. 1)** requires vendor certification, recertification, good standing, and fidelity to vendor materials. Alongside **120B.123, subd. 5a(a)–(d) (pp. 13–14)** on licensure and employer-paid training, this would broaden the account of educator regulation, financial protection, and private vendor authority.
- **120B.124, subds. 2(a) and 5(a) (pp. 16–17)** require equal review cycles for alternatives to sound-based approaches. These complement the sign-language observation with an explicit accommodation in material review. Subd. 1(a)(8) also makes geographically equitable training an implementation duty.
- **120B.118, subd. 2 (p. 1)** aligns the entire curriculum/materials/practices/training apparatus with science of reading. This offers a compact additional LEX/PRE passage about the breadth of the policy's scientific warrant.
