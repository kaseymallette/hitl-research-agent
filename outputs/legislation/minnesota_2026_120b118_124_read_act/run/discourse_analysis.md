# Discourse analysis: Minnesota Read Act

The observations concern the supplied Minnesota Statutes 2026 text, sections 120B.118 through 120B.124, including 120B.12. The incomplete neighboring provisions are outside this analysis. The codebook's definitions guide coding; its unverified examples are not evidence about this document.

## LEX — Lexical choices and definitional framing

### LEX observation 1: Evidence is defined through success and instructional components

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 4.
- **Passage:** “"Evidence-based" means the instruction or item described is based on reliable, trustworthy, and valid evidence and has demonstrated a record of success in increasing students' reading competency in the areas of phonological and phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary development, reading fluency, and reading comprehension.” “Evidence-based literacy instruction is explicit, systematic, and includes phonological and phonemic awareness, phonics and decoding, spelling, fluency, vocabulary, oral language, and comprehension that can be differentiated to meet the needs of individual students.”
- **Code:** LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor; LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language (counter-example).
- **Reading:** The five-component list supplies an evidentiary foundation, while the second sentence expands instructional content to spelling and oral language. Evidence is simultaneously an empirical warrant, expressed through validity and demonstrated success, and a specified way of teaching. The adjectives do not supply a study-design threshold, but the term is not left undefined: component, method, and differentiation criteria constrain its meaning. The National Reading Panel is not named.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the definitional work and the expanded list. The document does not establish how competing evidence would be weighed in practice.

### LEX observation 2: Structured literacy translates science into a teaching approach

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 15.
- **Passage:** “Structured literacy is characterized by the provision of systematic, explicit, sequential, and diagnostic instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary and oral language development, and reading comprehension.” “This approach is consistent with the principles identified in the science of reading and is designed to ensure all students develop strong foundational literacy skills.”
- **Code:** LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition.
- **Reading:** The sequence of descriptors defines legitimate instruction through its organization and delivery, beyond a list of content. Consistency with science then warrants that organization, making structured literacy an actionable expression of scientific authority. This fits the code's descriptor-based branch; the statute does not invoke the International Dyslexia Association or adopt its definition by name.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain. The relationship asserted is consistency with scientific principles, not an explicit statement that structured literacy exhausts all reading research.

### LEX observation 3: Comprehension combines component skills with active meaning-making

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 14.
- **Passage:** “"Reading comprehension" means a function of word recognition skills and language comprehension skills.” “It is an active process that requires intentional thinking during which meaning is constructed through interactions between the text and reader.”
- **Code:** LEX-3: Simple View / Research Body (partial).
- **Reading:** The first sentence gives comprehension a two-component architecture; the next describes the reader as actively constructing meaning. These formulations keep language comprehension and reader activity inside the statutory definition rather than reducing reading to accurate word recognition. The fit is partial because the text says “a function,” not a mathematical product, defines comprehension rather than all reading, and names neither a model nor its authors.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about identification with a specific theoretical model. The same wording can be read simply as a statutory account of interacting skills, without an intended citation to the Simple View.

### LEX observation 4: Exclusion makes the evidence boundary enforceable

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivisions 4 and 16; 120B.12, subdivision 3(c).
- **Passage:** “Evidence-based instruction does not include the three-cueing system, as defined in subdivision 16.” “"Three-cueing system," also known as "meaning structure visual (MSV)," means a method that teaches students to use meaning, structure and syntax, and visual cues when attempting to read an unknown word.” “Starting in the 2026-2027 school year, a district must use only evidence-based literacy interventions.”
- **Code:** LEX-5: Method Prohibition Language.
- **Reading:** Naming and defining the excluded method closes an interpretive route by which it might otherwise be claimed as evidence-based. Read with the exclusive intervention requirement, a definition performs regulatory work without needing a separately worded ban in that paragraph. The exclusion concerns the defined unknown-word method; it should not be extended to all uses of meaning or syntax, which the comprehension definition itself accommodates.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the defined exclusion and its connection to mandatory evidence-based interventions.

## PRE — Presuppositions

### PRE observation 1: Universal capability is the premise, with differentiated goals

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 9; 120B.12, subdivision 1(a).
- **Passage:** “The MTSS framework provides access to layered tiers of culturally and linguistically responsive, evidence-based practices and relies on the understanding and belief that every student can learn and thrive.” “The legislature seeks to have every child reading at or above grade level every year, beginning in kindergarten, and to support multilingual learners and students receiving special education services in achieving their individualized reading goals in order to meet grade-level benchmarks.”
- **Code:** PRE-1: Universal Learnability (qualified).
- **Reading:** Universal capacity is explicitly named as the framework's underlying belief rather than established through an argument. The legislative goal makes grade-level proficiency the common horizon, while individualized goals qualify how some students are to reach it. The text therefore joins universal expectation to differentiated support rather than stating an identical immediate trajectory for everyone.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the stated belief and qualification. “Seeks” expresses an aspiration, not proof that a particular intervention guarantees universal proficiency.

### PRE observation 2: Teacher learning is made a necessary route to the literacy goal

- **Where:** 120B.12, subdivision 1(b); 120B.123, subdivision 5(g).
- **Passage:** “To meet this goal, each district must provide teachers and instructional support staff with responsibility for teaching reading with training on evidence-based reading instruction that is approved by the Department of Education by the deadlines provided in section 120B.123, subdivision 5.” “An educator who was first enrolled in an elementary, special education, or early childhood education Minnesota-approved teacher preparation program on or after June 1, 2026, does not need to receive additional training according to paragraph (a).”
- **Code:** PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap (qualified); PRE-2: Instructional Deficit (partial).
- **Reading:** “To meet this goal” makes approved teacher learning an assumed enabling condition of improved reading. The preparation-cohort exemption differentiates presumed training needs, rather than presenting every educator as equally uninformed. Instructional capacity is a privileged remedy, but neither passage establishes that teachers lack knowledge individually or that instruction is the primary cause of all low achievement.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain. The requirement may presuppose a knowledge gap, but it may also establish a common professional standard without judging existing expertise. The causal claim in PRE-2 remains only partial and inferential.

### PRE observation 3: Proven effectiveness is a premise of entitlement

- **Where:** 120B.12, subdivision 1(a).
- **Passage:** “Students must receive evidence-based instruction that is proven to effectively teach children to read, consistent with sections 120B.118 to 120B.124.”
- **Code:** PRE-4: Research Consensus (partial).
- **Reading:** “Proven” presents instructional effectiveness as sufficiently established to ground a mandatory student entitlement. The sentence supplies no study, competing interpretation, or account of uncertainty. It gives the policy epistemic confidence, but confidence in qualifying instruction is narrower than an assertion that the entire research field is unanimous or finished.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain. This could be a threshold requiring demonstrated effectiveness rather than a presupposition of settled research consensus. The stronger, field-wide version of PRE-4 is not warranted here.

### PRE observation 4: The department is authorized to decide what qualifies

- **Where:** 120B.123, subdivision 7(b); 120B.124, subdivision 1(a)(1).
- **Passage:** “The Department of Education must partner with CAREI as required under section 120B.124 to approve professional development programs, subject to final determination by the department.” “A district is not required to use an approved curriculum;”
- **Code:** PRE-5: Legislative Competence (qualified).
- **Reading:** The approval clause treats the agency's authority to adjudicate qualifying professional development as given, and the final-determination phrase locates ultimate judgment there despite the research partnership. The curriculum exception limits the reach of that centralized authority: state evaluation does not automatically require district adoption of a listed product. Defining valid practice and prescribing a particular curriculum are distinct exercises of authority in this document.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the assigned jurisdiction and its limit; this is an institutional presupposition inferred from operative language, not an explicit legislative defense of its own competence.

## FOS — Foregrounding and silencing

### FOS observation 1: Screening organizes the response to reading difficulty

- **Where:** 120B.123, subdivision 1(a); 120B.124, subdivision 1(a)(7).
- **Passage:** “A district must administer an approved evidence-based reading screener to students in kindergarten through grade 3 within the first six weeks of the school year, by February 15 each year, and again within the last six weeks of the school year.” “develop MTSS model plans that districts may adopt to support efforts to screen, identify, intervene, and monitor the progress of students not reading at grade level;”
- **Code:** FOS-3: Assessment / Screening Foregrounded.
- **Reading:** The precise recurring timetable gives assessment a routine place in every early-grade student's school year. The ordered verbs then organize difficulty as something made visible by screening, classified, acted on, and monitored. MTSS model adoption remains optional, but the quoted screening duty is mandatory; those modal differences prevent treating the whole sequence as one uniform mandate.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain.

### FOS observation 2: Dyslexia receives dedicated institutional visibility

- **Where:** 120B.122, subdivision 1.
- **Passage:** “The department must employ a dyslexia specialist to provide technical assistance for dyslexia and related disorders and to serve as the primary source of information and support for schools in addressing the needs of students with dyslexia and related disorders.”
- **Code:** FOS-2: Dyslexia Foregrounded (qualified).
- **Reading:** A named condition receives a dedicated statewide post and a designated primary information source. This makes dyslexia an organizing category of expertise rather than an incidental example of difficulty. The qualification matters: the Act also organizes support around grade-level reading, multilingual needs, and broader literacy specialists, so this provision does not make dyslexia the explanation for every struggling reader.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about institutional foregrounding; an exclusive dyslexia-centered framework would overstate the passage.

### FOS observation 3: Foundational skills have priority within a broader instructional mandate

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 6; 120B.12, subdivision 1(a).
- **Passage:** “"Foundational reading skills" includes phonological and phonemic awareness, phonics and decoding, and fluency.” “By the 2026-2027 school year, districts must provide evidence-based reading instruction through a focus on student mastery of the foundational reading skills of phonemic awareness, phonics, and fluency, as well as the development of oral language, vocabulary, and reading comprehension skills.”
- **Code:** FOS-1: Phonics / Decoding Foregrounded (qualified).
- **Reading:** “Foundational” and “mastery” give the word-level cluster a privileged organizing status, while “as well as” adds the other areas to that focus. The statutory emphasis is on a cluster including fluency and phonemic awareness, not phonics alone. Because the same sentence expressly requires language and comprehension development, priority should not be recoded as their exclusion or as a requirement to postpone them.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about the strength of the hierarchy: “as well as” can indicate equal inclusion. The narrower foundational category and explicit focus nevertheless support a qualified foregrounding reading.

### FOS observation 4: Comprehension is present; knowledge-building is less explicit

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 14; 120B.12, subdivision 4(b)(5).
- **Passage:** “Comprehension skills are taught explicitly by demonstrating, explaining, modeling, and implementing specific cognitive strategies to help beginning readers derive meaning through intentional, problem-solving thinking processes.” “licensed teachers are trained in culturally responsive pedagogy that enables students to master content, develop skills to access content, and build relationships.”
- **Code:** FOS-4: Background Knowledge / Comprehension Silenced (counter-example for comprehension; partial for background knowledge).
- **Reading:** The comprehension definition explicitly specifies instruction, so comprehension silencing is contradicted. Within that account of how comprehension is taught, cognitive strategies and problem-solving receive attention, while building topic or background knowledge is not named. The staff-development list's attention to mastering and accessing content limits that absence claim: content is present, though not expressly formulated as a knowledge-building basis for comprehension.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about background-knowledge silencing. Content mastery and the broader definition's language-comprehension component could encompass it. The defensible observation is limited explicit specification in this instructional account, not document-wide exclusion.

### FOS observation 5: Multilingual learners' languages enter the requirements

- **Where:** 120B.12, subdivision 2(b)(3)–(4) and subdivision 4(b)(4).
- **Passage:** “if the program provides instruction in foundational reading skills in the partner language, the students receiving that instruction must be screened in the partner language;” “if no screener is available in the partner language, the districts must identify how students' reading proficiency is assessed and how the districts determine and provide targeted reading instruction in the partner language and supports to students identified as needing additional support in developing mastery of foundational reading skills;” “licensed teachers recognize students' diverse needs in cross-cultural settings and are able to serve the oral language and linguistic needs of students who are multilingual learners by maximizing strengths in their native languages in order to cultivate students' English language development, including academic language development, and build academic literacy;”
- **Code:** FOS-5: English Learners Silenced (counter-example).
- **Reading:** These screening conditions make the language of instruction consequential, including a required explanation when a tool is unavailable. The training requirement names native-language strengths as a resource rather than treating multilingualism only as an obstacle. The stated developmental direction includes English, but the specific partner-language assessment and instruction duties prevent an English-only reading of the provisions.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the counter-example. These requirements do not by themselves establish adoption of every multilingual pedagogical approach.

### FOS observation 6: Oral language, sign language, and writing are expressly included

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 10; 120B.12, subdivision 4(b)(1).
- **Passage:** “Oral language also includes sign language, in which speaking and listening skills are defined as expressive and receptive skills, and consists of phonology, including sign language phonological awareness, morphology, syntax, semantics, and pragmatics.” “elementary teachers are able to implement explicit, systematic, evidence-based instruction in the five reading areas of phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension with emphasis on mastery of foundational reading skills as defined in section 120B.119 and other literacy-related areas including writing until the student achieves grade-level reading and writing proficiency;”
- **Code:** FOS-7: Oral Language / Writing Silenced (counter-example).
- **Reading:** The definition broadens oral language beyond speech and hearing, making expressive and receptive sign-language skills legible within the framework. The staff-development list expressly extends instruction to writing and sets a writing-proficiency endpoint alongside reading. These are direct counter-examples in places that define language and specify instructional capacity, although writing is described as an additional literacy-related area rather than given the same definitional detail as reading.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain.

### FOS observation 7: The intervention menu gives structural conditions little explanatory space

- **Where:** 120B.12, subdivision 3(a); subdivision 4a(a)(11).
- **Passage:** “Intervention may include but is not limited to requiring student attendance in summer school, intensified reading instruction that may require that the student be removed from the regular classroom for part of the school day, extended-day programs, or programs that strengthen students' cultural connections.” “how the district used funding provided under the Read Act to implement the requirements of the Read Act;”
- **Code:** FOS-6: Structural Causes Silenced (qualified).
- **Reading:** Where the statute lists interventions for students below grade level, instructional intensity, time, placement, and cultural connection occupy the remedy space. Poverty, class size, racial segregation, and unequal school funding are not named there as conditions to change. This is a bounded omission from a remedy menu, not evidence that the statute asserts those conditions are irrelevant. The plan's funding-accountability requirement also prevents a claim that financial resources are entirely absent: money is treated here as an implementation input rather than an explanation of reading outcomes.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain. The menu is expressly nonexclusive and concerns targeted reading intervention; its scope may explain the omission without implying denial of structural causes.

## SUB — Subject positioning

### SUB observation 1: Educators are accountable learners, with differentiated treatment of delay

- **Where:** 120B.123, subdivision 5a(e).
- **Passage:** “An educator that fails to complete the required professional development within a vendor's subscription window due to medical, personal, or family leave or for reasons tied to a learning disability, must not be held financially responsible for the costs of extending the training.” “An educator that fails to complete the required professional development within a vendor's subscription window for reasons of insubordination or willful refusal to comply with state and district directives regarding the professional development are subject to the disciplinary procedures outlined in their collective bargaining agreement or set by their hiring charter school or cooperative.”
- **Code:** SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated (qualified).
- **Reading:** The passage makes training compliance an employment obligation, distinguishing protected delay from culpable refusal. “Insubordination” gives nonparticipation an authority-and-discipline vocabulary rather than treating it simply as pedagogical disagreement. Regulation is explicit; deficiency is not. Financial protection and reference to established disciplinary procedures qualify a reading of teachers as unconditionally sanctionable.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about regulation; uncertain about imputing deficient knowledge, since the passage addresses completion and conduct rather than what an educator knows.

### SUB observation 2: Educators also hold expertise and receive sustained support

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 8; 120B.123, subdivision 7(g).
- **Passage:** “"Literacy lead" means a literacy specialist with expertise in working with educators as adult learners.” “The department must provide ongoing coaching, mentoring, and support to certified trained facilitators.”
- **Code:** SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional.
- **Reading:** “Adult learners” positions educator learning as a specialized professional practice, while the literacy lead is an expert who supports other educators. The ongoing-support duty places responsibility on the department as well as on training recipients. This is a relational learning infrastructure alongside the compliance regime, although the quoted language does not give educators unrestricted authority to select instructional methods.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain.

### SUB observation 3: Students receive continuing support and protection from automatic retention

- **Where:** 120B.12, subdivision 3(a)–(b).
- **Passage:** “If a student does not read at or above grade level by the end of the current school year, the district must continue to provide aligned and targeted reading intervention as defined by the MTSS framework until the student reads at grade level.” “The personal learning plan may include grade retention, if it is in the student's best interest; a student may not be retained solely due to delays in literacy or not demonstrating grade-level proficiency.” “This paragraph does not apply to a student under an individualized education program.”
- **Code:** SUB-3: Student as Victim / Beneficiary (qualified).
- **Reading:** Falling below the benchmark triggers a continuing district duty rather than ending eligibility for assistance. The student is positioned as a beneficiary of intervention and an object of proficiency monitoring, but literacy status alone cannot justify retention. The final sentence limits the personal-learning-plan paragraph's scope; its retention provision should not be generalized beyond that scope. These passages do not explicitly cast students as victims of prior instructional harm.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about beneficiary positioning and the stated limits; a victim narrative would require evidence not supplied here.

### SUB observation 4: Parents have bounded decision-making authority

- **Where:** 120B.12, subdivision 2(c) and subdivision 2a(c).
- **Passage:** “A parent, in consultation with a teacher, may opt a student out of the literacy screener if the parent and teacher decide that continuing to screen would not be beneficial to the student.” “In such limited cases, the student must continue to receive progress monitoring and literacy interventions.” “A district may not use this section to deny a student's right to a special education evaluation.”
- **Code:** SUB-4: Parent as Rights-Holder (qualified).
- **Reading:** Within the older-student screening provision, parents can participate in a consequential decision rather than merely receive results. The authority depends on a joint judgment with a teacher and does not permit withdrawal of monitoring or intervention. The special-education sentence separately protects a student's evaluation right, constraining the district's use of this literacy procedure; it should not be redescribed as an unrestricted parental veto over instruction.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain.

### SUB observation 5: Districts report to the state but retain material-selection discretion

- **Where:** 120B.12, subdivision 4a(b); 120B.124, subdivision 1(a)(1) and (10).
- **Passage:** “Annually by June 15, the district must post its literacy plan on the official school district website and submit it to the commissioner of education using the template developed by the commissioner of education.” “A district is not required to use an approved curriculum;” “A district is not required to use an approved intervention model.”
- **Code:** SUB-5: District as Compliance Target (qualified).
- **Reading:** The deadline, public posting, and common template make districts answerable through a state-defined reporting form. The two exceptions preserve discretion over listed curricula and intervention models within the Act's evidence requirements. Districts are therefore both accountable implementers and selectors; compulsory reporting does not entail compulsory purchase from an approval list.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain.

### SUB observation 6: State approval regulates publishers without alleging culpability

- **Where:** 120B.124, subdivision 5(a).
- **Passage:** “A publisher may submit curriculum or intervention materials for review.” “The publisher is responsible for paying the cost of the review directly to a partnering institution of higher education or third party.” “The department and its partner may approve the curriculum or intervention materials if they determine that the curriculum or intervention materials are evidence-based, focused on structured literacy, culturally and linguistically responsive, and reflect diverse populations.”
- **Code:** SUB-6: Publisher / Curriculum as Suspect (qualified); SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter (qualified).
- **Reading:** Publishers are applicants who bear review costs; the department and reviewer confer approved status by applying multiple criteria. Scientific alignment, cultural responsiveness, and representation all enter the standard of legitimacy. This fits the approval-gatekeeping portions of the codes, but the provision does not accuse publishers of causing failure or narrate the state as rescuing students from them. Submission is permissive, and the optional-list-use provisions elsewhere limit approval's market authority.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the arbiter role; uncertain about “suspect,” because routine quality assurance is a reasonable alternative to a distrust reading.

## INT — Intertextuality

The principal connections are to implementing institutions, administrative standards, and other law. No named federal reading report, professional organization's reading definition, specific research study, other state's reform, or media report supplies an explicit evidentiary warrant in the analyzed sections. The observations below therefore use “no code fits” rather than treating generic research language as a citation or importing the codebook's examples.

### INT observation 1: Research authority enters through an institution rather than a cited study

- **Where:** 120B.119, subdivision 2; 120B.123, subdivision 7(b).
- **Passage:** “"CAREI" means the Center for Applied Research and Educational Improvement at the University of Minnesota.” “The Department of Education must partner with CAREI as required under section 120B.124 to approve professional development programs, subject to final determination by the department.”
- **Code:** no code fits.
- **Reading:** Naming a university research center establishes an institutional connection through which expertise enters approval. The statute incorporates participation and judgment, not a specified publication's findings; final agency determination also distinguishes research participation from autonomous research authority. INT-2 would require an adopted professional or advocacy standard, and INT-4 a research citation; neither is supplied by naming this partner.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the institutional connection. It would be uncertain to infer any particular research position or study from the center's name.

### INT observation 2: Other law and administrative standards bound implementation

- **Where:** 120B.123, subdivision 8(a)–(b); 120B.124, subdivision 5(a).
- **Passage:** “All professional development and digital curriculum resources must comply with the accessibility standards developed according to section 16E.03, subdivision 9.” “Professional development provided in accordance with the Read Act is subject to the requirements of section 363A.43.” “The department may modify the Read Act rubric as necessary to reflect the evolving needs of grade levels and special populations, including alignment with the Minnesota English Language Arts standards, the incorporation of culturally and linguistically responsive criteria, and the inclusion of criteria reflective of diverse populations.”
- **Code:** no code fits.
- **Reading:** Cross-references place the literacy project within a wider legal and standards framework: qualifying implementation must satisfy more than the reading-method definition. The mutable rubric links review to an evolving set of needs and state curricular standards. These are operative incorporations and alignment directions, not historical reading-policy precedents under INT-6. The contents of the referenced law and standards are not reproduced here and cannot be inferred beyond these stated relationships.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain.

### INT observation 3: National research is invoked as possible family information, not specified proof

- **Where:** 120B.12, subdivision 2a(b).
- **Passage:** “The dual language immersion program may provide information about national research on reading proficiency for students in dual language immersion programs in the parent notification.”
- **Code:** no code fits.
- **Reading:** Research enters as a permitted resource for explaining immersion reading proficiency to families. The provision opens the notification to a research discourse without naming a study, prescribing its findings, or using it as proof for the Act's instructional requirements. It therefore does not meet INT-4's specific-citation criterion, and “may” should not be converted into a research-information mandate.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the limited invocation; the content and intended conclusions of that research remain unspecified.
