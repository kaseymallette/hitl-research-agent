# Discourse analysis: Arkansas Code § 6-17-429 (2025)

## LEX — Lexical choices and definitional framing

### LEX observation 1: A field of study becomes a governing term

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(n)(1), p. 15.
- **Passage:** ““Science of reading” means the study of the relationship between cognitive science and educational outcomes; and”
- **Code:** LEX-3 (partial).
- **Reading:** The definition locates authority in a relationship between a scientific field and educational results. It defines an activity of study rather than a curriculum, a list of skills, or a particular finding. This fits the research orientation of LEX-3 only partly: neither a body of research nor the Simple View is expressly named. Its breadth leaves the earlier requirements for alignment dependent on administrative interpretation.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain as a code fit. This could be read simply as a broad statutory definition of a field, without the accumulated-research meaning that LEX-3 describes.

### LEX observation 2: Instruction defined through its manner

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(n)(2), p. 16.
- **Passage:** ““Structured literacy” means an approach by which licensed personnel teach reading in an explicit, systematic, cumulative, and diagnostic manner.”
- **Code:** LEX-2.
- **Reading:** Four descriptors make the manner of teaching central to the definition. The reference to licensed personnel also connects the approach to an authorized professional role. These same descriptors govern approved materials in (f), carrying the definition into purchasing criteria. The text does not attribute this definition to the International Dyslexia Association.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the procedural framing and code fit; the passage supplies no basis for attributing the wording to an unnamed organization.

### LEX observation 3: Five components as intervention content

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(k)(2)(B)(v), p. 12.
- **Passage:** “The intensive, evidence-based literacy intervention program aligned to the science of reading the student's teacher will use to address the areas of phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension;”
- **Code:** LEX-1 (qualified).
- **Reading:** The five components make an otherwise broad alignment requirement operational: they specify what the planned intervention will address. Their inclusion anchors required intervention content without replacing the separate definition in (n)(1). The National Reading Panel is not named, and the list establishes no explicit hierarchy among its components.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the five-component anchor; qualified because this is an intervention requirement rather than the definition of reading science for the whole section.

### LEX observation 4: Evidence as an approval label

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(f)(3) and (g)(2)(B), p. 4.
- **Passage:** “Evidence-based reading programs that are grounded in the science of reading.” Also: “Evidence-based research regarding the alternative curriculum program.”
- **Code:** LEX-4 (qualified).
- **Reading:** Evidence language legitimates programs and assigns off-list purchasers the task of supplying a research warrant. These clauses specify no study design, evidence threshold, or named research source. Yet the standard is not wholly unspecified: (f) adds instructional descriptors, (n) defines terms, and (g)(2) creates an approval process. The unresolved issue is what evidence qualifies within that process.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain that the quoted clauses leave evidentiary thresholds unstated; qualified because procedural and pedagogical criteria appear elsewhere in the section.

### LEX observation 5: Drawing a boundary through prohibition

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(i)(3)(A), (i)(3)(A)(i)-(ii), pp. 5-6.
- **Passage:** “A public school district or an open-enrollment public charter school shall not use a program of instruction for students in kindergarten through grade two (K-2) that is based in any practice or intervention program that utilizes:” The listed items include “The three-cueing system model of reading;” and “Visual memory as the primary basis for teaching word recognition; or”.
- **Code:** LEX-5.
- **Reading:** The negative command defines an instructional boundary through named practices, supplementing the affirmative definitions and approval list. The restriction on visual memory is specifically about its use as the primary basis for word recognition. The K-2 scope also matters: the passage does not state a prohibition for every grade or a ban on teaching meaning or comprehension.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the named exclusions and their stated scope.

The section contains no legislative findings or crisis declaration. Implementation deadlines alone do not establish LEX-6.

## PRE — Presuppositions

### PRE observation 1: Expertise requires a prescribed demonstration

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(d)(2), p. 3.
- **Passage:** “All teachers shall demonstrate proficiency or awareness in knowledge and practices of scientific reading instruction as is applicable to their teaching position by completing the prescribed proficiency or awareness in knowledge and practices of scientific reading instruction credential either:”
- **Code:** PRE-3 (qualified).
- **Reading:** Existing teacher status is insufficient as evidence of the required knowledge; a prescribed credential makes that knowledge administratively recognizable. The distinction between proficiency and awareness differentiates expectations by role. The provision can suggest an unverified knowledge gap, but it does not establish that all teachers actually lack this knowledge; (d)(3) recognizes prior approved demonstration for a specified endorsement exception.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about the deficit presupposition. A reasonable alternative is that this standardizes verification of knowledge teachers may already possess.

### PRE observation 2: Instruction is the route from diagnosis to improvement

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(k)(2)(B)(v) and (vii), p. 12.
- **Passage:** “The intensive, evidence-based literacy intervention program aligned to the science of reading the student's teacher will use to address the areas of phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension;” Also: “Any additional services the student's teacher determines are available and appropriate to accelerate the student's reading skill development;”.
- **Code:** PRE-2 (partial).
- **Reading:** The plan translates diagnosed needs into an instructional program and additional services intended to accelerate development. This presents instruction as an actionable remedy, making the teacher's selection of appropriate services consequential. It does not expressly say that inadequate prior instruction caused the difficulty, so the causal claim in PRE-2 is supported only indirectly.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain. The same provisions can be read as specifying a school's responsibilities without asserting the primacy of instructional causes.

### PRE observation 3: The state can judge instructional validity

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(g)(2), p. 4.
- **Passage:** “A public school district or open-enrollment public charter school that chooses to purchase a curriculum program that is not from the division's approved list of curriculum programs shall submit the following to the division for approval of the alternative curriculum program:”
- **Code:** PRE-5 (qualified).
- **Reading:** The provision treats the division's authority to approve instructional alternatives as an ordinary premise of purchasing. A local choice initiates a submission rather than completing the decision. The qualification is substantive: districts can propose alternatives with rationale and evidence, so authority is centralized without making the existing list the only conceivable source of acceptable materials.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the allocation of decision authority; the passage does not establish that local professional judgment is eliminated.

There is no express universal-learnability finding, claim of conclusive research consensus, or Mississippi reference. Repeated alignment language alone is insufficient to establish PRE-1, PRE-4, or PRE-6.

## FOS — Foregrounding and silencing

### FOS observation 1: Assessment organizes action

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(k)(2)(A)(ii) and (k)(2)(B)(iii), p. 12.
- **Passage:** “Measured by a high-quality literacy screener or the state annual accountability assessment.” Also: “How the student's progress will be monitored and evaluated;”.
- **Code:** FOS-3.
- **Reading:** Under the lead-in requiring a plan for each K-3 student below the board's standard, assessment determines entry into individualized planning; monitoring then becomes mandatory plan content. The student is made visible through a recurring sequence of measurement, intervention, and evaluation. This foregrounds assessment as an organizing mechanism without demonstrating that the section itself supplies every detail of a universal screening mandate.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about assessment's central procedural role.

### FOS observation 2: Dyslexia receives a named place in materials policy

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(f)(1)(A)-(B), p. 4.
- **Passage:** “Dyslexia programs that are evidence-based and:” followed by “Aligned to structured literacy; or” and “Grounded in the Orton-Gillingham methodology;”.
- **Code:** FOS-2 (partial).
- **Reading:** Dyslexia is the first expressly named program category on the approved-materials list and receives two specified routes of alignment. This gives it greater definitional specificity than the general intervention and reading-program categories that follow. The list is nonexhaustive, however, and later student-support provisions use reading standards and risk more broadly; this is not evidence that all struggling readers are classified through dyslexia.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the local prominence; uncertain about stronger claims that dyslexia organizes the entire section.

### FOS observation 3: Comprehension is present; knowledge building is unnamed

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(k)(2)(B)(i)(d)-(e) and (v), p. 12.
- **Passage:** “Vocabulary-building strategies; and” followed by “Self-regulated use of reading comprehension strategies, as identified by high-quality literacy screener data;”. Subdivision (v) also requires the program “to address the areas of phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension;”.
- **Code:** FOS-4 (counter-example) for comprehension; FOS-4 (partial) for background knowledge.
- **Reading:** Comprehension is an explicit diagnostic and instructional area, so a claim that it is silenced would misread this list. Its most detailed formulation emphasizes the learner's self-regulated strategies and screener data. Background knowledge and a knowledge-building curriculum are not named in this specification of needs and intervention content; vocabulary is the nearest named area, but it is not explicitly equated with knowledge building.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about comprehension's presence and the lack of an explicit knowledge-building item. Uncertain about exclusion: the needs list says including without limitation, allowing additional content. The passage also gives no clear basis for coding phonics as primary under FOS-1 merely because it appears earlier.

### FOS observation 4: Oral language and writing fall outside the named intervention areas

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(k)(2)(B)(v), p. 12.
- **Passage:** “The intensive, evidence-based literacy intervention program aligned to the science of reading the student's teacher will use to address the areas of phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension;”
- **Code:** FOS-7 (qualified).
- **Reading:** This enumeration specifies what the literacy intervention will address, making it an appropriate site for examining absence. It names five reading components without naming oral language development or writing instruction as intervention areas. Their omission narrows what the statute explicitly makes visible, even though vocabulary and phonemic awareness may overlap with aspects of language work.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the unnamed areas; uncertain about practical exclusion. The wider plan allows additional needs and services, and the provision concerns reading intervention rather than the whole literacy curriculum.

### FOS observation 5: A general skill framework leaves multilingual instruction unspecified

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(k)(2)(B)(i), (i)(a)-(e), p. 12.
- **Passage:** “The student's specific, diagnosed reading skill needs, including without limitation:” The named needs are “Phonemic awareness;”, “Phonics decoding;”, “Text reading fluency;”, “Vocabulary-building strategies; and”, and “Self-regulated use of reading comprehension strategies, as identified by high-quality literacy screener data;”.
- **Code:** FOS-5 (qualified).
- **Reading:** The mandatory plan framework for K-3 students below the standard specifies skills and data without naming language proficiency, multilingual resources, translanguaging, or EL-specific instructional adaptations. It addresses diagnosed reading skills instead. This is a bounded omission within the instructional specification, not evidence that English learners are absent from the document or that individualized adaptations are forbidden.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about what the list names; uncertain about the extent of silencing because its nonexhaustive wording can accommodate language-related needs.

### FOS observation 6: English learners are visible in promotion exceptions

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(l)(2)(B)(i), p. 13; (k)(1)(B), p. 11.
- **Passage:** “Limited English Proficiency students who have had less than three (3) years of instruction in an English language learner program;”. The notification provision states: “The written notification in a parent-friendly manner required under subdivision (k)(1)(A) of this section may be in the language of communication preferred by a parent, legal guardian, or person standing in loco parentis to a student;”.
- **Code:** FOS-5 (counter-example, qualified).
- **Reading:** The exemption list recognizes time in an English language learner program as relevant to promotion, and the notice rule recognizes families' language preferences. These provisions counter a document-wide absence claim. Their scope differs from instructional adaptation: one identifies an exemption category, while the other permits, rather than requires, preferred-language notification.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about this bounded recognition. The notice provision concerns family communication and does not itself establish multilingual instruction.

FOS-6 is not assigned: the section specifies instructional remedies without offering a developed causal account of reading outcomes. Funding is present as appropriation, grants, compensation, and sanction; that does not amount to naming inadequate funding as a cause, but it makes an unqualified claim that resources are wholly silenced unsustainable.

## SUB — Subject positioning

### SUB observation 1: Teachers as credentialed and evaluated subjects

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(d)(1)(A)(ii), p. 3.
- **Passage:** “A teacher described in subdivision (d)(1)(A)(i) of this section who has not demonstrated proficiency by the 2023-2024 school year may be afforded an opportunity to demonstrate proficiency by being placed in an intensive support status under § 6-17-2807 for a period of time specified by the teacher's evaluator in the professional growth plan for the teacher; and”.
- **Code:** SUB-1 (qualified).
- **Reading:** The teacher whose proficiency is undemonstrated enters a status supervised by an evaluator, who specifies its duration. Opportunity and support language soften a relationship still structured by required demonstration and external judgment. The modal may makes the opportunity permissive, while failure to demonstrate proficiency remains distinct from proof of actual ignorance.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about regulation; uncertain about construing the teacher as inherently deficient rather than insufficiently credentialed.

### SUB observation 2: Professional partnership within evaluation

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(j)(1)(D)(vi), p. 7; (j)(1)(D)(ix), p. 8; (k)(2)(B)(vii), p. 12.
- **Passage:** “Partner with teachers to integrate professional learning into classroom practice;”. Also: “Provide feedback on teachers’ evidence-based instruction and practices that may be used for teacher evaluations;” and “Any additional services the student's teacher determines are available and appropriate to accelerate the student's reading skill development;”.
- **Code:** SUB-2 (qualified); SUB-1 for the evaluation connection.
- **Reading:** Teachers are partners in implementation and judges of appropriate additional services, not merely recipients of orders. Yet coaching feedback can enter evaluation, connecting professional support to oversight. These coexisting positions prevent either a wholly autonomous or wholly passive account of the teacher subject.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the stated partnership, judgment, and possible evaluative use; the text does not establish how teachers experience that combination.

### SUB observation 3: District discretion subject to approval and sanction

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(g)(2)(A)-(B), p. 4; (i)(3)(B)(ii)(a), p. 6.
- **Passage:** “A rationale for choosing the alternative curriculum program; and” followed by “Evidence-based research regarding the alternative curriculum program.” The sanction includes the instruction “to withhold a maximum of ten percent (10%) of the monthly distribution of state foundation funding aid”.
- **Code:** SUB-5 (qualified).
- **Reading:** Districts may initiate an alternative curriculum choice, but must make that choice defensible to the division. Separately, failure to remedy a prohibited-method violation within sixty days of notice activates a funding sanction. Local initiative and institutional vulnerability coexist; the sanction is specific to the named violation sequence, and ten percent is a ceiling rather than a fixed deduction.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about conditional discretion and the specified enforcement relationship.

### SUB observation 4: Curricula await authorization

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(g)(1), p. 4.
- **Passage:** “A public school district and open-enrollment public charter school that purchases a curriculum program shall choose a curriculum program from the division's approved list of curriculum programs created under subsection (f) of this section.”
- **Code:** SUB-6 (partial); SUB-7.
- **Reading:** A curriculum becomes presumptively usable through state approval, making administrative authorization part of its legitimacy. The text does not directly accuse publishers of causing failure, so SUB-6 applies to the governed status of curricula rather than to an explicit construction of publishers as culpable. The alternative-approval route in (g)(2) limits the list's exclusivity.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the state's arbiter position; uncertain about suspect as a characterization, since approval could also be read as routine quality assurance.

### SUB observation 5: Students as beneficiaries and objects of progression rules

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(l)(1), (l)(2)(A), p. 13; (l)(3)(A), p. 14.
- **Passage:** “By the beginning of the 2025-2026 school year, if a public school student has not met the third-grade reading standard, as defined by the state board, or the student does not have a good-cause exemption, as provided under this subsection, the student shall not be promoted to grade four (4).” Also: “A student in grade three (3) who does not meet the reading standard for promotion to grade four (4) may be promoted by the school district for good cause.” Required support includes: “Provide at least ninety (90) minutes of evidence-based literacy instruction aligned to the science of reading during each school day;”.
- **Code:** SUB-3 (qualified).
- **Reading:** Students below the standard are governed through promotion restrictions and guaranteed instructional duties placed on schools. Under (l)(3), those promoted with good-cause exemptions remain within the support group during the subsequent summer and school year. The text thus combines accountability and benefit without expressly identifying students as victims of prior instruction. The printed or in (l)(1) sits uneasily with the good-cause permission in (l)(2)(A); it is retained here rather than silently repaired.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the dual positioning; uncertain about precisely how the conflicting promotion wording is to operate.

### SUB observation 6: Families as informed participants and home supporters

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(k)(1)(A), p. 11; (k)(2)(B)(vi), p. 12.
- **Passage:** “Notify all parents, legal guardians, or persons standing in loco parentis to a student, in writing, in a parent-friendly manner, of their student’s reading progress each time the student is assessed throughout the year using high-quality literacy screener results, as required by § 6-15-2907(a)(2) and § 6-15-2006(a)(1)(C).” Also: “The strategies the student's parents, legal guardians, or persons standing in loco parentis to the student are encouraged to use in assisting the student to achieve the student's reading goal; and”.
- **Code:** SUB-4; no code fits the additional positioning as encouraged home implementers.
- **Reading:** Repeated, accessible notice constructs families as entitled to information rather than dependent on discretionary disclosure. The plan also recruits them into helping achieve a school-defined reading goal. Encouraged participation does not impose a parental compliance duty or give parents a curriculum veto.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about information rights and encouragement; the passage does not show that families co-determine the reading standard or intervention.

## INT — Intertextuality

### INT observation 1: A named instructional lineage without organizational attribution

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(f)(1)(B), p. 4.
- **Passage:** “Grounded in the Orton-Gillingham methodology;”.
- **Code:** No code fits.
- **Reading:** Naming a methodology gives one branch of the dyslexia-program criterion an identifiable instructional reference rather than a generic evidence label. The or linking it to structured literacy makes it an alternative route within that category. The text names neither an organization whose definition is adopted nor a particular study, so INT-2 and INT-4 would overstate the attribution.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the named methodological reference and its alternative position; its detailed content is not supplied here.

### INT observation 2: Disability law as a qualification benchmark

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(j)(1)(C)(iv), p. 7.
- **Passage:** “An understanding of the child find mandate of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, 20 U. S. C. § 1400 et seq., as it existed on January 1, 2023.”
- **Code:** No code fits.
- **Reading:** The coach's required knowledge includes a named federal legal mandate, joining reading expertise to disability-law responsibilities. The date fixes the version invoked by this document. This is a legal reference, not a federal research report under INT-1 or an invocation of historical reading reform under INT-6.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the explicit legal intertext; the passage does not itself explain the mandate's requirements.

### INT observation 3: Existing state evaluation machinery supplies authority

- **Where:** § 6-17-429(l)(3)(B)(i)-(ii), p. 15, under the conditional staffing alternative in (l)(3)(B), p. 14.
- **Passage:** “With a highly-effective rating according to the Teacher Excellence and Support System, § 6-17-2801 et seq., where possible; or” followed by “Deemed to be a high-performing teacher as defined by a Master Professional Educator designation;”.
- **Code:** No code fits.
- **Reading:** Existing ratings and a professional designation make teacher quality legible for assigning students who require additional support. The statute borrows administrative categories as authority rather than offering a research citation to establish instructional effectiveness. These alternatives apply when a teacher meeting the preceding top-quartile value-added criterion cannot be identified; they are not unconditional or interchangeable with the primary criterion.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the administrative intertexts and their conditional use.

The document names no National Reading Panel report, International Dyslexia Association definition, specific research study, media report, or other state's policy as warrant. Its five-component list is therefore not INT-1, and evidence-based wording is not INT-4. The three-cueing/MSV prohibition supplies LEX-5 evidence, but names no opposing text or organization sufficient to require INT-7. These limits concern only this supplied codified document, not an unexamined legislative record.
