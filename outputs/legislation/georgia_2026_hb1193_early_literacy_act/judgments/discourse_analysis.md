## B5. Coverage

There are **4 LEX, 3 PRE, 5 FOS, 6 SUB, and 2 INT observations**. All five dimensions receive substantive treatment. INT is the thinnest numerically, with an appropriate reason: the supplied bill offers little direct evidence for named research studies, federal reports, media, or interstate models. The analysis does not fabricate those links from generic references to research or from the codebook's examples. PRE is selective but addresses support, authority, and the shared scientific standard without manufacturing findings the bill does not state.

The following are additional possibilities in my own reading, not failures to inventory every provision:

- **LEX/FOS:** The definition of a unified literacy plan in §20-2-153.1(b)(16), p. 10, extends literacy from birth through career readiness and emphasizes coherence, alignment, and continuity. The analysis quotes its component requirements but could develop this broader life-course framing.
- **FOS/PRE:** The funding contingencies for coaches (§20-2-186.1(b), p. 3), the written funding-insufficiency notification and state assistance (§20-2-153.1(d)(2)(B), p. 15), and annual materials-funding recommendations (§20-2-1015.1(c), p. 21) complicate a purely instructional account of implementation. This is a reason to examine resources carefully before adding a structural-silencing claim; the present analysis makes none.
- **SUB:** Parents are also encouraged participants in literacy (§20-2-153.1(a.1)(3), p. 9) and have enrollment choices (§20-2-151(b)(1)(B)(viii), pp. 30–31). Teachers contribute to first-grade readiness decisions (§20-2-151(b)(2)(B)(i), p. 31), and IEP committees replace the standard placement committee in specified cases (pp. 33 and 40). These would broaden the account beyond retention appeal and ordinary committee procedures.
- **INT:** The handbook's IDA and other recognized accreditation references (§20-2-153.1(c)(5)(C), p. 13) offer another external-authority observation. Internal legal references, including the task force's exemption from Chapter 14 of Title 50 (§20-14-202(c)(5)(C), p. 26), could further develop the reported codebook gap for intra-state legal intertextuality.

The analysis is faithful to the supplied document and carefully limits its inferences. Its main strengths are exact quotations after amendment, attention to open categories and procedural conditions, and counter-examples that prevent broad silencing claims. Its main limitation is selective coverage of resource conditions, broader family/teacher agency, and internal legal intertextuality; the knowledge-gap and consensus observations remain interpretive possibilities rather than explicit legislative diagnoses. These limits warrant researcher attention but do not produce grounding failures or factual errors. Acceptance remains the researcher's decision.
