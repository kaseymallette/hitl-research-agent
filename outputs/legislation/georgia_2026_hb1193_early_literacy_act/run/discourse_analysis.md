# Discourse analysis: Georgia House Bill 1193

The document is House Bill 1193, as passed House and Senate, titled the Georgia Early Literacy Act of 2026. The observations concern the supplied bill, with amendment markings resolved to the resulting text. Scientific alignment, professional support, and administrative accountability work together in its discourse: literacy is an instructional practice that must be supported, measured, and made institutionally coherent. These are readings of the bill's language, not findings about implementation or effectiveness.

## LEX — Lexical choices and definitional framing

### LEX observation 1: Structured literacy gives scientific alignment a pedagogical form

- **Where:** Section 1-4; new Code Section 20-2-208.2(a), p. 8.
- **Passage:** "an evidence based approach to teaching oral and written language aligned to the science of reading founded on the science of how children learn to read and characterized by explicit, systematic, cumulative, and diagnostic instruction in phonology, sound-symbol association, syllable instruction, morphology, syntax, and semantics."
- **Code:** LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition.
- **Reading:** The endorsement provision turns scientific alignment into a recognizable account of how instruction proceeds and which language structures it addresses. The movement from phonology to semantics makes this more than a phonics-only description. The code fits the pedagogical descriptors; this passage does not itself attribute them to the International Dyslexia Association or supply a general definition of the science of reading.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the descriptors and language scope; the passage defines the approach required for an endorsement, so its scope should not be enlarged into a complete definition governing every use of the term.

### LEX observation 2: The five components are an instructional foundation

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(d.1)(1)(B)(ii), p. 16.
- **Passage:** "Prepare teachers to provide explicit, systematic, sequential, diagnostic, and cumulative approaches to teaching phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, and text comprehension; and"
- **Code:** LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor; LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition.
- **Reading:** The five components specify the required content of preparation, while the adjectives prescribe a common instructional organization across them. Text comprehension belongs inside this foundation rather than appearing only as an eventual outcome of decoding. The National Reading Panel is not named here; the component list supports LEX-1, not a federal-report citation code.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain. This is a preparation requirement, not a standalone definition of reading.

### LEX observation 3: Alignment is established through legal reference and approval

- **Where:** Section 2-2; Code Section 20-2-1010(c)(1), p. 20.
- **Passage:** "Only high-quality instructional materials and content that are aligned to the science of reading, as such terms are defined in Code Section 20-2-153.1, shall be used for literacy and reading education in kindergarten through grade three; and"
- **Code:** No code fits.
- **Reading:** "Only" makes alignment a boundary of legitimate classroom materials, while the cross-reference treats the meaning of the key terms as already legally available. The supplied amendment does not reproduce all the definitions to which it refers. This creates a documentary limit for this analysis: the reference establishes legal authority but does not let the reader reconstruct those unprinted definitions. LEX-4 would be too strong here because the bill expressly supplies a legal source for the terms; LEX-5 also does not fit because this passage names no prohibited instructional method or program.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the exclusive boundary and cross-reference. The full substantive boundary cannot be established from the printed amendment alone.

### LEX observation 4: Intent uses constructive rather than crisis vocabulary

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(a.1)(1)(A)-(D), p. 9.
- **Passage:** "Early literacy instruction in this state shall be:"; "Comprehensive;"; "Focused on structured literacy and foundational literacy skills;"; "Aligned to the science of reading; and"; "Anchored by high-quality instructional materials that are aligned to the science of reading;"
- **Code:** LEX-6: Crisis / Urgency Framing (absent — notable).
- **Reading:** At the point where the legislature states its instructional intent, it presents desirable qualities rather than a narrative of emergency or failure. "Focused," "aligned," and "anchored" frame reform as the organization of instruction around a common center. The observation is limited to this intent passage; it does not imply that the bill lacks deadlines or consequential interventions.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the vocabulary in the cited intent list. No comparison with other jurisdictions is needed to establish that local absence.

## PRE — Presuppositions

### PRE observation 1: Educator learning is framed as access and application

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(a.1)(2), p. 9.
- **Passage:** "Educators shall have access to and be provided with research on the science of reading and receive comprehensive curriculum based professional learning to support them in applying such research in instructional practices; and"
- **Code:** PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap (reframed).
- **Reading:** The provision presupposes that making research accessible and supporting its application are necessary parts of the reform. It locates the remedy partly in the system's provision of resources, rather than explicitly accusing teachers of ignorance. The important gap may be between research and classroom application, as well as between existing and desired knowledge.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain. Professional learning can support already knowledgeable teachers; the language does not establish an existing knowledge deficit. The reframed code marks an interpretive possibility, not a stated diagnosis.

### PRE observation 2: State authority to adjudicate instructional quality is taken as workable

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(c)(2)(A), p. 10; Section 2-4; new Code Section 20-14-203(a), p. 27.
- **Passage:** "Upon the recommendation of the Office of Student Achievement, approve high-quality instructional materials aligned to the science of reading to be used for teaching students in kindergarten through third grade to read; and"; "Within 60 days of receiving any recommendation from the Georgia Literacy Task Force, the State Board of Education shall vote to adopt or reject such recommendation."
- **Code:** PRE-5: Legislative Competence.
- **Reading:** The bill proceeds as though instructional quality and research alignment can appropriately be adjudicated through state recommendation and approval. It specifies who decides and when without arguing for that allocation of authority. Expertise enters through administrative recommendations, but a board vote gives those recommendations their policy status; approval is not automatic.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the institutional presupposition. The passage does not establish the accuracy of any future judgment.

### PRE observation 3: Exclusivity suggests a common scientific boundary, without asserting unanimity

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(d.1)(1)(B)(i), p. 16.
- **Passage:** "Are exclusively aligned to the science of reading;"
- **Code:** PRE-4: Research Consensus (partial).
- **Reading:** The definite singular formulation and "exclusively" make the science of reading function as a common standard against which preparation can be judged. The provision needs that boundary to be administratively identifiable, but it supplies no discussion of scientific disagreement or limits. Its strongest claim is curricular exclusivity, not an express declaration that all research is settled.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain. A shared research orientation can contain disagreement and revision. The passage may presuppose an identifiable field rather than unanimous findings, so full PRE-4 would overstate the evidence.

## FOS — Foregrounding and silencing

### FOS observation 1: Measurement organizes even the comprehensive literacy plan

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(b)(16)(A), p. 10, and (c)(3)(A), p. 11.
- **Passage:** "Establish measurable literacy goals for all tested grade levels, using state-wide assessment results and approved universal reading screener data to monitor student progress, guide instructional decision making, and evaluate program effectiveness;"; "Provide screening and diagnostic capabilities for monitoring student progress;"
- **Code:** FOS-3: Assessment / Screening Foregrounded.
- **Reading:** The first required component of the unified plan makes assessment data serve three purposes: monitoring learners, directing teaching, and evaluating programs. Screener requirements reinforce that connection between individual diagnosis and system evaluation. Measurement is thus an organizing mechanism of literacy governance, not merely a preliminary test before teaching begins.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the specified central role. This does not mean assessment exhausts the plan: its next components concern early-learning collaboration and transitions.

### FOS observation 2: Dyslexia is a specifically named need within a broader risk category

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(c)(3)(C), p. 12, and (c)(5)(B), p. 13.
- **Passage:** "Identify students who are significantly at risk of not attaining grade level reading proficiency, including, but not limited to, identifying students with characteristics of dyslexia; and"; "Guidance on developing support plans for students with characteristics of dyslexia;"
- **Code:** FOS-2: Dyslexia Foregrounded (qualified).
- **Reading:** Dyslexia receives explicit recognition both in general screening and in dedicated handbook guidance. The repeated formulation "characteristics of dyslexia" makes support-relevant identification visible without equating a screener result with a formal diagnosis. "Including, but not limited to" also prevents dyslexia from exhausting the bill's category of reading risk; the framework addresses other struggling readers.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about special visibility and the broader category. A claim that all struggling readers are organized exclusively around dyslexia would exceed this evidence.

### FOS observation 3: Comprehension is present; background knowledge is less explicit

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(d.1)(1)(B)(ii) and (C), p. 16.
- **Passage:** "Prepare teachers to provide explicit, systematic, sequential, diagnostic, and cumulative approaches to teaching phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, and text comprehension; and"; "Content based literacy and mathematics practices;"
- **Code:** FOS-4: Background Knowledge / Comprehension Silenced (counter-example for comprehension; partial for background knowledge).
- **Reading:** The preparation list expressly includes teaching text comprehension, so comprehension cannot be treated as absent. It also names content based literacy. It does not explicitly require background-knowledge development or a knowledge-building curriculum in this list; its specificity instead lies in instructional approaches and reading components. This is a bounded difference in explicitness, not evidence that knowledge is excluded from instruction.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about the background-knowledge component: content based literacy may encompass knowledge building, and the parent list is expressly nonexhaustive. Certain that the passage counters comprehension silencing.

### FOS observation 4: Oral and written language are explicit parts of literacy preparation

- **Where:** Section 1-4; Code Section 20-2-208.2(a), p. 8; Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(b)(16)(B), p. 10.
- **Passage:** "teaching oral and written language"; "Include explicit strategies for collaboration with early learning providers for learners from birth to age five to support school readiness and early language development; and"
- **Code:** FOS-7: Oral Language / Writing Silenced (counter-example).
- **Reading:** The endorsement's teaching remit expressly includes both oral and written language. The plan requirements also make early language development a purpose of collaboration before age five. These inclusions prevent a general finding that this bill reduces literacy to reading print or silences language development and writing. They do not specify a full writing curriculum, and the endorsement provision has a particular professional scope.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about explicit inclusion; uncertain about how extensively written-language instruction would be developed in implementation, which the bill does not show.

### FOS observation 5: English learners are named, but the screener list remains general

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(d.1)(1)(D), p. 16; Section 3-3; Code Section 20-2-151(b)(2)(F), p. 34; Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(c)(3)(B), pp. 11-12.
- **Passage:** "Strategies appropriate for the instruction of English language learners;"; "The guidelines shall include information pertinent to consideration of the placement of students who have been identified as being disabled or limited-English-proficient."; "Measure, at a minimum, a student's foundational reading skills, including, but not limited to, a student's phonological awareness, oral language, the alphabetic principle, including letter naming, letter sound, and sound letter correspondences, decoding, encoding, accuracy, vocabulary, and comprehension;"
- **Code:** FOS-5: English Learners Silenced (counter-example in preparation and placement; partial in screener measurement requirements).
- **Reading:** Preparation and placement guidance explicitly recognize English learners, defeating a document-wide absence claim. The screener measurement list, however, specifies reading and language skills without naming multilingual assessment, languages of administration, or how learning English is distinguished from reading difficulty. It addresses a general skill profile instead. This is an omission at that particular specification site, not a claim that approved tools cannot address multilingual learners.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the named provisions and the local omission. Uncertain about its practical significance: the list sets a minimum, and the placement guidance may provide relevant differentiation.

## SUB — Subject positioning

### SUB observation 1: Teachers are recipients of protected professional support

- **Where:** Section 1-2; Code Section 20-2-186.1(c)(3)(A), p. 3, and (c)(4)(A)-(B), p. 4.
- **Passage:** "Coaching educators using nonevaluative, evidence based practices, including modeling instruction, analyzing instructional practice and data, providing actionable feedback, and delivering side-by-side classroom support;"; "A school based literacy coach shall not be:"; "Authorized or required to perform administrative functions or duties, including, but not limited to, performing any rotating duty assignments or serving as an evaluator, substitute teacher, assessment coordinator, or school administrator;"; "Invited, required, or permitted to attend placement committee meetings; or"
- **Code:** SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional.
- **Reading:** "Nonevaluative" and "side-by-side" position teachers as practitioners supported through shared classroom work. The role prohibitions reinforce that position by separating coaching from evaluation and placement decisions. These are substantive limits on the support role, not merely a friendly vocabulary for supervision.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the protected role. This does not make teachers' curricular choices unrestricted.

### SUB observation 2: Professional expertise is also regulated through fidelity and verification

- **Where:** Section 1-2; Code Section 20-2-186.1(c)(3)(G)-(H), p. 4.
- **Passage:** "Supporting the implementation and monitoring of evidence based literacy curricula and instructional materials with fidelity, ensuring alignment to the science of reading; and"; "Demonstrating competency through approved, periodic, practice based assessment that verifies his or her ability to apply the science of reading, learning science, and instructional science to deliver explicit, systematic, evidence based literacy instruction directly with students."
- **Code:** SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated (qualified: regulation of literacy coaches).
- **Reading:** Coaches are expert helpers whose own competence must be periodically demonstrated. "Fidelity" makes successful professional action partly a matter of implementing an approved instructional orientation faithfully. This coexists with nonevaluative support for teachers: the bill differentiates the classroom coaching relationship from accountability for the coach's practice.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about regulation; uncertain about deficiency. Verification need not imply incompetence, especially where coach qualifications also require successful prior experience. The subject here is specifically the coach, not every classroom teacher.

### SUB observation 3: District flexibility becomes conditional on literacy planning

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(f)(2)(A), p. 19, effective within the provision beginning January 1, 2027.
- **Passage:** "No local school system shall be eligible for any waiver or variance granted under Code Section 20-2-244 or Article 4 of this chapter or to enter into or renew a charter system contract or strategic waivers school system contract with the State Board of Education unless such local school system has adopted a unified literacy plan;"
- **Code:** SUB-5: District as Compliance Target.
- **Reading:** The negative conditional construction makes a literacy plan an entrance requirement for specified forms of institutional flexibility. Local systems retain responsibility for adopting their plans, but the state can withhold these permissions when the condition is unmet. Literacy planning thus becomes a condition of institutional standing as well as an instructional task.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain. The stated condition concerns plan adoption; this sentence alone does not require proof that the plan has improved outcomes.

### SUB observation 4: Students are both supported learners and objects of placement decisions

- **Where:** Section 3-4; Code Section 20-2-283(b)(4)(A)(iii), (B)(i), (B)(iv)(II), and (B)(vii), pp. 39-41.
- **Passage:** "The student shall receive intensive reading intervention in accordance with Code Section 20-2-153.1."; "The school principal or the principal's designee shall retain the student for the next school year except as otherwise provided in this paragraph;"; "whether the student is retained or promoted"; "No student shall remain in the first grade program for more than two years."
- **Code:** SUB-3: Student as Victim / Beneficiary (qualified).
- **Reading:** The student is a beneficiary of mandatory intervention and a subject whose grade placement officials determine. Retention follows the paragraph's continued-risk condition after intervention; it is not simply an immediate consequence of one screening result. The committee must prescribe further instruction whether it retains or promotes, and the two-year ceiling limits first-grade retention. The language establishes support and control without explicitly constructing students as victims of prior instruction or naming racial or income groups here.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the combined positions and qualifications. The passage supplies no evidence of retention's effects.

### SUB observation 5: Parents have procedural authority within a constrained appeal

- **Where:** Section 3-4; Code Section 20-2-283(b)(4)(B)(iii)-(iv)(I), p. 40, and (d), p. 41.
- **Passage:** "If the parent or guardian appeals the decision to retain the student, then the school principal or the principal's designee shall establish a placement committee composed of the school principal or the principal's designee, the student's parent or guardian, and the student's teacher"; "A decision to promote must be a unanimous decision"; "This Code section does not create a property interest in promotion."
- **Code:** SUB-4: Parent as Rights-Holder (qualified).
- **Reading:** Parents can initiate the first-grade retention appeal and participate in the decision-making committee, making them more than recipients of school information. Their authority is shared: promotion requires unanimity and a further finding about likely grade-level performance with intervention. The express denial of a property interest in promotion bounds the entitlement discourse; participation does not become a guaranteed promotion outcome.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the procedure and printed limitation. No broader legal consequences beyond the bill's words are inferred.

### SUB observation 6: The state arbitrates a regulated materials market

- **Where:** Section 2-2; Code Section 20-2-1010(c)(2), p. 20; Section 2-4; Code Section 20-14-202(d), p. 27.
- **Passage:** "Beginning with the 2029-2030 school year and continuing thereafter, no public school that receives funds under Article 6 of this chapter shall use any portion of such funds for the purchase or use of instructional materials and content for literacy and reading education in kindergarten through grade three that are not approved for such purpose by the State Board of Education upon the recommendation of the Georgia Literacy Task Force."; "The task force shall endeavor to include multiple vendors"; "The task force shall make public all rubrics and measurements used to determine eligibility and compliance for all universal reading screeners and high-quality instructional materials accepted for consideration."
- **Code:** SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter (qualified: arbiter); SUB-6: Publisher / Curriculum as Suspect (qualified).
- **Reading:** State approval determines which materials can be purchased or used with the specified funds. Materials must pass an institutional test rather than receive presumptive legitimacy from local selection. The public-criteria requirement and encouragement of multiple vendors also position suppliers as eligible participants in a regulated market, not named culprits for reading failure. The bill establishes an arbiter role without this passage narrating the state's rescue of failed schools.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about approval and market conditions; uncertain about "suspect" as a characterization of publishers. Quality assurance is another reasonable reading, and no publisher is accused here.

## INT — Intertextuality

### INT observation 1: An external organization supplies an updateable definition

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(b)(6), p. 10.
- **Passage:** "'Dyslexia' shall have the same meaning as the definition adopted and amended from time to time by the International Dyslexia Association and approved by the board and posted on the websites of the department and the Office of Student Achievement."
- **Code:** INT-2: IDA / Professional Organisation Citation.
- **Reading:** The bill incorporates the association's definition instead of printing a fixed description. "Amended from time to time" accommodates an evolving external text, while board approval and public posting mediate its statutory use. This is a specific delegation of definitional authority over dyslexia, not evidence that the association defines every aspect of the bill's reading science.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about incorporation and the state approval condition. The contents of the incorporated definition are not reproduced here and are not supplied from outside the document.

### INT observation 2: Existing Georgia policy supplies continuity and legitimacy

- **Where:** Section 2-1; Code Section 20-2-153.1(e)(1)(A), p. 17; Section 3-5; Code Section 20-2-284(a), p. 41.
- **Passage:** "Maintain and amend as needed the Georgia Literacy Plan originally created by the Georgia Council on Literacy;"; "consistent with the Georgia Academic Placement and Promotion Policy."
- **Code:** No code fits.
- **Reading:** These references present the new governance and placement requirements as continuations and revisions of existing Georgia policy. Maintaining an inherited plan gives the new committee an established textual object to administer; consistency with the named placement policy situates local decisions within another authoritative framework. Neither reference is an interstate model, a research citation, or an explicit account of a historical reading policy's success or failure, so INT-3, INT-4, and INT-6 would overstate what is printed.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the continuity work. The bill does not reproduce those policy texts, so their substantive contents cannot be inferred from their titles.

The supplied bill offers little direct evidence for research-study, federal-report, media, or interstate citation codes. Its repeated references to research should not be converted into named empirical warrants, and the codebook's examples from other documents supply none for this bill.
