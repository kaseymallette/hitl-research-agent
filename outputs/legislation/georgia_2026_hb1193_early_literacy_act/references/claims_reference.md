## Caption, purpose recital, and short title

- **R001 [Purpose; named sources] — Caption and recital, pp. 1–2, lines 1–30.** The bill is an Act to amend Title 20 of the Official Code of Georgia Annotated to promote and advance statewide literacy and reading education. The recital enumerates: funding school based literacy coaches in all public schools serving any K–3 grade; RESA-employed regional coaches; literacy coaching endorsements; substantial revision of the Georgia Early Literacy and Dyslexia Act; legislative intent and a definition; revised universal-screener selection and requirements; professional learning; unified literacy plans; educator preparation and PSC evaluation; certification and funding of high-quality instructional materials; an OSA Georgia Literacy Coordinating Committee; a State Literacy Director and a “Georgia Coordinator of Literacy Coaches” (the recital's wording); coach position descriptions, minimum qualifications, and duties; a Georgia Literacy Task Force, its appointments, qualifications, duties, compensation, recommendations, and SBOE consideration; a statewide literacy-awareness campaign; termination of the Georgia Council on Literacy; encouragement of attendance or home study from age five; placement of new enrollees; parental elections of kindergarten for first-grade-eligible initial enrollees and Pre-K for four- or five-year-olds; first-grade readiness assessments and annual reports; revised placement, promotion, and retention; a short title and effective date; related matters, repeal of conflicting laws, and other purposes. This is a purpose list, not a substitute for the operative conditions below.
- **R002 [Requirement; permission; title] — §1-1, p. 2.** The Act shall be known and may be cited as the “Georgia Early Literacy Act of 2026.”

## Part I — Coaches and endorsement

### §1-2: new §20-2-186.1 (pp. 2–5)

- **R003 [Definition] — (a), p. 3.** “Eligible school” means a school with any of grades kindergarten through three. The printed scope phrase is “As used in this paragraph.”
- **R004 [Requirement; authority; funding; exception] — (b), p. 3.** Subject to General Assembly appropriations, SBOE shall provide grants to local units of administration with eligible schools to reimburse expenditures sufficient for the beginning salary and benefits of one school based literacy coach per eligible school. A school with **200 or fewer full-time equivalent students** shall earn **50 percent** of that amount. This is conditional grant funding, not a printed fixed-dollar appropriation.
- **R005 [Requirement; authority] — (c)(1), p. 3.** SBOE, collaborating with OSA, shall establish school based literacy coaches' minimum qualifications and duties.
- **R006 [Requirements; criteria, open minimum list] — (c)(2)(A)–(C), p. 3.** Minimum qualifications shall include, but not be limited to: (A) PSC certification; (B) at least five years of documented successful classroom teaching or literacy-intervention experience at assigned grade levels; (C) knowledge of scientifically based reading research, special expertise in quality reading instruction and intervention, dyslexia specific interventions, and data analysis.
- **R007 [Requirements; duties; criteria, open minimum list] — (c)(3)(A)–(H), pp. 3–4.** Minimum duties shall include, but not be limited to: (A) nonevaluative, evidence based educator coaching, including modeling instruction, analyzing instructional practice and data, actionable feedback, and side-by-side classroom support; (B) collaborating with the principal on a strategic literacy-coaching plan; (C) spending **no less than 70 percent of the school day** in classrooms working directly with students and teachers; (D) prioritizing teachers, activities, and roles with greatest impact on reading achievement, such as classroom coaching and mentoring; (E) analyzing student data, including screening, diagnostic, and progress-monitoring data, to inform instruction, grouping, and intervention within a multi-tiered system of supports; (F) synthesizing student-performance and educator-practice data for coaching goals and targeted, needs based educator support; (G) supporting faithful implementation and monitoring of evidence based literacy curricula and instructional materials aligned to the science of reading; (H) demonstrating competency through approved, periodic, practice based assessment verifying the ability to apply the science of reading, learning science, and instructional science to deliver explicit, systematic, evidence based literacy instruction directly to students.
- **R008 [Prohibitions; criteria] — (c)(4)(A)–(C), p. 4.** A school based coach shall not be (A) authorized or required to perform administrative functions or duties, including but not limited to rotating duties or service as evaluator, substitute teacher, assessment coordinator, or administrator; (B) invited, required, or permitted to attend placement committee meetings; or (C) designated teacher of record. The administrative examples are open; the three prohibitions are expressly enumerated.
- **R009 [Requirement; authority; deadline] — (d), p. 4.** By **August 1, 2026**, SBOE shall establish necessary implementing policies, rules, and regulations, including but not limited to the process by which local units shall request and receive grants.
- **R010 [Permission; exception; funding] — (e), p. 5.** Nothing in the section shall be construed to prohibit local units from hiring or contracting with school based coaches using other funds available for that purpose.

### §1-3: new §20-2-270.3 (pp. 5–8)

- **R011 [Requirement; authority] — (a), p. 5.** Each RESA board of control shall authorize regional literacy coaches and regional leadership literacy coaches to provide coaching, support, and related services to local systems and other public schools in its service area.
- **R012 [Requirements; authority; minimum staffing] — (b), p. 5.** In collaboration with the State Literacy Director and Georgia Literacy Coach Coordinator, each RESA board shall assign regional literacy coaches to systems and public schools in its service area; **at least one regional leadership literacy coach** shall be assigned as primary contact with system and school leaders.
- **R013 [Requirements; accountability] — (c), p. 5.** Each authorized regional literacy coach and regional leadership literacy coach shall be employed by that RESA and report to its director.
- **R014 [Requirement; accountability] — (d), p. 5.** Both kinds of regional coach and each RESA director shall fully cooperate with the Georgia Literacy Coordinating Committee, State Literacy Director, and Georgia Literacy Coach Coordinator.
- **R015 [Requirement; accountability] — (e), p. 5.** Both kinds of regional coach shall fully cooperate with observation, evaluation, and feedback from the Georgia Literacy Coach Coordinator or designee.
- **R016 [Requirement; authority] — (f)(1), p. 6.** SBOE, collaborating with OSA, shall establish minimum qualifications and duties for regional literacy coaches.
- **R017 [Requirements; criteria, open minimum list] — (f)(2)(A)–(C), p. 6.** Regional literacy coaches shall have at least: (A) PSC certification; (B) five years of documented successful classroom teaching or literacy-intervention experience at assigned grade levels; (C) knowledge of scientifically based reading research, special expertise in quality reading instruction and intervention, dyslexia specific interventions, and data analysis. The list is expressly nonexclusive.
- **R018 [Requirements; duties; criteria, open minimum list] — (f)(3)(A)–(E), p. 6.** Minimum regional-coach duties shall include: (A) nonevaluative, evidence based coaching, including modeling, analyzing practice and data, actionable feedback, and side-by-side classroom support; (B) collaborating with principals, school based coaches, DOE representatives, and OSA representatives; (C) ongoing support for effective implementation and continuous improvement of comprehensive system and school literacy programs; (D) ongoing professional-learning activities and materials for teachers and coaches including science-of-reading research and curriculum based professional learning to apply training and research in practice; (E) analyzing screening, diagnostic, progress-monitoring, and other student data to inform instruction, grouping, and intervention within multi-tiered supports. The duties list is expressly nonexclusive.
- **R019 [Requirement; authority] — (g)(1), p. 7.** SBOE, collaborating with OSA, shall establish minimum qualifications and duties for regional leadership literacy coaches.
- **R020 [Requirements; criteria, open minimum list] — (g)(2)(A)–(I), p. 7.** Regional leadership coaches' minimum qualifications shall include: (A) PSC certification; (B) at least five years of documented success as literacy coach or classroom teacher or administrator in K–3; (C) the reading-research, instruction/intervention, dyslexia-intervention, and data-analysis knowledge/expertise specified above; (D) successful school, district, or regional literacy-program leadership experience, to include principal, curriculum director, superintendent, or state-level literacy specialist; (E) extensive knowledge of implementing a structured literacy program founded in the science of reading; (F) successful work with adult learners; (G) exceptional interpersonal skills; (H) exceptional communication skills; (I) successful leadership of educational programs and/or initiatives. List expressly nonexclusive.
- **R021 [Requirements; duties; criteria, open minimum list] — (g)(3)(A)–(I), pp. 7–8.** Minimum leadership-coach duties shall include: (A) attending and participating in literacy-improvement/coaching professional learning in the RESA network; (B) collaborating with superintendents and principals on literacy rich environments, prioritizing schools including K–3; (C) joining them in side-by-side classroom observations to support teachers and paraprofessionals applying reading science; (D) helping regional coaches prioritize system needs using student data and communication with leaders; (E) facilitating system/regional-coach communication and collaboration; (F) guiding development and implementation of local unified literacy plans; (G) facilitating system/community collaboration on birth-to-five literacy; (H) facilitating collaboration with local and regional postsecondary institutions; (I) collaborating with other regional leadership coaches. List expressly nonexclusive.

### §1-4: new §20-2-208.2 (pp. 8–9)

- **R022 [Requirement; authority; deadline; criteria] — (a), p. 8.** By **December 31, 2026**, PSC shall establish a literacy coaching endorsement for teachers and other certificated professional personnel trained in an evidence based approach to oral and written language teaching aligned to the science of reading, founded on how children learn to read, and characterized by explicit, systematic, cumulative, diagnostic instruction in the six enumerated domains: phonology, sound-symbol association, syllable instruction, morphology, syntax, and semantics.
- **R023 [Requirement; criteria, enumerated inclusion] — (b), pp. 8–9.** Endorsement requirements shall include training on universal reading screeners to measure and monitor foundational-literacy progress, providing guidance to parents, and providing training or guidance to other teachers and school personnel.

## Part II — Literacy Act, materials, and statewide bodies

### §2-1: amendments to §20-2-153.1 (pp. 9–19)

- **R024 [Intent; requirements within intent; criteria] — (a.1)(1)(A)–(D), p. 9.** General Assembly intent: early-literacy instruction shall be (A) comprehensive; (B) focused on structured literacy and foundational literacy skills; (C) aligned to the science of reading; (D) anchored by high-quality instructional materials aligned to that science. Four enumerated characteristics.
- **R025 [Intent; requirement within intent] — (a.1)(2), p. 9.** Educators shall have access to and be provided science-of-reading research and receive comprehensive curriculum based professional learning supporting its instructional application.
- **R026 [Intent; encouragement] — (a.1)(3), p. 9.** Ongoing parent/guardian involvement in comprehensive statewide literacy efforts shall be encouraged and integrated into their children's educations.
- **R027 [Definition; approval; publication] — (b)(6), p. 10.** “Dyslexia” shall have the meaning of the definition adopted and amended from time to time by the **International Dyslexia Association**, approved by the board, and posted on DOE and OSA websites. The bill incorporates that definition; it does not print its substantive diagnostic wording.
- **R028 [Definition; requirements; criteria] — new (b)(16)(A)–(C), p. 10.** “Unified literacy plan” means a comprehensive, evidence based framework ensuring coherent, aligned, continuous literacy development from birth through career readiness. As appropriate to grades served, the plan shall (A) establish measurable goals for all tested grades, using statewide assessments and approved screener data to monitor progress, guide instructional decisions, and evaluate program effectiveness; (B) include explicit collaboration strategies with birth-to-five early-learning providers for readiness and early-language development; (C) establish literacy transition plans from kindergarten to elementary school. Three enumerated, grade-conditioned components.
- **R029 [Requirement; authority] — (c)(1), p. 10.** SBOE, consulting DOE and OSA, shall establish necessary policies, rules, and regulations for uniform grade-appropriate literacy metrics.
- **R030 [Requirement; authority; deadline] — (c)(2)(A), p. 10.** By **March 1, 2027**, SBOE shall, on OSA's recommendation, approve high-quality instructional materials aligned to the science of reading for teaching K–3 students to read.
- **R031 [Requirement; authority; accountability; timing] — (c)(2)(B), p. 10.** By the same date, SBOE shall establish a procedure for every public school and local system to certify annually to DOE that its locally approved instructional materials and content, as defined in §20-2-1017(a), are high-quality materials aligned to the science of reading.
- **R032 [Requirement; authority; criteria; timing] — (c)(3), pp. 11–12.** By **April 1, 2028**, and April 1 every third year thereafter, on OSA's recommendation, SBOE shall approve a list of **at least two and no more than five** universal reading screeners for public schools/systems' comprehensive literacy programs; one shall be free. The number range is closed.
- **R033 [Requirements; criteria, enumerated with open sublists] — (c)(3)(A)–(D), pp. 11–12.** Every such screener shall (A) provide screening and diagnostic capabilities to monitor progress; (B) measure at minimum foundational reading skills, including but not limited to phonological awareness, oral language, alphabetic principle (including letter naming, letter sound, sound letter correspondences), decoding, encoding, accuracy, vocabulary, comprehension; (C) identify students significantly at risk of not attaining grade-level reading proficiency, including but not limited to characteristics of dyslexia; (D) have robust reporting capabilities, including but not limited to generating and directly providing DOE and OSA results by (i) student, (ii) subgroups by grade, educational program, demographic characteristics, and other Task Force criteria, (iii) school, (iv) local system. Four expressly enumerated reporting levels within an open capabilities requirement.
- **R034 [Requirements; publication; timing] — (c)(4), p. 12.** DOE shall publish and regularly update its public website's free screener and other approved-screener list; each public school and local system shall adopt and administer one listed screener as part of its comprehensive literacy program. The former July 15 publication deadline is deleted.
- **R035 [Requirement; timing; criteria, open list] — (c)(5)(A)–(D), pp. 12–13.** DOE shall publish on its public website and electronically distribute to each system and public school by **July 1 each year** a dyslexia informational handbook with guidance, technical assistance, and training on evidence based instruction for students with dyslexia characteristics. It shall include, but not be limited to: (A) evidence based practices specifically for those students; (B) support-plan guidance; (C) a list and brief description of dyslexia training programs accredited by the International Dyslexia Association and any other nationally or internationally recognized accrediting organization; (D) PSC-approved dyslexia endorsement programs, with approval in collaboration with OSA.
- **R036 [Requirement; authority; consultation; purpose] — (c)(6), pp. 13–14.** DOE shall develop or procure one or more K–3 teacher professional-learning programs on science of reading, structured literacy, and foundational skills enabling literacy standards, consulting University System of Georgia, PSC, OSA, Georgia Literacy Coordinating Committee, RESAs, and literacy experts. Programs shall emphasize applying reading research in instruction and using high-quality materials; shall promote teaching all students to read, including students with dyslexia; and shall ensure teachers and coaches can use approved screeners and assessment data for needs based instruction.
- **R037 [Requirement; authority; criteria; funding; timing] — (c)(6), pp. 13–14.** One program shall be a statewide comprehensive curriculum based professional-learning program in structured literacy aligned to and including science-of-reading research supporting K–3 teachers and literacy coaches in instructional application. It shall be recommended by the Georgia Literacy Task Force under §20-14-202 and made available **free to public-school K–3 teachers beginning no later than 2027–2028**.
- **R038 [Requirement; authority] — (c)(7), p. 14.** DOE shall provide technical assistance to public schools and systems implementing this Code section.
- **R039 [Requirement; consequence/accountability; timing; criteria, open list] — (c)(8)(A)–(B), p. 14.** By **November 1 annually**, DOE shall report implementation impacts, including but not limited to school/system results from (i) grade-three comprehensive summative assessments under §20-2-281(a), (ii) grades-one-and-two formative reading assessments under §20-2-280. Report shall also analyze impacts of universal screeners, high-quality materials, and instructional practices aligned to reading science. It shall go to the Governor, Senate President, House Speaker, chairpersons of House Education, Senate Education and Youth, House Higher Education, Senate Higher Education, House Appropriations, Senate Appropriations committees, and SBOE; and be publicly accessible on DOE's website.
- **R040 [Requirements; duties; criteria, enumerated inclusion] — (d)(2)(A)–(E), p. 15.** Utilizing school based coaches and other available resources, each public school and local system shall provide K–3 teachers instructional support including (A) onsite professional learning and research materials on reading science, structured literacy, foundational skills, and evidence based decision making; (B) demonstrated lessons supporting application of learning/research; (C) school based coaches working side-by-side with teachers in classrooms; (D) those coaches working directly with individual students and small groups **as time permits**; (E) prompt instructional-improvement feedback.
- **R041 [Requirement; authority; criteria, open list] — (d.1)(1)(A)–(I), pp. 15–16.** PSC shall adopt rules establishing core-curriculum criteria for every state-approved educator preparation program. Rules shall include, but not be limited to: (A) SBOE content standards aligned to reading science to guide curricula/instruction; (B) evidence based reading-teaching standards that (i) align exclusively to reading science, (ii) prepare explicit, systematic, sequential, diagnostic, cumulative teaching of phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, text comprehension, (iii) employ multisensory and multimodal interventions; (C) content based literacy and mathematics; (D) English-language-learner strategies; (E) disability strategies; (F) strategies including assessment results to identify strengths/weaknesses and differentiate instruction to needs; (G) practices supporting research based content aligned to state standards and grading practices; (H) early reading-deficiency identification; (I) technology and distance-learning support.
- **R042 [Requirements; authority/discretion; accountability; timing] — (d.1)(2), pp. 16–17.** Beginning **January 1, 2027**, PSC shall continually review each state-approved preparation program's compliance, especially (1)(B) and (F). Its Executive Director has discretion over review order/priority, but **every program shall be reviewed at least every four years**. By **November 1 annually**, PSC shall report its review and significant findings to: Chancellor of the Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia; governing body of each non-University-System state-approved program; SBOE; OSA; Georgia Council on Literacy; chairpersons of House Higher Education, Senate Higher Education, House Education, Senate Education and Youth committees. The Council recipient remains printed despite its separately accelerated abolition.
- **R043 [Authority; requirements; criteria, open minimum membership] — (e)(1), p. 17.** Establishes within OSA the **Georgia Literacy Coordinating Committee**. At minimum it shall comprise State Literacy Director, Georgia Literacy Coach Coordinator, a DOE representative, at least one RESA representative, representatives of research based education organizations with subject expertise identified by DOE and OSA, and other subject experts identified by DOE and OSA.
- **R044 [Requirements; duties; criteria] — (e)(1)(A)–(G), pp. 17–18.** Committee shall act in collaboration with SBOE, DOE, Department of Early Care and Learning, Department of Public Health, University System of Georgia, Technical College System of Georgia, and other appropriate OSA-determined stakeholders (open collaborator list), to: (A) maintain/amend as needed the Georgia Literacy Plan originally created by Georgia Council on Literacy; (B) review to ensure oversight, accountability, and effectiveness of this section and other Plan literacy efforts for schools/systems, children/families, adult learners; (C) support statewide program coordination across state/regional/local agencies, systems, initiatives; (D) review literacy-outcome conditions, needs, issues, problems and recommend legislation/appropriations for improvement; (E) review/recommend on local unified plans; (F) review statewide birth-to-five initiatives and suggest policy/appropriation changes; (G) review community-literacy best practices and recommend improvements. Seven enumerated duties.
- **R045 [Requirement; authority] — (e)(2), p. 18.** OSA shall employ State Literacy Director and Georgia Literacy Coach Coordinator, whose duties/responsibilities shall be provided in Article 4 of Chapter 14 of Title 20 (see R055–R058).
- **R046 [Requirement; deadline] — (f)(1), p. 18.** By **January 1, 2027**, each local board and other public-school governing body shall adopt a unified literacy plan.
- **R047 [Prohibition; consequence; condition; timing] — (f)(2)(A), p. 19.** From **January 1, 2027**, a local system without an adopted unified literacy plan is ineligible for any waiver/variance under §20-2-244 or Article 4 of Chapter 2, or to enter/renew a charter-system or strategic-waivers-system contract with SBOE.
- **R048 [Prohibition; condition; timing] — (f)(2)(B), p. 19.** From that date neither SBOE nor a local board shall authorize a new or renewal local-charter-school petition unless it includes that school's unified literacy plan; “local charter school” is cross-referenced to §20-2-2062.
- **R049 [Prohibition; condition; timing] — (f)(2)(C), p. 19.** From that date neither SBOE nor State Charter Schools Commission shall authorize a new or renewal state-charter-school petition without that school's unified plan; “state charter school” is cross-referenced to §20-2-2081.

### §2-2: revised §20-2-1010 (pp. 19–20)

- **R050 [Requirement; authority; definition; criteria, open] — (a), pp. 19–20.** SBOE shall prescribe by regulation “instructional materials and content” to include but not be limited to systematically designed material in any medium, including digital materials/content and necessary supporting hardware, software, technical equipment, constituting the principal source of study for a state-funded public-school course, including K–12. SBOE may regulate multiple listings for the various grades and may in its discretion authorize local systems and other public schools to choose between listed/adopted materials for a grade.
- **R051 [Exception denied; prohibition of construction] — (b), p. 20.** Nothing in this section exempts state-acquired computer hardware or related equipment from competitive bidding.
- **R052 [Policy; requirement; prohibition] — (c)(1), p. 20.** Notwithstanding contrary law, including this article, only high-quality materials/content aligned to reading science, as those terms are defined in §20-2-153.1, shall be used for K–3 literacy/reading education.
- **R053 [Prohibition; funding; condition; timing] — (c)(2), p. 20.** From **2029–2030 onward**, a public school receiving Article 6, Chapter 2 funds shall use no part of those funds to purchase or use K–3 literacy/reading materials/content not approved for that purpose by SBOE on the Task Force's recommendation.

### §2-3: new §20-2-1015.1 (pp. 20–21)

- **R054 [Requirement; encouragement; funding; timing] — (a)–(c), pp. 20–21.** (a) Local boards and other public-school governing bodies shall approve high-quality K–3 materials as required by §20-2-153.1. (b) They are **strongly encouraged** to provide exclusively for high-quality K–3 literacy/reading materials aligned to reading science, with terms cross-referenced to §20-2-153.1. (c) SBOE shall **annually** determine reasonable funding to help these bodies attain high-quality materials; that level shall annually be presented to the General Assembly for consideration of appropriations. No fixed sum or unconditional appropriation is printed. These are three separately checkable provisions with different force.

### §2-4: new Article 4, Chapter 14, §§20-14-200–204 (pp. 21–28)

- **R055 [Authority; requirement; permission/condition] — §20-14-200(a)–(b), p. 21.** Reestablishes/identifies the Coordinating Committee within OSA with membership/responsibilities as in §20-2-153.1, the Georgia Early Literacy and Dyslexia Act. On State Literacy Director recommendation, OSA is authorized to engage subject experts to work with it.
- **R056 [Requirement; authority; accountability] — §20-14-201 opening and (1), pp. 21–22.** OSA shall employ State Literacy Director, who shall report to OSA's director.
- **R057 [Requirements; duties; criteria] — §20-14-201(1)(A)–(G), p. 22.** Director shall be responsible for: (A) overseeing maintenance, amendment, implementation, alignment, evaluation of statewide plan collaboratively under §20-2-153.1; (B) evaluating/recommending continuous improvement/alignment of statewide literacy/reading programs across state/regional/local bodies; (C) primary liaison for statewide literacy/reading with DOE, Early Care and Learning, Public Health, University System, Technical College System, RESAs, PSC, OSA; (D) recommending effective implementation/continuous improvement of Georgia Early Literacy and Dyslexia Act and other literacy laws, policies, rules, regulations across all ages/grades; (E) chairing Coordinating Committee; (F) chairing Task Force; (G) supervising/evaluating staff assigned to either body, including but not limited to Coach Coordinator. Seven enumerated duties; staff examples open.
- **R058 [Requirement; authority; accountability; duties; criteria] — §20-14-201(2)(A)–(E), pp. 22–23.** OSA shall employ Georgia Literacy Coach Coordinator, who shall report to State Literacy Director and be responsible for: (A) Coordinating Committee membership; (B) statewide coordination of regional leadership, regional, and school based coaches; (C) collaboration/coordination with DOE- or RESA-affiliated state/federally funded literacy coaches and specialists, including but not limited to regional coaches; (D) observing, evaluating, and giving feedback to those coaches/specialists; (E) reviewing and collaborating in development/implementation of statewide literacy/reading professional-learning, development, and training programs. Five enumerated duties, with open coach examples.
- **R059 [Definition] — §20-14-202(a), p. 23.** “Task force” means Georgia Literacy Task Force established under this Code section.
- **R060 [Authority; requirement; deadline; purpose] — §20-14-202(b), p. 23.** Establishes a **standing** Georgia Literacy Task Force; OSA shall convene it by **September 1, 2026**. It shall recommend to SBOE, OSA, Coordinating Committee, State Literacy Director on the three subjects in R061–R067.
- **R061 [Requirement; criteria; funding] — §20-14-202(b)(1), p. 23.** Recommend the statewide comprehensive curriculum based structured-literacy professional-learning program, aligned to and including reading research for instructional application by K–3 teachers/coaches, to be available at no cost under §20-2-153.1.
- **R062 [Requirement; criteria, closed numerical range] — §20-14-202(b)(2)(A), p. 24.** Recommend **two to five** universal reading screeners that are valid and reliable reading-screening, formative, diagnostic assessment systems for system/public-school selection/use.
- **R063 [Requirements; criteria, enumerated with open sublists] — §20-14-202(b)(2)(B)(i)–(iv), p. 24.** Each recommended screener shall: (i) provide screening/diagnostic monitoring; (ii) measure at minimum foundational skills as cross-referenced to §20-2-153.1, including but not limited to phonological awareness, oral language, alphabetic principle (letter naming, letter sound, sound letter correspondences), decoding, encoding, accuracy, vocabulary, comprehension; (iii) identify significant reading-proficiency risk including but not limited to dyslexia characteristics; (iv) robustly report, including but not limited to directly generating/providing DOE and OSA results by student, subgroup (grade, program, demographics, other Task Force criteria), school, and system. This repeats the four capabilities required in §20-2-153.1(c)(3), at the recommendation stage.
- **R064 [Requirement; criteria, open minimum list] — §20-14-202(b)(2)(C)(i)–(iii), pp. 24–25.** Task Force shall at minimum consider administration time with intention of minimizing instructional-time impact, integration of results with teacher/student instructional support, and timeliness of results to teachers, administrators, parents.
- **R065 [Requirements; funding; deadlines] — §20-14-202(b)(2)(D)–(E), p. 25.** Task Force shall recommend **one free screener** and submit the recommended list, including that free screener, to SBOE by **April 1, 2028**, and April 1 every third year thereafter.
- **R066 [Prohibition; condition] — §20-14-202(b)(2)(F), p. 25.** SBOE shall approve no universal screener unless Task Force recommended it.
- **R067 [Requirements; criteria; deadlines] — §20-14-202(b)(3)(A)–(B), p. 25.** Task Force shall recommend to SBOE **two to five** systematically designed programs of high-quality K–3 instructional materials aligned to reading science (terms cross-referenced to §20-2-153.1); submit list by **April 1, 2027**, and April 1 every fourth year thereafter.

- **R069 [Requirements; authority; criteria, specified membership] — §20-14-202(c)(2)(A)–(E), pp. 25–26.** In addition to Director, membership shall include these appointed members, **each with at least three years' scientifically based reading-instruction experience**: (A) Governor appoints three, at least two with significant literacy/reading specialist, coach, teacher, or curriculum-specialist experience; (B) House Speaker appoints three with same two-member experience condition; (C) Senate President appoints three with same condition; (D) OSA appoints one who has been a certified academic dyslexia therapist for at least three years; (E) SBOE appoints one. The printed appointment list specifies eleven appointees plus chair; it does not say “but not limited to.”
