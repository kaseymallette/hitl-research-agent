## B5. Coverage

The analysis contains **LEX 3, PRE 2, FOS 1, SUB 4, INT 2: 12 observations total**. All five dimensions receive attention. FOS is thin for an explicit, document-based reason: the act regulates teacher preparation and credentials rather than specifying components of student instruction. PRE similarly acknowledges the absence of findings and outcome explanations. INT identifies both principal external regulatory anchors rather than stretching the text into research or interstate citations. No dimension is silently abandoned.

My own reading would add attention to preparation providers as a distinct regulated subject: (b)(2) requires website disclosure, (e) annual departmental reporting, and (f)(2) permits penalties up to approval revocation. These provisions combine public visibility, routine supervision, and institutional vulnerability; the analysis's state-arbiter discussion mentions providers but does not develop that position. My own LEX reading would also compare the title's “cursory knowledge” (page 1, lines 11-12) with the operative “awareness” language. My own FOS reading would consider the staggered deadlines and recurring reporting as mechanisms making reading knowledge administratively visible. These are additional interpretive opportunities, not required exhaustive coverage or factual errors.

The analysis is faithful to the short act and careful about the limits of its evidence. It preserves consequential legal distinctions and avoids treating absent instructional detail as automatic silencing. Its main weakness is stretching PRE-5 from instructional-method arbitration to credential administration; the qualified SUB-2 reading also warrants retaining its stated uncertainty. The researcher can weigh those coding boundaries alongside the five observations for which the current codebook has no suitable code. This judgment makes no acceptance decision.
