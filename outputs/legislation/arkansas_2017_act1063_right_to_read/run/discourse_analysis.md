# Discourse analysis: Arkansas Act 1063 (2017)

## LEX — Lexical choices and definitional framing

### Scientific authority without a substantive definition

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(b)(1)(A), introductory clause (page 1).
- **Passage:** “No later than 2023, the following shall have proficient knowledge and skills to teach reading consistent with the best practices of scientific reading instruction:”
- **Code:** LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language (qualified).
- **Reading:** “Scientific” authorizes the instructional standard, while “best practices” presents a preferred body of practice as already identifiable. Neither phrase supplies components, research-selection criteria, or a named research authority. This fits LEX-4's undefined scientific standard, although (b)(2) supplies an external assessment alignment point, so the act is not wholly without an institutional anchor. The wording does not itself establish a five-pillars, structured-literacy, or Simple View definition, and it names no prohibited method.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the absence of a substantive definition in this act and the authoritative work of the vocabulary. What the referenced assessment might specify cannot be established from this document.

### Knowledge divided into credential levels

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(d)(1)(A)-(B) (page 2).
- **Passage:** “All teachers employed in a teaching position that requires an elementary education (K-6) license or special education (K-12) license shall demonstrate proficiency in knowledge and practices of scientific reading instruction; and” / “All other teachers shall demonstrate awareness in knowledge and practices of the scientific reading instruction.”
- **Code:** no code fits.
- **Reading:** “Proficiency” and “awareness” turn knowledge of reading instruction into differentiated levels of professional obligation. The division here follows the license required by the teaching position and expressly includes special education across K-12 in the proficiency group. This lexical hierarchy defines who must know how much more clearly than it defines what reading science contains. It should not be projected back as a complete resolution of (b)(1), where the broad program-completer proficiency category is followed by an awareness requirement for non-elementary program completers.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the differentiated wording and coverage here; the act does not specify the substantive threshold separating the two levels.

### Reading named as a right

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(a) (page 1).
- **Passage:** “This section shall be known and may be cited as the "Right to Read Act".”
- **Code:** no code fits.
- **Reading:** The short title places the technical requirements under an entitlement-oriented name. Reading is framed as something owed, giving teacher preparation and credential regulation a moral purpose beyond administrative compliance. The title does not identify a right-holder or establish an individual remedy; the operative provisions organize institutional duties rather than elaborating the named right.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about how strongly the title should be read as an entitlement claim. It may function principally as a concise legislative label, although its rights vocabulary still frames the provisions that follow.

## PRE — Presuppositions

There are no findings or purpose provisions explaining reading outcomes. The strongest presuppositional observations concern the machinery of preparation and state authority, rather than explicit claims of universal learnability, crisis, or research consensus.

### A knowledge gap made actionable through preparation

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(c)(1), introductory clause and (A) (page 2).
- **Passage:** “Beginning with the 2018-2019 school year, a public school district and an open-enrollment public charter school shall provide the following professional development in scientific reading instruction:” / “For teachers licensed at the elementary level, professional development for one (1) of the prescribed pathways to obtaining a proficiency credential in knowledge and practices in scientific reading instruction; and”
- **Code:** PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap (reframed).
- **Reading:** The requirement treats additional, prescribed learning and a credential as necessary without first establishing what teachers currently know. The gap is institutionalized as something employers must address through provision, rather than asserted as individual teacher incompetence. Professional knowledge is treated as capable of being developed and certified. This supports a preparation-gap reading, but not the stronger PRE-2 claim that deficient instruction primarily causes low reading outcomes: no such outcomes or causal account are supplied.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain. The requirement may standardize evidence of knowledge teachers already possess, rather than presuppose that they lack the knowledge itself. The text establishes the obligation, not an empirical diagnosis of deficiency.

### State authority treated as the ordinary route to implementation

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(g)(1)-(2) (page 3).
- **Passage:** “The department is vested with the authority to and shall enforce this section.” / “The department shall promulgate rules to implement this section.”
- **Code:** PRE-5: Legislative Competence (qualified).
- **Reading:** These clauses explicitly confer authority while taking the appropriateness of departmental enforcement and rulemaking for granted. The text requires no justification for moving from an instructional label to a state-administered credential and compliance system. The qualification matters: the legislature specifies obligations and delegates implementation, but does not itself choose or ban a named classroom method. Its assumed competence is clearest in regulating professional preparation and certification.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the assignment of authority. Reading the absence of a justification as a presupposition is interpretive, but does not require assuming that the act directly settles particular teaching methods.

## FOS — Foregrounding and silencing

### Credential pathways foregrounded instead of instructional components

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(c)(1)(A)-(B) (page 2), under the introductory requirement to provide professional development.
- **Passage:** “For teachers licensed at the elementary level, professional development for one (1) of the prescribed pathways to obtaining a proficiency credential in knowledge and practices in scientific reading instruction; and” / “For teachers licensed at a level other than the elementary level, professional development for one (1) of the prescribed pathways to obtaining an awareness credential in knowledge and practices in scientific reading instruction.”
- **Code:** no code fits.
- **Reading:** The two-item list organizes professional development by teacher category and credential destination. It makes pathways and proof of professional knowledge the visible implementation mechanisms; it does not enumerate the instructional content of the pathways. None of FOS-1 to FOS-3 describes this focus: there is no priority given here to phonics, dyslexia, or student screening. The stand-alone assessment in (b)(2) is an alignment reference for preparation, not a universal student screening mandate.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about what this list foregrounds. It does not establish the relative attention any eventual training gives particular reading components.

This act offers little defensible evidence for the codebook's specific silencing codes. The quoted list specifies credential routes, not what literacy instruction must contain; therefore the absence of comprehension, background knowledge, oral language, and writing from it does not warrant FOS-4 or FOS-7. Nor does the act set out learner-facing instruction, screening, or intervention requirements sufficiently to infer FOS-5 from the lack of multilingual provisions. It gives no explanation of reading outcomes against which to establish the exclusion of structural causes under FOS-6. Special education is expressly present in (d)(1)(A), quoted above, but that teacher-license category is neither a dyslexia provision nor a counter-example establishing attention to multilingual learners. These limits prevent a narrow credential statute from being treated as an exhaustive account of reading instruction.

## SUB — Subject positioning

### Teachers regulated, with an obligation on employers to support learning

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(d)(2), introductory clause and (A) (pages 2-3); § 6-17-429(c)(1), introductory clause (page 2).
- **Passage:** “by completing the prescribed proficiency or awareness in knowledge and practices of the scientific reading instruction credential either:” / “As a condition of licensure; or” / “a public school district and an open-enrollment public charter school shall provide the following professional development in scientific reading instruction:”
- **Code:** SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated (partial); SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional (partial).
- **Reading:** Teachers become subjects who must demonstrate authorized knowledge through a prescribed credential. Licensure makes the requirement consequential, although (d)(2)(B) also provides a one-year completion route for already licensed teachers and those employed under a licensure waiver. The employer's duty to provide development positions teachers as entitled to institutional learning support as well as obliged to qualify. SUB-1 fits regulation more securely than deficiency; SUB-2 fits supported learning, but the act does not expressly foreground teacher judgment, collaboration, or autonomy.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about regulation and required provision. Uncertain about interpreting that provision as a broader developing-professional identity; it can also be read as support designed principally to secure compliance.

### Districts made answerable for employment and training

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(f)(1)(A) (page 3).
- **Passage:** “A public school district that employs an educator in violation of this section or that does not provide the professional development as required under this section shall be in violation of the Standards for Accreditation of Arkansas Public Schools and Schools Districts and may be placed in probationary status by the department.”
- **Code:** SUB-5: District as Compliance Target.
- **Reading:** Districts are positioned as accountable employers and providers, with institutional standing tied to the credential system. The mandatory accreditation violation and discretionary probation give the department supervisory power without making every consequence automatic. This differs from (c)(2)'s mandatory public-school probation for failure to provide development; preserving the distinct wording avoids making enforcement appear more uniform than the act makes it.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain. The relationship between the two probation formulations remains unresolved in the act.

### Parents entitled to information about institutional failure

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(f)(1)(B) (page 3).
- **Passage:** “A public school district placed in probationary status under subdivision (f)(1)(A) of this section shall send written notification to the parents of the students in the public school district of the reason for being placed in probationary status.”
- **Code:** SUB-4: Parent as Rights-Holder (qualified).
- **Reading:** The mandatory written notice positions parents as legitimate recipients of an explanation of district failure. This is a bounded informational entitlement triggered by probation, not an express power to select methods or participate in credential decisions. Students appear here through their parents' notification relationship rather than as speakers or directly empowered claimants.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the notice entitlement and its trigger. The passage does not support a stronger claim about parental control over instruction or student victimhood.

### The department as implementing arbiter

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(g)(1)-(2) (page 3).
- **Passage:** “The department is vested with the authority to and shall enforce this section.” / “The department shall promulgate rules to implement this section.”
- **Code:** SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter (partial).
- **Reading:** The department is the active enforcer and rulemaker, while teachers, districts, and preparation providers must satisfy the act's requirements. The closing clauses concentrate implementation authority in a named public institution. The arbiter position is explicit; a rescuer narrative is not, since the text does not describe a prior reading crisis or local instructional failure that the state will overcome.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the arbiter position; the partial qualifier excludes an unsupported rescue claim.

## INT — Intertextuality

### Another statutory assessment supplies an alignment anchor

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(b)(2) (page 2).
- **Passage:** “scientific reading instruction aligned with but not limited to the content measured by the stand-alone reading assessment adopted by the State Board of Education under § 6-17-402.”
- **Code:** no code fits.
- **Reading:** The act invokes another Arkansas statutory provision and a board-adopted assessment to anchor the preparation program's publicly described instruction. Authority is routed through an existing legal and assessment apparatus rather than a named study or professional organization's definition. “But not limited to” keeps that anchor from becoming an exhaustive boundary. The reference is intertextual, but the codebook has no category for this intrastate statutory/assessment linkage; it is not INT-3's reference to another state or INT-6's invocation of a historical reading policy as precedent.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the reference and its non-exclusive wording. The assessment's content and research basis are not reproduced and cannot be inferred here.

### Accreditation standards recruited as enforcement authority

- **Where:** Section 1, § 6-17-429(f)(1)(A) (page 3).
- **Passage:** “shall be in violation of the Standards for Accreditation of Arkansas Public Schools and Schools Districts and may be placed in probationary status by the department.”
- **Code:** no code fits.
- **Reading:** Naming the standards connects the act's reading-knowledge obligations to institutional accreditation. The cross-reference makes failure legible within another regulatory framework, giving the new duties consequences beyond the credential itself. It supplies administrative authority rather than research evidence; the document does not reproduce the standards or establish their independent instructional content.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain.

The act names no federal reading report, professional organization, researcher, study, media report, advocacy report, other state's policy, or opposing instructional program. INT-1 through INT-7 therefore supply no positive fit for its principal references. That limitation concerns this document alone and establishes nothing about an unseen legislative record or implicit borrowing.
