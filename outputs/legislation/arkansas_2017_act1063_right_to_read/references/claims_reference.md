## Inventory in document order

### Title, purpose, amendment, and short title

- **P01 — Declared purposes.** Page 1, lines 7–13, act title: create the Right to Read Act; require licensed teachers at the elementary level to be proficient in scientific reading instruction; require all other licensed teachers to have a cursory knowledge of scientific reading instruction; and provide for other purposes. The final phrase leaves the purpose statement open. The title uses “cursory knowledge”; the operative provisions below use awareness and proficiency requirements as specified there.
- **P02 — Subtitle purpose.** Page 1, lines 16–17: create the Right to Read Act. There are no separately stated legislative findings or empirical findings anywhere in the document.
- **P03 — Amendment adding a section.** Section 1, page 1, lines 22–24: amend Arkansas Code Title 6, Chapter 17, Subchapter 4 by adding a new section, numbered 6-17-429 and headed “Right to Read Act”. This is an addition, not a replacement of quoted existing statutory language.
- **P04 — Mandatory name and permitted citation.** Subsection (a), page 1, lines 25–26: the section **shall** be known as, and **may** be cited as, the “Right to Read Act”. This supplies a short title, not a substantive definition of scientific reading instruction.

### Preparation programs and people completing programs or seeking licensure

- **P05 — Proficiency for program completers.** Subsection (b)(1)(A)(i), page 1, lines 27–31: **no later than 2023**, a person who completes a state-approved educator preparation program **shall have proficient knowledge and skills to teach reading consistent with the best practices of scientific reading instruction**. This is the first member of the closed two-item list in (b)(1)(A).
- **P06 — Proficiency for reciprocity or endorsement applicants.** Subsection (b)(1)(A)(ii), page 1, lines 27–29 and 32–33: **no later than 2023**, a person seeking teacher licensure **by reciprocity or by adding an endorsement shall have** the same proficient knowledge and skills described in P05. These are the two specified licensure routes within the second member of the (b)(1)(A) list.
- **P07 — Awareness for non-elementary program completers.** Subsection (b)(1)(B), page 1, lines 34–36 through page 2, line 1: **no later than 2023**, a person who completes a state-approved educator preparation program **other than a teacher of elementary education program shall demonstrate an awareness of the best practices of scientific reading instruction**. Preserve this qualification as printed. Subsection (b)(1)(A)(i) itself contains no express elementary-only limitation, and (B) does not expressly say that it replaces or waives (A)(i); the inventory does not resolve their overlap by rewriting either provision.
- **P08 — Program website disclosure and assessment alignment.** Subsection (b)(2), page 2, lines 2–7: **beginning no later than the 2022-2023 school year**, **each state-approved educator preparation program shall post on its website** information describing its program to prepare teachers to teach reading using scientific reading instruction. That instruction is to be **aligned with but not limited to** the content measured by the **stand-alone reading assessment adopted by the State Board of Education under § 6-17-402**. The assessment content is an expressly non-exhaustive reference standard; the document does not reproduce the assessment, its content, or § 6-17-402. The board's assessment-adoption role is referenced here, rather than created as a separate new command to adopt an assessment.

### District and charter-school professional development; school consequences

- **P09 — Elementary-level professional development.** Subsections (c)(1) and (c)(1)(A), page 2, lines 8–14: **beginning with the 2018-2019 school year**, **a public school district and an open-enrollment public charter school shall provide** professional development in scientific reading instruction for teachers licensed at the elementary level. It must be professional development for **one (1) of the prescribed pathways to obtaining a proficiency credential in knowledge and practices in scientific reading instruction**.
- **P10 — Other-level professional development.** Subsections (c)(1) and (c)(1)(B), page 2, lines 8–10 and 15–18: the same district and charter-school providers, beginning with the same **2018-2019 school year**, **shall provide**, for teachers licensed at a level **other than the elementary level**, professional development for **one (1) of the prescribed pathways to obtaining an awareness credential in knowledge and practices in scientific reading instruction**. Together (A) and (B) form the closed two-part professional-development list. The act references prescribed pathways but does not enumerate them or identify a particular course, curriculum, or provider.
- **P11 — Mandatory school probation for failure to provide development.** Subsection (c)(2)(A), page 2, lines 19–22: **beginning with the 2020-2021 school year**, a **public school that does not provide the professional development under (c)(1) shall be placed in probationary status**. The subject here is “a public school”; the provision does not name the actor who must place it on probation. This mandatory consequence must be kept distinct from the department's discretionary district-probation power in P19.
- **P12 — School notice to parents.** Subsection (c)(2)(B), page 2, lines 19–21 and 23–24: on the same failure condition and **beginning with the 2020-2021 school year**, the **public school shall provide notice to parents that the public school district has not met the requirements of this section**. The text does not specify written notice here. Probation and notice are the two cumulative, closed-list consequences in (c)(2), joined by “and”.

### Teachers' demonstration and credential requirements

- **P13 — Elementary and special-education teaching positions.** Subsections (d)(1) and (d)(1)(A), page 2, lines 25–29: **by the beginning of the 2021-2022 school year**, **all teachers employed in a teaching position that requires an elementary education (K-6) license or special education (K-12) license shall demonstrate proficiency in knowledge and practices of scientific reading instruction**. Coverage turns on the license required for the position; special education is expressly included across K-12.
- **P14 — All other teachers.** Subsections (d)(1) and (d)(1)(B), page 2, lines 25 and 30–31: **by the beginning of the 2021-2022 school year**, **all other teachers shall demonstrate awareness in knowledge and practices of the scientific reading instruction**. Together P13 and P14 state the two exhaustive teacher categories in (d)(1).
- **P15 — Credential completion by teachers beginning employment.** Subsection (d)(2), page 2, lines 32–36 through page 3, line 1: **all teachers who begin employment in the 2021-2022 school year and each school year thereafter shall demonstrate proficiency or awareness in knowledge and practices in scientific reading instruction, as applicable to their teaching position, by completing the prescribed proficiency or awareness credential**. The credential is the prescribed credential in knowledge and practices of scientific reading instruction. The two routes/timing alternatives in P16–P17 complete this requirement; it is not an unconditional one-year allowance for all teachers.
- **P16 — Licensure condition.** Subsection (d)(2)(A), page 3, lines 1–2, continuing P15: completion of the applicable prescribed credential is **a condition of licensure** under the first of the two alternatives, joined by “or”.
- **P17 — One-year alternative for specified teachers.** Subsection (d)(2)(B)(i)–(ii), page 3, lines 3–5, continuing P15: complete the applicable credential **within one (1) year if the teacher is either already licensed or employed under a waiver from licensure**. This is a closed list of two qualifying circumstances for the one-year alternative. The text recognizes employment under a licensure waiver; it does not itself grant a waiver, describe a waiver process, or exempt those teachers from the reading-credential obligation. It says “within one (1) year” without separately stating the event from which the year runs.

### Reporting, accreditation, penalties, and implementation

- **P18 — Annual program-provider report.** Subsection (e), page 3, lines 6–9: **a provider of a state-approved educator preparation program shall include in its annual report to the Department of Education** a description of the provider's program to prepare educators to teach reading using scientific reading instruction. The annual report is the specified vehicle; no calendar due date or separate commencement date is stated.
- **P19 — District accreditation violation and possible probation.** Subsection (f)(1)(A), page 3, lines 10–14: **a public school district that employs an educator in violation of this section OR does not provide professional development as required under this section shall be in violation of the Standards for Accreditation of Arkansas Public Schools and Schools Districts**, and **may be placed in probationary status by the department**. The two alternative triggers are a closed list; either suffices. The accreditation violation is mandatory on the stated condition; department-imposed probation is discretionary. The printed standards name contains “Schools Districts”; it is retained here rather than corrected. This provision supplies an accountability consequence for noncompliant employment as well as for missing professional development.
- **P20 — Written district notification following probation.** Subsection (f)(1)(B), page 3, lines 15–18: **a public school district placed in probationary status under (f)(1)(A) shall send written notification to the parents of the students in the public school district of the reason for being placed in probationary status**. Unlike P12, this provision expressly requires writing and is conditioned on probation under (f)(1)(A), not simply the underlying failure. No notification deadline is specified.
- **P21 — Possible penalties for noncompliant program providers.** Subsection (f)(2), page 3, lines 19–21: **a provider of a state-approved educator preparation program that does not comply with the requirements of this section may be subject to penalties up to and including having the provider's approval status revoked**. Penalties are discretionary, and revocation is expressly included as the upper stated consequence. The text does not enumerate intermediate penalties, make revocation automatic, or expressly identify the actor imposing these penalties in (f)(2); general departmental enforcement authority appears in P22.
- **P22 — Departmental enforcement authority and duty.** Subsection (g)(1), page 3, lines 22–23: **the department is vested with the authority to enforce this section and shall enforce it**. This is both a grant of authority and a mandatory duty. “Department of Education” is named in (e); subsequent provisions refer to “the department” without adding a separate definition.
- **P23 — Departmental rulemaking duty.** Subsection (g)(2), page 3, lines 24–25: **the department shall promulgate rules to implement this section**. No rulemaking deadline or list of required rule contents is specified.

## Category index and express absences

### Definitions

There is no formal definitions subsection or substantive definition of a term. P04 establishes the statutory name and permitted citation. In particular, the document does not define scientific reading instruction, best practices, proficiency, awareness, credentials, or prescribed pathways. The license grade spans in P13 are explicit coverage criteria, not definitions of reading instruction.

### Findings and purpose

P01–P02 contain the title/subtitle purposes. No separate findings, research findings, or statement of legislative intent appears.

### Requirements and authority

P04–P20 and P22–P23 contain the mandatory naming, knowledge, demonstration, disclosure, professional-development, probation, notice, credential, annual-report, accreditation, enforcement, and rulemaking provisions, subject to each entry's stated actor and condition. P21 is a possible consequence, not a mandatory sanction. P19 gives the department discretion over district probation; P22–P23 give it enforcement authority and duties. P08 names the State Board of Education's assessment role. The district, charter school, school, teacher, program, and program-provider duties remain assigned to the actors printed in their respective provisions.

### Prohibitions

No separate “shall not”, “must not”, or other express prohibition is printed. P19 makes employment of an educator in violation of the section an accreditation violation and permits probation; the underlying affirmative obligations and this consequence should not be recast as a separately worded categorical prohibition.

### Permissions and encouragement

The explicit permissions/discretions are: the section **may be cited** by its short title (P04); a district **may be placed** on probation by the department (P19); a noncompliant provider **may be subject** to penalties through approval revocation (P21). There are no “should” provisions or express encouragements. P17 is a conditional timing alternative, not a waiver of the reading obligation.

### Criteria and lists

- P05–P06: closed two-item list of people subject to (b)(1)(A), with reciprocity or endorsement as the two named licensure routes in the second item.
- P07: non-elementary-program qualification; preserve its relationship to the unqualified program-completer language in P05 without adding an unstated exception.
- P08: expressly open assessment-content alignment standard (“aligned with but not limited to”); no itemized instructional components are supplied.
- P09–P10: closed elementary/other-level development categories; one prescribed pathway toward the applicable credential, but no pathways enumerated.
- P11–P12: closed cumulative pair of school consequences, probation **and** parent notice.
- P13–P14: elementary K-6/special-education K-12 position criterion and all-other-teachers category.
- P15–P17: applicable proficiency/awareness credential; closed alternatives of licensure condition **or** one-year completion, the latter conditional on either of two specified teacher circumstances.
- P19: two alternative district-violation triggers. P21: noncompliance condition and penalty range up to and including approval revocation, without an exhaustive list of penalties within that range.

### Consequences and accountability

P08 (website disclosure), P11–P12 (mandatory school probation and parent notice), P15–P17 (credential and licensure/timing conditions), P18 (annual reporting), P19 (accreditation violation and possible district probation), P20 (written parent notification), P21 (possible provider penalties and approval revocation), P22–P23 (enforcement and implementing rules).

### Exceptions, qualifications, and waivers

P07 provides the distinct awareness requirement for non-elementary program completers. P09–P10 and P13–P15 differentiate requirements by license level or teaching position. P17 allows one-year credential completion for teachers already licensed or employed under a licensure waiver. No general exemption from the section, new power to waive its requirements, or waiver procedure is stated.

### Funding and timing

- **No later than 2023:** P05–P07 knowledge/skills and awareness requirements.
- **Beginning no later than 2022-2023:** P08 program website information.
- **Beginning with 2018-2019:** P09–P10 professional development.
- **Beginning with 2020-2021:** P11–P12 mandatory school consequences for failure to provide that development.
- **By the beginning of 2021-2022:** P13–P14 teacher proficiency/awareness demonstrations.
- **Teachers beginning employment in 2021-2022 and each year thereafter:** P15–P17 applicable credential, as a licensure condition or within one year under the specified circumstances.
- **Annual:** P18 program-provider reporting, without a stated calendar due date.
