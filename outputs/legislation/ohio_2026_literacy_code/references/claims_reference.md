## 1. Section 3313.6028 — Literacy curriculum

**L01 — Header, p. 1 [funding/timing; named source].** The section is presented under Ohio Revised Code, Title 33 Education-Libraries, Chapter 3313 Boards of Education. Printed effective date: October 9, 2026. Latest legislation: Senate Bill 19 (GA 136), House Bill 455 (GA 136).

**L02 — (A)(1), p. 1 [definition; criteria/list; finding/purpose].** For Title XXXIII of the Revised Code, “science of reading” means an interdisciplinary body of scientific evidence with all four specified characteristics:

- (a) It informs how students learn to read and write proficiently.
- (b) It explains why some students have difficulty with reading and writing.
- (c) It indicates that all students benefit from explicit and systematic instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension, and writing to become effective readers. All six instructional areas are enumerated.
- (d) It does not rely on any model of teaching students to read based on meaning, structure and syntax, and visual cues, including a three-cueing approach. “Including” makes three-cueing an included example within the excluded class, rather than the sole excluded model.

**L03 — (A)(2), p. 1 [definition; criteria/list].** In this section, “three-cueing approach” means any model of teaching students to read based on meaning, structure and syntax, and visual cues.

**L04 — (B), first paragraph, p. 1 [requirement; authority; criteria/list].** The department of education and workforce shall establish two lists: (1) high-quality core curriculum and instructional materials in English language arts, and (2) evidence-based reading intervention programs. These must be aligned with the science of reading and strategies for effective literacy instruction. The actual products/programs are not listed in the document.

**L05 — (B), second paragraph, pp. 1–2 [requirement; authority; criteria/list; consequence].** The department shall establish a rubric and scoring system to evaluate whether, or to what extent, core English language arts curricula facilitate a student's skill building and sequential advancement through mastery; shall post both on its publicly accessible website; and, upon request, shall provide a scored rubric to the entity that submitted a curriculum for scoring and to any member of the Ohio general assembly.

**L06 — (C), p. 2 [requirement; prohibition; criteria/list; funding/timing; named source].** Beginning no later than the 2024-2025 school year, each school district, community school established under Chapter 3314., and STEM school established under Chapter 3326. shall use only items from the (B) lists for (1) core English language arts curriculum and instructional materials in each of grades pre-kindergarten to five, and (2) evidence-based reading intervention programs in each of grades pre-kindergarten to twelve. The grade ranges differ and the list restriction is exclusive.

**L07 — (C), second sentence, p. 2 [prohibition; exception].** Except under (D), no district or school shall use core curriculum, instructional materials, or an intervention program in grades pre-kindergarten to five that uses the three-cueing approach to teach reading.

**L08 — (D), opening, p. 2 [permission; authority; exception].** A district or school may apply to the department for an individual-student waiver to use curriculum, instructional materials, or an intervention program employing three-cueing to teach reading in grades pre-kindergarten through five, subject to (D)(1) and (2).

**L09 — (D)(1), p. 2 [prohibition; exception; named source].** No student with a reading improvement and monitoring plan developed under section 3313.608(C) is eligible for a waiver.

**L10 — (D)(2), p. 2 [exception; prohibition].** A student whose individualized education program explicitly indicates that three-cueing is appropriate for the student's learning needs shall not be required to have a waiver.

**L11 — (D), concluding paragraph, p. 2 [requirement; authority; criteria/list; named source].** When deciding whether to approve a waiver, the department shall consider the student's district or school's performance on the state report card issued under section 3302.03, including its early literacy component. The inclusion is mandatory and is not an exhaustive statement of all possible considerations.

**L12 — (E)(1), p. 3 [requirement; authority; criteria/list].** The department shall identify vendors providing educators with professional development on use of the high-quality core curriculum, instructional materials, and reading intervention programs on the (B) lists. Educators expressly include pre-service teachers and faculty employed by educator preparation programs; this is an inclusive, not exclusive, enumeration.

**L13 — (E)(2), p. 3 [requirement; authority; consequence; named source].** A professional development committee established under section 3319.22 shall qualify any completed professional development coursework from an (E)(1) vendor to count toward coursework requirements for teacher licensure renewal.

**L14 — (E)(3), p. 3 [requirement; permission; consequence; funding/timing].** A professional development committee shall permit a teacher to apply any hours earned over the minimum professional development coursework hours required for teacher licensure renewal under (E)(2) to the next renewal period for that license.

**L15 — (F), p. 3 [requirement; authority; consequence; funding/timing; named source].** No later than June 30 of each year, the department shall prepare and issue a report on implementation of this section, including implementation by districts and schools. It shall provide the report to the governor and, in accordance with section 101.68, to the general assembly. The report's scope is inclusive, not limited to district/school implementation.

## 2. Section 3313.608 — Third-grade reading guarantee

**G01 — Header, p. 5 [funding/timing; named source].** This section appears under Ohio Revised Code, Title 33 Education-Libraries, Chapter 3313 Boards of Education. Printed effective date: September 30, 2025. Latest legislation: House Bill 96 - 136th General Assembly.

### Promotion and retention — (A)

**G02 — (A)(1), p. 5 [requirement; permission; criteria/list; consequence; exception; funding/timing; named source].** Beginning with students entering third grade in the school year starting July 1, 2009, and until June 30, 2013, the following rule applies unless the student is excused from the assessment under section 3301.0711(C). For a student who fails to attain at least the equivalent achievement level designated under section 3301.0710(A)(3) on that section's assessment of English language arts skills expected at the end of third grade, each school district, in accordance with its section 3313.609 policy, shall choose one of three enumerated dispositions (a closed set of alternatives):

- (a) Promote to fourth grade if the principal and reading teacher agree that other reading-skill evaluations demonstrate academic preparedness for fourth grade.
- (b) Promote to fourth grade with intensive intervention services in fourth grade.
- (c) Retain in third grade.

**G03 — (A)(2), opening, p. 5 [prohibition; consequence; exception; funding/timing; named source].** Beginning with students entering third grade in 2013-2014, unless excused from the assessment under section 3301.0711(C), no district shall promote to fourth grade a student who fails to attain at least the equivalent achievement level under section 3301.0710(A)(3) on that section's end-of-third-grade English language arts assessment unless one of (a)–(f) applies. These are six enumerated alternative exceptions, not cumulative requirements; the assessment excuse is separate.

**G04 — (A)(2)(a), p. 6 [exception; criteria/list; funding/timing].** The student is an English learner who both has been enrolled in United States schools for less than three full school years and has received less than three years of English-as-a-second-language instruction.

**G05 — (A)(2)(b), p. 6 [exception; criteria/list; named source].** The student is a child with a disability entitled to special education and related services under Chapter 3323., and the student's individualized education program exempts the student from retention under this division.

**G06 — (A)(2)(c), p. 6 [exception; authority; criteria/list].** The student demonstrates an acceptable level of performance on an alternative standardized reading assessment, as determined by the department of education and workforce.

**G07 — (A)(2)(d)(i)–(iv), p. 6 [exception; criteria/list; funding/timing; named source].** All four conditions must apply: (i) the student is a child with a disability entitled to special education and related services under Chapter 3323.; (ii) the student took the third-grade English language arts achievement assessment under section 3301.0710; (iii) the student's individualized education program or section 504 plan shows two school years of intensive reading remediation but continuing reading deficiency; and (iv) the student previously was retained in any of grades kindergarten to three. The section 504 citation is the “Rehabilitation Act of 1973,” 87 Stat. 355, 29 U.S.C. 794, as amended.

**G08 — (A)(2)(e)(i), p. 6 [exception; criteria/list; funding/timing].** The student received intensive reading remediation for two school years, still demonstrates a reading deficiency, and previously was retained in any of grades kindergarten to three. These conditions are cumulative; this exception does not itself require a disability.

**G09 — (A)(2)(e)(ii), p. 7 [requirement; consequence; criteria/list].** A student promoted under (e)(i) shall continue intensive reading instruction in grade four. It shall include an altered instructional day with specialized diagnostic information and specific research-based reading strategies for that student that have succeeded in improving reading among low-performing readers. These are required inclusions, not an exhaustive curriculum specification.

**G10 — (A)(2)(f), first paragraph, p. 7 [exception; permission; criteria/list; authority].** A parent or guardian, in consultation with the student's reading teacher and building principal, requests promotion to fourth grade regardless of whether the student is reading at grade level.

**G11 — (A)(2)(f), second paragraph, p. 7 [requirement; consequence; funding/timing].** A student promoted under (f) shall continue intensive reading instruction in the same manner as a retained student until able to read at grade level.

### Assessment, notification, intervention, and retained students — (B)

**G12 — (B)(1), p. 7 [finding/purpose; requirement; authority; consequence; funding/timing].** Beginning in 2012-2013, to assist students in meeting the third-grade guarantee, each school district board of education shall adopt policies and procedures under which it annually assesses every kindergarten-through-third-grade student's reading skills and identifies students reading below grade level. The reading skills assessment shall be completed by September 30.

**G13 — (B)(1), p. 7 [requirement; criteria/list; named source].** Each district shall use a diagnostic assessment appropriate to the grade, adopted or approved under section 3301.079, to identify those students. Its policies and procedures shall require classroom teachers' involvement in assessing and identifying students reading below grade level.

**G14 — (B)(1), last sentence, p. 7 [permission; criteria/list].** The assessment may be administered electronically through live, two-way video and audio connections; the administering teacher may be in a separate location from the student.

**G15 — (B)(2), pp. 7–8 [requirement; consequence; criteria/list].** For every student the prescribed diagnostic assessment identifies as reading below grade level, the district shall do both (a), written parent/guardian notification, and (b), immediate services and assessments, detailed in G16–G22. These are both required, not alternatives.

**G16 — (B)(2)(a)(i), p. 8 [requirement; consequence].** Written information to the parent or guardian shall notify them that the student has been identified as having a substantial reading deficiency.

**G17 — (B)(2)(a)(ii), p. 8 [requirement; consequence].** It shall describe the services currently provided to the student.

**G18 — (B)(2)(a)(iii), p. 8 [requirement; consequence; finding/purpose].** It shall describe proposed supplemental instructional services and supports that will be provided and are designed to remediate the identified areas of reading deficiency.

**G19 — (B)(2)(a)(iv), first sentence, p. 8 [requirement; consequence; exception; named source].** It shall notify the parent or guardian that a student scoring in the range designated under section 3301.0710(A)(3) on that section's end-of-third-grade English language arts assessment shall be retained unless exempt under (A).

**G20 — (B)(2)(a)(iv), second sentence, p. 8 [requirement; consequence; finding/purpose; named source].** The notification shall specify that the section 3301.0710 assessment is not the sole determinant of promotion and that additional evaluations and assessments are available to help parents and the district know when a student reads at or above grade level and is ready for promotion.

**G21 — (B)(2)(a)(v), p. 8 [requirement; consequence].** It shall contain a statement connecting the child's reading proficiency level to long-term outcomes of success related to reading proficiency. All five (a)(i)–(v) notification components are required; the text does not make them optional examples.

**G22 — (B)(2)(b), p. 8 [requirement; consequence; criteria/list; funding/timing; named source].** Immediately after identifying a reading deficiency and until development of the (C) reading improvement and monitoring plan, the district shall provide intensive reading instruction services and regular diagnostic assessments. Intervention services shall align with the science of reading as defined in section 3313.6028 and include both research-based reading strategies shown successful with low-performing readers and instruction targeted to the student's identified deficiencies. These are mandatory inclusions.

**G23 — (B)(3)(a), p. 9 [requirement; consequence; criteria/list; funding/timing].** For every student retained under (A), the district shall provide intense remediation until the student can read at grade level. It shall include intensive reading interventions addressing identified deficiencies, including, but not limited to, at least ninety minutes of reading instruction per day. This is explicitly an open set of interventions with a mandatory daily minimum.

**G24 — (B)(3)(a)(i)–(vii), p. 9 [permission; criteria/list].** Remediation may include any of seven specified options: (i) small group instruction; (ii) reduced teacher-student ratios; (iii) more frequent progress monitoring; (iv) tutoring or mentoring; (v) transition classes containing third- and fourth-grade students; (vi) extended school day, week, or year; (vii) summer reading camps. These are optional choices within G23's expressly nonexclusive intervention framework, not seven mandatory components.

**G25 — (B)(3)(b), p. 9 [requirement; authority; consequence; criteria/list].** The district shall establish a mid-year promotion policy for students retained under (A) who demonstrate reading at or above grade level.

**G26 — (B)(3)(c), p. 9 [requirement; consequence; criteria/list].** The district shall provide each student retained under (A) with a teacher satisfying one or more of the (H) criteria. All three duties in (B)(3)(a)–(c) are required.

**G27 — (B)(3), concluding paragraph, pp. 9–10 [requirement; permission; authority; consequence; criteria/list].** The district shall offer students the option of applicable services from one or more providers other than the district. Providers shall be screened and approved by the district or the department. If a student participates in remediation and demonstrates reading proficiency according to department-adopted standards before fourth grade starts, the district shall promote the student to fourth grade.

**G28 — (B)(4), p. 10 [requirement; consequence; criteria/list].** For each student retained under (A) who has demonstrated proficiency in a specific academic ability field, each district shall provide instruction in that field commensurate with the student's achievement levels.

**G29 — (B)(4), concluding sentence, p. 10 [definition; named source].** For this division, “specific academic ability field” has the same meaning as in section 3324.01. Its substantive definition is incorporated by reference and is not reproduced in the document.

### Reading improvement and monitoring plans — (C)

**G30 — (C), opening, p. 10 [requirement; consequence; funding/timing].** For every student required to receive intervention under this section, the district shall develop a reading improvement and monitoring plan within sixty days after receiving the student's (B)(1) diagnostic assessment results. It shall involve the student's parent or guardian and classroom teacher in developing the plan.

**G31 — (C)(1), p. 10 [requirement; criteria/list].** The plan shall identify the student's specific reading deficiencies.

**G32 — (C)(2), p. 10 [requirement; criteria/list; finding/purpose].** It shall describe additional instructional services and support to be provided to remediate those deficiencies.

**G33 — (C)(3), p. 10 [requirement; criteria/list].** It shall include opportunities for the parent or guardian to participate in the (C)(2) instructional services and support.

**G34 — (C)(4), p. 10 [requirement; criteria/list; consequence].** It shall include a process to monitor the extent to which the student receives the (C)(2) services and support.

**G35 — (C)(5)(a)–(c), pp. 10–11 [requirement; criteria/list; finding/purpose].** It shall include a reading curriculum during regular school hours doing all three things: (a) assisting students to read at grade level; (b) providing scientifically based and reliable assessment; (c) providing initial and ongoing analysis of each student's reading progress. All three are required, not alternatives.

**G36 — (C)(6), p. 11 [requirement; criteria/list; consequence; named source].** It shall contain a statement that a student who does not attain at least the equivalent achievement level designated in section 3301.0710(A)(3) on that section's end-of-third-grade English language arts assessment may be retained in third grade. Preserve “may” in this required statement; it is not worded like the “shall” notice in (B)(2)(a)(iv).

**G37 — (C)(7), p. 11 [requirement; criteria/list; authority; named source].** It shall provide high-dosage tutoring aligned with classroom instruction, either through a state-approved vendor on the section 3301.136 list of high-quality tutoring vendors or through a locally approved program aligning with department-identified high-dosage tutoring best practices. These are alternative approved routes. Items (C)(1)–(7) are all required plan inclusions.

**G38 — (C)(7), second and third sentences, p. 11 [requirement; permission; criteria/list; funding/timing].** High-dosage tutoring shall include at least three days of instruction per week, or at least fifty hours over thirty-six weeks. These are alternative minimum schedules. Tutoring may be incorporated into regular classroom instruction.

**G39 — (C), paragraph after (7), p. 11 [requirement; consequence; funding/timing].** The district shall continue providing the plan until the student achieves the required reading skill level for the student's current grade.

**G40 — (C), next paragraph, p. 11 [requirement; consequence; criteria/list; funding/timing].** Every student with a plan under this division who enters third grade after July 1, 2013, shall be assigned a teacher satisfying one or more (H) criteria. The printed condition is “after,” whereas (H) uses “on or after” and “for the first time.”

**G41 — (C), final paragraph, p. 11 [requirement; authority; consequence].** The district shall report any information the department requests about the plans developed under this division, in the department-required manner.

### Reporting, services standards, and legal limitation — (D)–(G)

**G42 — (D), first sentence, p. 11 [requirement; authority; consequence; funding/timing].** Each district shall report annually to the department on implementation of and compliance with this section, using department-prescribed guidelines.

**G43 — (D), second sentence, pp. 11–12 [requirement; authority; consequence; criteria/list; exception; funding/timing; named source].** The director of education and workforce shall report annually to the governor and general assembly: (1) numbers and percentages of kindergarten-through-fourth-grade students reading below grade level, based on (B) diagnostic assessments and English language arts achievement assessments under section 3301.0710(A)(1)(a) and (b), aggregated by district and building; (2) types of intervention services provided; and (3) if available, an evaluation of those services' efficacy. These are required reporting components, with availability expressly conditioning the third.

**G44 — (E)(1)–(3), p. 12 [requirement; criteria/list; funding/timing; finding/purpose].** Any summer remediation offered by districts under this section and funded wholly or partly by the state shall meet all three enumerated conditions: (1) methods based on reliable educational research; (2) district assessments before and after participation to facilitate monitoring remediation results; and (3) parents involved in programming decisions. This provision conditions state-funded summer services; it makes no appropriation and specifies no dollar amount.

**G45 — (F), p. 12 [requirement; criteria/list].** Any intervention or remediation required by the section shall include intensive, explicit, and systematic instruction. All three characteristics are required inclusions.

**G46 — (G), p. 12 [exception; consequence].** The section creates neither a new cause of action nor a substantive legal right for any person.

### Teacher qualifications and alternatives — (H)–(I)

**G47 — (H)(1), opening, p. 12 [requirement; criteria/list; exception; funding/timing].** Except under (H)(2), (3), and (4), every student described in (B)(3) or (C) who enters third grade for the first time on or after July 1, 2013, shall be assigned a teacher with at least one year of teaching experience and one or more of the six enumerated (a)–(f) qualifications below. The experience requirement is cumulative with at least one qualification; the six qualifications are alternatives.

**G48 — (H)(1)(a), p. 12 [criteria/list].** The teacher holds a reading endorsement and has passed the corresponding endorsement assessment, as applicable.

**G49 — (H)(1)(b), p. 12 [criteria/list].** The teacher completed a master's degree program with a major in reading.

**G50 — (H)(1)(c), pp. 12–13 [criteria/list; authority; funding/timing; named source].** The teacher was rated “most effective” in reading instruction for the most recent two consecutive years, based on assessments of student growth measures developed by a vendor and on the department-approved student assessment list under section 3319.112(B)(2). No particular vendor is named.

**G51 — (H)(1)(d), p. 13 [criteria/list; authority; funding/timing].** The teacher was rated “above expected value added,” in reading instruction, under department-established criteria, for the most recent two consecutive years.

**G52 — (H)(1)(e), p. 13 [criteria/list; authority].** The teacher passed a rigorous department-approved test of principles of scientifically research-based reading instruction.

**G53 — (H)(1)(f), p. 13 [criteria/list; funding/timing].** The teacher holds an educator license for teaching grades pre-kindergarten through three or four through nine, issued on or after July 1, 2017. The issuance date applies to the license.

**G54 — (H)(2), p. 13 [permission; exception; criteria/list; funding/timing].** Notwithstanding (H)(1), a (B)(3) or (C) student first entering third grade on or after July 1, 2013, may have a teacher with less than one year of experience if that teacher meets one or more (H)(1)(a)–(f) criteria and is assigned a teacher mentor meeting (H)(1) qualifications. Both conditions apply.

**G55 — (H)(3), p. 13 [permission; exception; requirement; authority; criteria/list; funding/timing; named source].** Notwithstanding (H)(1), a (B)(3) or (C) student first entering third grade on or after July 1, 2013, but before July 1, 2016, may have a teacher holding a department-approved alternative credential or successfully completing department-approved training based on scientifically research-based reading instruction principles. Those credentials and training shall align with department-adopted reading competencies under section 3301.077.

**G56 — (H)(4), pp. 13–14 [permission; exception; criteria/list; funding/timing; named source].** Notwithstanding (H)(1), a (B)(3) or (C) student first entering third grade on or after July 1, 2013, may receive reading intervention or remediation from a person employed as a speech-language pathologist who holds both a license issued by the state speech and hearing professionals board under Chapter 4753. and a registration under section 3319.221.

**G57 — (H)(5), first paragraph, p. 14 [permission; requirement; exception; criteria/list; consequence].** A teacher other than the student's teacher of record may provide any required services if the other teacher meets (H)'s requirements and both the teacher of record and school principal agree. Any such assignment shall be documented in the student's reading improvement and monitoring plan.

**G58 — (H)(5), second paragraph, p. 14 [definition].** In this division, “teacher of record” means the classroom teacher to whom a student is assigned.

**G59 — (I), p. 14 [permission; requirement; exception; authority; criteria/list; funding/timing; named source].** Notwithstanding (H), a teacher may teach reading to an English language learner who has been in the United States three years or less, or to a student with an individualized education program under Chapter 3323., if the teacher holds a department-approved alternative credential or successfully completed department-approved training based on scientifically research-based reading instruction principles. The credentials and training shall align with department-adopted reading competencies under section 3301.077. The English-learner duration differs from (A)(2)(a)'s two “less than three” conditions.

### Staffing plans and implementation assistance — (J)–(K)

**G60 — (J), first paragraph, pp. 14–15 [requirement; consequence; criteria/list; funding/timing].** If, on or after June 4, 2013, a district or community school cannot furnish enough teachers meeting one or more (H) criteria for 2013-2014, it shall develop and submit a staffing plan by June 30, 2013. The plan shall include (1) criteria for assigning (B)(3) or (C) students to teachers, (2) credentials or training of teachers currently teaching at the school, and (3) how the district or school will meet this section's requirements. These are mandatory inclusions. The district or community school shall post the plan on its website for the applicable school year.

**G61 — (J), second paragraph, p. 15 [requirement; consequence; funding/timing].** A district or community school that submitted a (J) plan shall submit to the department a detailed progress report on meeting this section's requirements by March 1, 2014, and on March 1 of every year thereafter.

**G62 — (J), third paragraph, p. 15 [permission; requirement; authority; exception; funding/timing].** A district or community school may request a staffing-plan extension beyond 2013-2014. Requests must reach the department no later than April 30 before the applicable school year begins. The department may grant extensions valid through 2015-2016. The text does not make an extension automatic.

**G63 — (K), p. 15 [requirement; authority; finding/purpose; criteria/list].** The department shall designate one or more staff members to guide and assist districts and community schools in implementing the third-grade guarantee, including any implementation standards or requirements, and to provide information and support for reading instruction and achievement. “Including” does not close the scope of implementation assistance.

## 3. Section 3319.2310 — Science of reading training for licensed educators

**T01 — Header, p. 17 [funding/timing; named source].** Presented under Ohio Revised Code, Title 33 Education-Libraries, Chapter 3319 Schools - Superintendent; Teachers; Employees. Printed effective date: October 9, 2026. Latest legislation: House Bill 455 - 136th General Assembly.

**T02 — (A), p. 17 [definition; named source].** “Other public school,” as used in this section, has the same meaning as in section 3301.0711. Its substantive definition is not reproduced.

**T03 — (B)(1), p. 17 [requirement; authority; finding/purpose].** The department of education and workforce shall maintain a licensed-educator training course introducing the science of reading.

**T04 — (B)(2), p. 17 [requirement; authority; finding/purpose; criteria/list].** The department shall develop a competency-based licensed-educator course that updates and reinforces knowledge and skills in the science of reading.

**T05 — (B)(3), p. 17 [requirement; authority; permission; criteria/list].** The department shall develop a list of approved courses more rigorous than (B)(2)'s course. An individual may choose one of those courses in place of (B)(2). All three (B)(1)–(3) departmental tasks are required; no particular course provider or title is printed.

**T06 — (C), opening, p. 17 [requirement; criteria/list].** Each person employed by a school district or other public school as a teacher, administrator, school psychologist, or speech-language pathologist shall complete science-of-reading training in accordance with (C). These four job categories specify the covered employees.

**T07 — (C)(1), p. 17 [requirement; criteria/list; funding/timing].** A teacher or administrator hired before July 1, 2025, shall complete (B)(2)'s course or a course on (B)(3)'s list by June 30, 2030, and every five years thereafter.

**T08 — (C)(2), first sentence, p. 18 [requirement; criteria/list; funding/timing].** A teacher or administrator hired on or after July 1, 2025, shall complete (B)(1)'s introductory course within one year of hire, then (B)(2) or a (B)(3)-listed course every five years thereafter, subject to the introductory-course exception below.

**T09 — (C)(2)(a)–(b), p. 18 [exception; prohibition; authority; criteria/list; funding/timing].** The individual shall not be required to take (B)(1) if the district superintendent or other public school's head administrator verifies that, within the five years before hire, the individual either (a) completed that training or similar training, with similarity determined by the department, or (b) completed appropriate science-of-reading coursework in an educator or licensure preparation program. These are two alternative qualifying routes; verification and the five-year lookback condition apply. The exception is to (B)(1), not the recurring (B)(2)/(B)(3) obligation.

**T10 — (C)(3), p. 18 [requirement; criteria/list; funding/timing].** A school psychologist or speech-language pathologist employed by the district or other public school shall complete (B)(1)'s course by June 30, 2027, and (B)(2) or a (B)(3)-listed course every five years thereafter.

**T11 — (D), first sentence, p. 18 [requirement; authority; consequence; named source].** A professional development committee established under section 3319.22 shall count (B) training toward educator licensure renewal professional development requirements.

**T12 — (D), second sentence, p. 18 [requirement; permission; consequence; funding/timing].** The committee shall allow an individual to apply any hours above the minimum professional development coursework hours required for licensure renewal to the next renewal period for that license.
