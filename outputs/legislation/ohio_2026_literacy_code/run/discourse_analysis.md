# Discourse analysis

## LEX — Lexical choices and definitional framing

### A research body made into a statutory definition

- **Where:** Section 3313.6028(A)(1), opening, and (A)(1)(a)-(b) (PDF p. 1).
- **Passage:** "interdisciplinary body of scientific evidence that:"; "Informs how students learn to read and write proficiently;"; "Explains why some students have difficulty with reading and writing;"
- **Code:** LEX-3: Simple View / Research Body (research-body branch).
- **Reading:** The definition names a body of evidence rather than a commercial program or a single discipline. Its verbs give that evidence both instructional and explanatory authority: it informs proficiency and explains difficulty. Reading and writing are paired from the start. The passage does not name the Simple View, so that branch of the code is not supported.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the research-body framing; it does not establish which studies qualify as evidence.

### A component list broader than the five pillars alone

- **Where:** Section 3313.6028(A)(1)(c) (PDF p. 1).
- **Passage:** "Indicates that all students benefit from explicit and systematic instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension, and writing to become effective readers;"
- **Code:** LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor (qualified); LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition (partial).
- **Reading:** The five listed reading components anchor the definition, with writing added as a sixth component. Explicit and systematic instruction describes how the components should be taught. This partially fits the structured-literacy descriptors, but neither that framework's name nor IDA appears, and the passage does not supply the full sequence of descriptors in LEX-2. The list establishes a common instructional foundation without placing phonics above comprehension or writing.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the components; uncertain whether the two descriptors alone warrant LEX-2 beyond a partial fit. They can also be read as general instructional requirements without adopting a named framework.

### Exclusion defines the boundary, with student-specific exceptions

- **Where:** Section 3313.6028(A)(1)(d), (C), and (D)(2) (PDF pp. 1-2).
- **Passage:** "Does not rely on any model of teaching students to read based on meaning, structure and syntax, and visual cues, including a three-cueing approach."; "Except as provided in division (D) of this section, no district or school shall use any core curriculum, instructional materials, or intervention program in grades pre-kindergarten to five that use the three-cueing approach to teach students to read."; "If a student has an individualized education program that explicitly indicates the three-cueing approach is appropriate for the student's learning needs, the student shall not be required to have a waiver."
- **Code:** LEX-5: Method Prohibition Language (qualified).
- **Reading:** Exclusion occurs inside the definition of science as well as in an operative prohibition. Three-cueing is thereby placed outside the preferred evidence category before school conduct is regulated. Yet (D) permits individual waiver applications, excludes reading-plan recipients from waiver eligibility in (D)(1), and separately dispenses with a waiver for the specified IEP provision. The text therefore does not support describing the prohibition as absolute or as applying expressly to every grade.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain. The definition excludes the named model, while the operative provisions retain the printed exceptions.

### Research authority without an evidentiary test

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(E)(1) (PDF p. 12).
- **Passage:** "The remediation methods are based on reliable educational research."
- **Code:** LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language.
- **Reading:** Reliability functions as a condition for state-funded summer remediation, but this provision supplies no study, threshold, or criteria for judging it. The phrase authorizes an evidentiary standard while leaving its application outside the sentence. This is narrower than claiming that science of reading is undefined throughout the document: section 3313.6028 does define that term.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the absence of criteria here; the supplied text cannot establish what criteria may exist in external materials.

## PRE — Presuppositions

These sections contain no legislative findings or purpose preamble. The observations below concern assumptions embedded in definitions and duties, rather than an unprinted legislative account of crisis or failure.

### Universal benefit, without a guarantee of universal attainment

- **Where:** Section 3313.6028(A)(1)(c) (PDF p. 1).
- **Passage:** "Indicates that all students benefit from explicit and systematic instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension, and writing to become effective readers;"
- **Code:** PRE-1: Universal Learnability (partial); PRE-4: Research Consensus (partial).
- **Reading:** Universal benefit is built into the legal meaning of the evidence, rather than presented as a proposition requiring support within this document. This makes the listed instruction the shared baseline for students. The statement does not say that every student will attain proficiency, nor that every research question is settled; it states a categorical benefit claim within a definition.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain as a full instance of either code. Universal benefit is explicit, but learnability and research unanimity are stronger claims than the passage makes.

### Training presumes a continuing knowledge need, while recognizing existing expertise

- **Where:** Section 3319.2310(B)(2) and (C)(2)(b), read with the exemption conditions in (C)(2) (PDF pp. 17-18).
- **Passage:** "Develop a competency-based training course for licensed educators that updates and reinforces educators' knowledge and skills in the science of reading;"; "Completed appropriate coursework in the science of reading as part of the individual's educator or licensure preparation program."
- **Code:** PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap (qualified).
- **Reading:** Recurring training makes continuing maintenance of science-of-reading knowledge a normal workforce requirement. But updating and reinforcing presumes knowledge already exists, rather than depicting every educator as ignorant. Verified recent preparation can remove the introductory-course requirement for newer teachers and administrators; recurring training remains. This is a differentiated model of knowledge maintenance, not an explicit diagnosis of universal teacher failure.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about a deficit interpretation: the requirement can be read as responding to a gap, but the wording also supports ordinary continuing professional education.

### State judgment is built into curricular governance

- **Where:** Section 3313.6028(B), second paragraph, and (D), concluding paragraph (PDF pp. 1-2).
- **Passage:** "The department shall establish a rubric and scoring system to evaluate whether or the extent to which core curricula in English language arts facilitates a student's skill building and sequential advancement through mastery."; "In determining whether to approve a waiver requested under this section, the department shall consider the performance of the student's district or school on the state report card issued under section 3302.03 of the Revised Code, including on the early literacy component prescribed under that section."
- **Code:** PRE-5: Legislative Competence.
- **Reading:** Departmental competence to judge curriculum and method exceptions is assumed by assigning it scoring and approval duties. Even an individual student's waiver is connected to institution-level performance, making school accountability evidence relevant to permission over method. The text does not argue why the department is the appropriate arbiter or give a numerical report-card threshold here.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the authorized arbiter; uncertain about the implied relationship between school performance and individual instructional appropriateness. The report card could be intended as one administrative safeguard rather than a direct measure of that student's needs.

## FOS — Foregrounding and silencing

### Assessment organizes identification and action

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(B)(1) and (B)(2)(b) (PDF pp. 7-8).
- **Passage:** "The reading skills assessment shall be completed by the thirtieth day of September."; "Provide intensive reading instruction services and regular diagnostic assessments to the student immediately following identification of a reading deficiency until the development of the reading improvement and monitoring plan required by division (C) of this section."
- **Code:** FOS-3: Assessment / Screening Foregrounded.
- **Reading:** Annual kindergarten-to-third-grade assessment in (B)(1) provides the entry point into identification, notice, immediate services, and a subsequent plan. The date and the immediate-service instruction make assessment an organizing mechanism, rather than simply an end-stage check. Repeated diagnosis remains part of the intervention itself.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain. This is a reading-skills assessment system; the text does not name it a universal dyslexia screen.

### Comprehension and writing are present; background knowledge and oral language are unnamed

- **Where:** Section 3313.6028(A)(1)(c) (PDF p. 1).
- **Passage:** "Indicates that all students benefit from explicit and systematic instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension, and writing to become effective readers;"
- **Code:** FOS-4: Background Knowledge / Comprehension Silenced (counter-example for comprehension; partial for background knowledge); FOS-7: Oral Language / Writing Silenced (counter-example for writing; partial for oral language).
- **Reading:** This definitional list explicitly includes comprehension and writing, so neither can be coded as absent. It does not separately name background knowledge, a knowledge-building curriculum, or oral language development. The omission gives those areas less explicit statutory visibility than the six named components, without proving that they are excluded from instruction. Nothing in the list ranks phonics or decoding as primary, so it does not by itself warrant FOS-1.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about what is named; uncertain about substantive silencing. Vocabulary and comprehension could accommodate knowledge building and oral language without separately naming them.

### Multilingual learners are recognized through exceptions

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(A)(2)(a) (PDF p. 6); related teacher route in (I) (PDF p. 14).
- **Passage:** "The student is an English learner who has been enrolled in United States schools for less than three full school years and has had less than three years of instruction in an English as a second language program."
- **Code:** FOS-5: English Learners Silenced (counter-example, qualified).
- **Reading:** The promotion-exception list recognizes language-learning history as relevant to retention; multilingual learners are therefore not wholly absent. Section (I) also recognizes certain English language learners in an alternative teacher-qualification route, with a differently worded three-year boundary. These are accommodations in promotion and staffing, rather than an articulated multilingual instructional framework.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about explicit recognition. Its adequacy for multilingual instruction cannot be determined from these exceptions alone.

### The intervention specification does not articulate multilingual pedagogy

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(B)(2)(b), final sentence (PDF p. 8).
- **Passage:** "These intervention services shall be aligned with the science of reading as defined under section 3313.6028 of the Revised Code and include research-based reading strategies that have been shown to be successful in improving reading among low-performing readers and instruction targeted at the student's identified reading deficiencies."
- **Code:** FOS-5: English Learners Silenced (partial).
- **Reading:** Where the text specifies intervention content, it addresses generic low performance and individualized deficiencies, without naming EL-specific research, home-language resources, or translanguaging. This is a local absence within an instructional specification, not a claim that English learners never appear in the document. Targeting identified needs could allow language-responsive provision, but no such provision is explicit here.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about substantive exclusion: the generic individualized requirement could encompass language-learning needs. The absence of an explicit multilingual requirement is certain.

### Remedies include an organizational lever but no structural causal account

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(B)(3)(a)(i)-(vii) (PDF p. 9).
- **Passage:** "Small group instruction;"; "Reduced teacher-student ratios;"; "More frequent progress monitoring;"; "Tutoring or mentoring;"; "Transition classes containing third and fourth grade students;"; "Extended school day, week, or year;"; "Summer reading camps."
- **Code:** FOS-6: Structural Causes Silenced (qualified).
- **Reading:** The list of possible remediation arrangements centres instruction, monitoring, grouping, and time. Poverty, school-funding inequality, and racial segregation are not named as causes or targets within this remedy menu. Reduced ratios nevertheless supplies an organizational resource lever, limiting any assertion that the only remedy is a different teaching method. These items are permitted options following a required minimum of ninety minutes of daily reading instruction, not seven universal mandates.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about a causal silencing interpretation because the list specifies services rather than explaining why outcomes occur. The narrower observation is that these structural causes remain unnamed in this remedy specification; their absence cannot establish a legislative denial of them.

## SUB — Subject positioning

### Teachers as regulated workers and developing professionals

- **Where:** Section 3319.2310(C), opening; (B)(2); and (D), first sentence (PDF pp. 17-18).
- **Passage:** "Each individual employed by a school district or other public school as a teacher, administrator, school psychologist, or speech-language pathologist shall complete training in the science of reading in accordance with division (C) of this section."; "Develop a competency-based training course for licensed educators that updates and reinforces educators' knowledge and skills in the science of reading;"; "A professional development committee established under section 3319.22 of the Revised Code shall count training described in division (B) of this section toward professional development requirements for educator licensure renewal."
- **Code:** SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated (qualified); SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional (qualified).
- **Reading:** The completion duty makes participation a condition of the covered professional role, extending beyond teachers to administrators and specialists. At the same time, the text recognizes existing knowledge, provides developmental courses, and integrates them into renewal credit, with excess-hour carryover in (D). Teachers appear as professionals whose learning receives institutional recognition, but within a state-controlled training structure. Deficiency is not expressly asserted.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about regulation and developmental support; uncertain that mandated learning alone constructs educators as deficient.

### Students as deficient readers, service beneficiaries, and capable in other fields

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(B)(2)(a)(i), (B)(3)(a), and (B)(4) (PDF pp. 8-10).
- **Passage:** "Notification that the student has been identified as having a substantial deficiency in reading;"; "Provide intense remediation services until the student is able to read at grade level."; "For each student retained under division (A) of this section who has demonstrated proficiency in a specific academic ability field, each district shall provide instruction commensurate with student achievement levels in that specific academic ability field."
- **Code:** SUB-3: Student as Victim / Beneficiary (qualified).
- **Reading:** Students become administratively identifiable through deficiency and grade-level comparison, and retained students become recipients of sustained remediation. The text does not expressly portray them as victims of prior instruction. Its separate recognition of proficiency in other fields prevents reading status from defining the student's entire academic capacity; this complicates a wholly deficit-based subject position.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about beneficiary and accountability positioning. Harm by earlier instruction is not established by these passages.

### Parents can alter the promotion route, within stated limits

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(A)(2)(f), its following paragraph, and (G) (PDF pp. 7, 12).
- **Passage:** "A student's parent or guardian, in consultation with the student's reading teacher and building principal, requests that the student, regardless of if the student is reading at grade level, be promoted to the fourth grade."; "A student who is promoted under division (A)(2)(f) of this section shall continue to receive intensive reading instruction in the same manner as a student retained under this section until the student is able to read at grade level."; "This section does not create a new cause of action or a substantive legal right for any person."
- **Code:** SUB-4: Parent as Rights-Holder (qualified).
- **Reading:** The parent or guardian is an actor whose request, made in consultation, is a listed exception to the promotion restriction. That agency changes grade progression while preserving intensive instruction. Section (G) limits the rights-creating force of the section, so this should be described as a statutory participation and decision route without inferring a new substantive legal right or cause of action.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the printed role and limiting clause. The passage supplies no basis for deciding broader enforceability questions.

### Districts implement and report; the department judges and assists

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(D), opening sentence; (K), excerpt (PDF pp. 11, 15).
- **Passage:** "Each school district shall report annually to the department on its implementation and compliance with this section using guidelines prescribed by the department."; "to provide guidance and assistance to school districts and community schools in implementing the third grade guarantee established by this section"
- **Code:** SUB-5: District as Compliance Target; SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter (qualified).
- **Reading:** Compliance reporting places districts under departmental supervision and gives the department control over how implementation is represented. The assistance provision also makes the state a provider of implementation support, not solely an inspector. The arbiter role is explicit across the document; a dramatic rescuer narrative correcting documented local failure is not.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about compliance and support; uncertain about the stronger rescuer interpretation.

### Curriculum is subject to approval rather than presumed suitable

- **Where:** Section 3313.6028(B), first sentence, and (C), first sentence (PDF pp. 1-2).
- **Passage:** "The department of education and workforce shall establish a list of high-quality core curriculum and instructional materials in English language arts, and a list of evidence-based reading intervention programs, that are aligned with the science of reading and strategies for effective literacy instruction."; "only from the lists established under division (B) of this section."
- **Code:** SUB-6: Publisher / Curriculum as Suspect (qualified); SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter.
- **Reading:** Suitability is something that materials acquire through the state selection system; local availability alone does not authorize use. The restriction covers core materials in pre-kindergarten through grade five and intervention programs through grade twelve. This fits the approval-list branch of SUB-6, but the document does not accuse a named publisher of failure or establish commercial misconduct.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about gatekeeping; uncertain whether suspect is the best characterization. Quality assurance is also a reasonable reading of the selection system.

## INT — Intertextuality

The most developed intertextual relations are statutory cross-references and administrative incorporation. The supplied sections name no National Reading Panel report, IDA standard, peer-reviewed study, journalist, advocacy report, or other state as a warrant; the component list alone does not establish INT-1, and generic research language does not establish INT-4.

### One statute supplies another's scientific standard

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(B)(2)(b), final sentence (PDF p. 8).
- **Passage:** "These intervention services shall be aligned with the science of reading as defined under section 3313.6028 of the Revised Code"
- **Code:** no code fits (intrastate statutory incorporation).
- **Reading:** The guarantee borrows the curriculum statute's definition rather than restating or independently evidencing it. This carries the definition's positive components and exclusions into immediate intervention services and connects otherwise separate statutory mechanisms. It is not interstate borrowing or a citation to a research study.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the explicit incorporation; no inference about unprinted external provisions is needed.

### Disability law supplies a recognized documentary authority

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(A)(2)(d)(iii), read within the cumulative conditions in (d)(i)-(iv) (PDF p. 6).
- **Passage:** "The student's individualized education program or plan under section 504 of the \"Rehabilitation Act of 1973,\" 87 Stat. 355, 29 U.S.C. 794, as amended, shows that the student has received intensive remediation in reading for two school years but still demonstrates a deficiency in reading."
- **Code:** no code fits (federal disability-law cross-reference).
- **Reading:** The federal citation gives the student's plan a legally recognizable status in documenting a promotion exception. Disability law enters as an authority governing student classification and documentation, rather than as evidence for a preferred reading method. This is one condition among several; the cited plan alone does not create this exception. INT-6 would overstate the fit because this is not a historical reading policy invoked as instructional precedent.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the citation and documentary function. The external act's full contents are not reproduced or assessed here.

### Administrative best practices can authorize a local alternative

- **Where:** Section 3313.608(C)(7), first sentence (PDF p. 11).
- **Passage:** "The provision of high-dosage tutoring aligned with the student's classroom instruction through a state-approved vendor on the list of high-quality tutoring vendors under section 3301.136 of the Revised Code or a locally approved program that aligns with high-dosage tutoring best practices identified by the department."
- **Code:** no code fits (administrative standard invoked by reference).
- **Reading:** The text invokes both a statutory vendor list and department-identified best practices. Local approval remains possible, but the local program's legitimacy is tied to a state-defined external standard. Neither the best-practice document nor its research basis is identified here, so this is an invocation of administrative authority rather than a verifiable research-literature citation.
- **Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the two authorization routes; uncertain about the content and source of the best practices, which this document does not reproduce.
