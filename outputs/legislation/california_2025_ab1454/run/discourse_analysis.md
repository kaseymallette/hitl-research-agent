# Discourse analysis: Assembly Bill No. 1454, Chapter 471

## LEX — Lexical choices and definitional framing

### LEX observation 1: Specified instructional content without a science-of-reading label

**Where:** SECTION 1, Education Code §44265.1(a)(2), p. 2; repeated in SEC. 2, §44270.6(a)(2), p. 3, and, with a materials-specific opening and without “to all pupils,” in SEC. 3, §60205(b)(2), p. 3.

**Passage:** “Evidence-based means of teaching foundational reading skills, which shall include explicit and systematic instruction in print concepts, phonological awareness, phonics and word recognition, and fluency to all pupils, and attending to oral and written language development, vocabulary and background knowledge, and comprehension, including tiered supports for pupils with reading difficulties, English learners, and pupils with exceptional needs.”

**Code:** LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor (partial).

**Reading:** The act never uses the label science of reading. Instead, it gives instructional content to evidence-based literacy through a repeated list spanning credential preparation and materials. The list resembles the five-component anchor but says phonological awareness rather than phonemic awareness, adds print concepts, language development, background knowledge, and tiered supports, and does not name the National Reading Panel. “Shall include” establishes required content without making the list exhaustive. This is neither an explicit five-pillars definition of reading nor an unelaborated appeal to evidence.

**Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain as a five-pillars coding: the shared components support the partial code, but the list can also be read as its own broader instructional specification. The lexical differences and absence of an NRP citation are certain; they preclude treating this as adoption of that report.

### LEX observation 2: Evidence as an authorizing adjective, with limits to its specification

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(b)(2), p. 3.

**Passage:** “Align to evidence-based means of teaching foundational reading skills, which shall include explicit and systematic instruction in print concepts, phonological awareness, phonics and word recognition, and fluency, and attending to oral and written language development, vocabulary and background knowledge, and comprehension, including tiered supports for pupils with reading difficulties, English learners, and pupils with exceptional needs.”

**Code:** No code fits.

**Reading:** “Evidence-based” gives the enumerated approach epistemic authority, while “align” makes it a standard against which materials can be judged. The provision specifies instructional features but supplies no study, evidence threshold, or procedure for deciding which evidence qualifies. LEX-4 does not fully describe this combination because that code requires the absence of a definition, named source, or qualifying criteria: here there are substantive criteria and adjacent framework and guideline references. Nor do “explicit and systematic” alone establish adoption of a named structured-literacy framework. The act bounds acceptable instruction through positive alignment requirements; it names no prohibited method or program.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the wording and the absence of a named study or method prohibition. Uncertain about how fully the instructional list resolves evidence judgments: it may function as an adequate practical specification, or leave substantial interpretive work to the adopting authorities.

## PRE — Presuppositions

There is no separate legislative findings section asserting a literacy crisis, universal proficiency, or a settled research consensus. The strongest presuppositions concern the institutions entitled to set standards and the preparation through which instruction is to be improved.

### PRE observation 1: State selection of instructional criteria treated as an ordinary duty

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(b), opening sentence, p. 3.

**Passage:** “The state board shall ensure that instructional materials adopted pursuant to this section adhere to all of the following:”

**Code:** PRE-5: Legislative Competence (qualified).

**Reading:** The sentence takes the appropriateness of state oversight for granted: the question becomes whether materials meet the specified criteria, rather than whether a state body should set instructional criteria. “All” makes the following framework, foundational-skills, dyslexia-guideline, and subset-material provisions cumulative obligations. The authority is qualified by the separate route for locally selected materials in §60205(d)(1), which requires local certification rather than exclusive use of the state list. State arbitration of criteria thus coexists with local selection.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain as an institutional presupposition enacted through the duty. This does not establish that the legislature claims exclusive pedagogical expertise or abolishes professional judgment.

### PRE observation 2: Preparation systems as the site of a presumed need

**Where:** SEC. 2, Education Code §44270.6(a), opening sentence, pp. 2–3; quoted portion on p. 2.

**Passage:** “include preparation on how to support teachers in delivering instruction through effective means for teaching literacy, aligned to all of the following:”

**Code:** PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap (reframed).

**Reading:** The requirement makes preparation for supporting teachers an object of intervention, locating responsibility in administrative preparation standards. Effective instruction appears as something that preparation and support can enable, rather than as an independently demonstrated outcome. The act does not explicitly say that current teachers lack knowledge, that existing methods caused low achievement, or that teachers need retraining. The reframed code marks a presumed need for institutional preparation, not a finding of individual deficiency.

**Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain. Adding a preparation requirement may imply that existing preparation is insufficient, but it could also formalize or harmonize practices already present. The passage alone cannot establish a teacher knowledge deficit or the stronger instructional-causation claim in PRE-2.

## FOS — Foregrounding and silencing

### FOS observation 1: Foundational skills receive the more directive instructional description

**Where:** SECTION 1, Education Code §44265.1(a)(2), p. 2; parallel lists in SEC. 2, §44270.6(a)(2), and SEC. 3, §60205(b)(2), p. 3.

**Passage:** “explicit and systematic instruction in print concepts, phonological awareness, phonics and word recognition, and fluency to all pupils, and attending to oral and written language development, vocabulary and background knowledge, and comprehension”

**Code:** FOS-1: Phonics / Decoding Foregrounded (qualified).

**Reading:** The list gives the first cluster both instructional descriptors and an explicit universal audience, then joins the broader language and meaning cluster through “attending to.” That asymmetry foregrounds the manner of teaching foundational skills more specifically than the manner of teaching comprehension or building knowledge. Phonics is nevertheless one item in a cluster, not the sole foundation named; language and comprehension remain within the same mandatory criterion. The wording supports a limited claim about relative emphasis, not a claim that decoding exhausts literacy.

**Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about the strength of the hierarchy. The verb contrast permits this reading, but “which shall include” can also be read as placing both coordinated clusters under an equally binding requirement.

### FOS observation 2: Knowledge, comprehension, oral language, and written language are present

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(b)(2), p. 3; also SECTION 1, §44265.1(a)(2), p. 2, and SEC. 2, §44270.6(a)(2), p. 3.

**Passage:** “and attending to oral and written language development, vocabulary and background knowledge, and comprehension, including tiered supports for pupils with reading difficulties, English learners, and pupils with exceptional needs.”

**Code:** FOS-4: Background Knowledge / Comprehension Silenced (counter-example); FOS-7: Oral Language / Writing Silenced (counter-example, partial).

**Reading:** At exactly the place where the act lists instructional content, it explicitly retains background knowledge, comprehension, and oral and written language development. These topics therefore cannot be reported as absent. The list also connects the criterion to differentiated supports rather than presenting a uniform instructional recipient. The counter-example is partial for writing: written language development is named, but the text does not separately elaborate writing instruction or a knowledge-building curriculum. Its limited detail should not be converted into wholesale silence.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about explicit inclusion. Uncertain about the practical breadth of written language development: it may encompass writing instruction, but the act does not spell out that relationship.

### FOS observation 3: English learner needs are an express materials criterion

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(b)(1), p. 3.

**Passage:** “Align to the current English Language Arts/English Language Development (ELA/ELD) Framework, including integrated and designated English language development instruction adopted by the state board, and including strategies to meet the varied language development needs of English learners.”

**Code:** FOS-5: English Learners Silenced (counter-example).

**Reading:** This criterion names English learners directly, differentiates language-development needs, and includes both integrated and designated instruction. Their needs are part of the required alignment, not an exception outside the literacy standard. Primary-language preparation also appears in §44265.1(b) and §44270.6(c), and primary-language materials in §60205(e), although those provisions use legislative-intent language. The text does not name translanguaging or EL-specific studies; those narrower absences do not justify coding English learners as silenced when their instruction is repeatedly addressed.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain. The distinction between mandatory alignment here and intent elsewhere limits the strength of the inclusion without removing it.

### FOS observation 4: The instructional remedy omits structural conditions, but funding is not wholly absent

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(b)(2), p. 3; SEC. 4, p. 4.

**Passage:** “Align to evidence-based means of teaching foundational reading skills” and “including tiered supports for pupils with reading difficulties, English learners, and pupils with exceptional needs.” SEC. 4 states: “If the Commission on State Mandates determines that this act contains costs mandated by the state, reimbursement to local agencies and school districts for those costs shall be made pursuant to Part 7 (commencing with Section 17500) of Division 4 of Title 2 of the Government Code.”

**Code:** FOS-6: Structural Causes Silenced (qualified).

**Reading:** The instructional-content and support list in §60205(b)(2), quoted in full in LEX observation 2, specifies pedagogical means and pupil categories without naming poverty, class size, racial segregation, or school-funding inequality as conditions requiring a response. Within that remedy, instruction and materials occupy the foreground. But the act supplies no causal findings about reading outcomes, and SEC. 4 explicitly addresses the fiscal consequences of implementation through conditional reimbursement. The bounded absence is structural conditions within the literacy remedy, not all mention of money or proof that the act attributes failure solely to teaching.

**Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain as a silencing interpretation. The omission may reflect the narrow remit of a credential-and-materials act rather than an implied denial of structural causes. Conditional mandate reimbursement is a counterweight to a broad claim of fiscal silence; it is not itself a structural explanation of reading outcomes.

## SUB — Subject positioning

### SUB observation 1: Teachers as professionals supported through other educators

**Where:** SECTION 1, Education Code §44265.1(a), opening sentence, p. 2; SEC. 2, §44270.6(b), p. 3.

**Passage:** “include preparation on how to deliver instruction and support teachers to deliver instruction through effective means for teaching literacy” and “The commission shall confirm that administrative preparation programs implement the standards pursuant to this section.”

**Code:** SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional (qualified).

**Reading:** Teachers are recipients of specialist and administrative support, while candidates are positioned as future instructional practitioners and supporters. Regulation operates through the commission's standards and confirmation of preparation-program implementation. The act does not impose a new training duty or sanction directly on every classroom teacher. Its supporting language consequently qualifies a simple teacher-as-deficient reading, even though support is to serve state-specified instructional criteria.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the support relationship and the regulated institutional actors. Uncertain about how much professional discretion that support entails: the text does not describe collaboration, coaching practices, or teacher participation in setting the criteria.

### SUB observation 2: Local agencies as accountable selectors

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(d)(1)–(2), p. 3.

**Passage:** “A local educational agency that adopts instructional materials that have not been adopted by the state board pursuant to subdivision (a) shall certify that the instructional materials align to the criteria in subdivision (b).” Also: “A local educational agency may adopt a set of instructional materials that, in combination, address all of the state standards and components of the English Language Arts/English Language Development (ELA/ELD) Framework.”

**Code:** SUB-5: District as Compliance Target (qualified).

**Reading:** “Shall certify” makes local agencies answerable for alignment, while “may adopt” recognizes their capacity to assemble a suitable set. Their role is constrained selection rather than merely purchasing from a single state list. The certification duty applies to the non-state-adopted route; the text does not make every local choice dependent on a separate state approval or attach a stated sanction. Materials are assessed against criteria, but no publisher or named program is accused of causing failure, so SUB-6 would overstate the passage.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain. The simultaneous duty and permission are explicit; the act does not describe how certification will be reviewed.

### SUB observation 3: State authority works through guidance as well as adoption

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(c), p. 3.

**Passage:** “Upon the adoption of instructional materials pursuant to subdivision (a), the state board shall update the state Guidance for Local Instructional Materials Adoptions to reflect the criteria in subdivision (b). A local educational agency shall follow this guidance and the processes described in Section 60002 for purposes of adopting instructional materials.”

**Code:** SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter (partial).

**Reading:** The board controls both the state adoption and the guidance through which criteria enter local decisions. The second sentence gives guidance obligatory force for local agencies, extending state arbitration beyond the state list itself. This is an arbiter position, but not a developed rescuer narrative: the act does not describe a failed locality, identify a villain, or promise recovery from a crisis.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the allocation of authority. The partial qualifier excludes a rescue story unsupported by the text.

### SUB observation 4: Pupils' languages as assets and teachers as builders upon them

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(e), p. 4.

**Passage:** “It is the intent of the Legislature that the state board’s adoption of instructional materials in English language arts and English language development include high-quality instructional materials in pupils’ primary languages to enable teachers to build upon pupils’ linguistic assets in programs for which English learner pupils are being instructed in their primary language.”

**Code:** No code fits.

**Reading:** Pupils possess linguistic resources on which teaching can build, rather than appearing only as recipients with difficulties or needs. Teachers are enabled to use those resources through materials provision. The codebook's victim/beneficiary position does not adequately capture this asset language, and its teacher-support code only partly captures the pupil position. The statement is legislative intent concerning primary-language programs; it does not create an individual entitlement to such a program or position parents as rights-holders.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the asset positioning and the intent form. The passage does not establish how that intent will be implemented.

## INT — Intertextuality

The act's principal explicit authorities are state frameworks, guidelines, guidance, and statutory procedures. It names no National Reading Panel report, professional or advocacy organization, researcher, study, media report, or other state's reform; the codebook's examples cannot supply those missing references.

### INT observation 1: State framework and dyslexia guidelines supply incorporated authority

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(b)(1) and (3), p. 3; corresponding references in SECTION 1, §44265.1(a)(1), (3), p. 2, and SEC. 2, §44270.6(a)(1), (3), p. 3.

**Passage:** “Align to the current English Language Arts/English Language Development (ELA/ELD) Framework” and “Align to the program guidelines for dyslexia developed pursuant to Section 56335.”

**Code:** No code fits.

**Reading:** The named framework and statutorily identified guidelines become alignment authorities without their full content being reproduced. Repeating these references across credentials and materials builds coherence through a shared set of state texts. “Current” situates the materials requirement in relation to the framework in force, rather than naming a research publication or date. Neither the dyslexia reference nor the instructional vocabulary establishes an IDA citation; INT-2 would require an organization or its standard to be invoked. Nor does the statute identify these texts as historical policies to be superseded, as INT-6 requires.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about incorporation by reference. The contents and research bases of the referenced texts cannot be inferred from this document alone.

### INT observation 2: Guidance and code references carry criteria into local procedure

**Where:** SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(c), p. 3.

**Passage:** “the state board shall update the state Guidance for Local Instructional Materials Adoptions to reflect the criteria in subdivision (b)” and “A local educational agency shall follow this guidance and the processes described in Section 60002 for purposes of adopting instructional materials.”

**Code:** No code fits.

**Reading:** The act directs the rewriting of another text and then makes that text, together with an existing code provision, part of local adoption procedure. Intertextuality here is an implementation mechanism: standards move from statute into guidance that agencies must follow. The reference also leaves procedural detail outside this act. It does not license attributing particular participation rights or review steps to §60002 without reading that separate law, and it supplies no evidence of borrowing from another jurisdiction.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the expressly required textual relationship; the contents of the referenced procedure remain outside the evidence supplied here.
