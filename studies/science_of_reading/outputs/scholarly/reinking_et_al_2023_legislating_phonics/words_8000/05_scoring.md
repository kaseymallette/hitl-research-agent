## Reading and scoring procedure

I read the PDF through Ghostscript’s `txtwrite` text extraction and checked rendered PDF-page images: printed pp. 113–115 for the figures and pp. 119 and 121 for quotation-layout ambiguities. Only the five authorized input files were opened; no cited work or outside source was consulted. The close-reading JSON mentioned by Method B was not opened.

Recall is substantive use, not a requirement to reproduce every word of a reference entry. An explicit assertion, close paraphrase, or combination of passages counts; citations, introductory setup, and illustrative follow-up questions need not all be repeated when the substantive claim is used. For overlapping cumulative entries, a shared prefix alone does not establish the added claim: for example, using R60’s finding does not also recall R61’s What Works Clearinghouse observation. Mere thematic similarity, a page citation, or a footnote callout is insufficient. The coverage notes below expose the semantic and combined-passage judgments; the same rules apply to both methods.

Each numbered answer is scored separately, including its headings and attribution labels. Claims from another question are not transferred. The general preface identifies the article and the Course; it is not a pool of additional substantive claims for every question. Relevance is exactly the supplied fixed lists. A claim can count once in each question where it is relevant; therefore the totals have 301 + 153 + 169 = **623 question–claim opportunities**, not 623 unique article claims.

A precision unit is one separately presented outer quotation. Nested quoted terms, titles, or speakers’ words inside that quotation are not counted again. The three explicitly unquoted figure descriptions in A–Q3 are checked for recall but are not quotations. Bullet numbers below are local to a question and include those descriptions, so A–Q3 has gaps in its quotation identifiers.

For exactness I compared all 188 quotations with the extracted PDF text using a script, normalizing whitespace, line-wrap hyphenation, quotation-mark/dash typography, superscript ordinals and note callouts, and removing running headers. Rendered pages resolve `letter-sounds` split across lines and superscript `1st`; a displaced footnote numeral is not treated as a word. Both inclusion and omission of a footnote callout preserve exact words. All 188 quotations matched after these layout corrections. No lexical alteration, invented wording, misleading ellipsis, or unmarked interpolation was found. Brackets such as `[We need]` and `[research]` are already present in the article.

For attribution, the labels distinguish (i) the authors’ own descriptions/criticisms, (ii) advocates’ positions the authors dispute, and (iii) the Course’s own embedded speech. NRP and DOE quotations are also identified. A description **by the authors of a position they dispute** is not scored as their endorsement of that position. Quoting only the Course’s words inside R206 or R256 is valid when the Course is explicitly identified, as both methods do. No disputing entry was credited under an incorrect speaker attribution.
