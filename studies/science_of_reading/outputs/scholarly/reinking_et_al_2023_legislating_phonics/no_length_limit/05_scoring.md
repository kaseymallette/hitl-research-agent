## Basis and counting rules

Only the five authorized input files were opened. I read the PDF using text extracted with Apple PDFKit through Swift and inspected rendered images of PDF pages 10–12 (journal pp. 113–115, all three figures) and PDF page 16 (journal p. 119). No cited works or outside sources were consulted, and the close-reading JSON mentioned inside Method B was not opened. Printed journal pagination is used below; PDF page 1 corresponds to journal p. 104.

All 322 numbered reference entries were assessed. I found no additional relevant substantive passage missing from the reference, so there are no R+ additions. The figure caveats, including the trial-basis warning in Figure 3, are already represented in R143 and are not counted again as new claims.

- **Relevance:** Q1 includes definitions, claimed scientific authority, political and classroom enactment, the historical development of those positions, and evidence/qualifications bearing on their claimed universality. Q2 includes professional authority, legitimate instructional knowledge and options, research/experience, mandated training, collaboration, and limits of expertise. Q3 includes the imagined learner, actual instructional experience, meaning and motivation, individual variation, research populations, assessment categories, and social conditions. Contextual evidence can bear on a question without naming it verbatim. The complete entry-by-entry classification appears below.
- **Recall:** The unit is the supplied R number, including overlapping entries. An explicitly equivalent assertion elsewhere in the same question’s answer can count; reproducing the exact reference sentence is not necessary. Mere topic overlap or a setup sentence does not suffice. Material additional assertions/qualifications are required: for example, R59 adds history and fees to R58; R61 adds the Clearinghouse comparison to R60; R260 adds continuing unresolved questions to R259. Source citations, rhetorical lead-ins, and supporting examples need not all be reproduced to use a substantive assertion. Standalone questions R145 and R155 count when their actual issue is explicitly addressed. No answer content is transferred across questions.
- **Precision:** Each separately delimited, outer quoted passage in an answer bullet is one quotation, including a multi-sentence passage. Embedded quotations are checked within that passage, rather than double counted. A passage can contain multiple reference claims and necessary context; it passes relevance when it makes a substantive contribution to the assigned question. All four conditions must pass. The nine explicitly labeled figure descriptions are paraphrases, so they count for recall but are not quotations in the precision denominator. Repeated quotations in different questions are assessed separately.
- **Exactness:** All 592 quotation passages were checked against extracted PDF text with a Python comparison, allowing whitespace, Unicode quotation/diacritic typography, line-wrap hyphenation, running headers, superscript note callouts, and captions that interrupt sentences across pages. The initial mismatches were resolved as layout effects. Image inspection confirmed the compound “letter-sounds,” the cross-page figure/body layout, and the figure data. No substituted, invented, or omitted internal words were found in either method’s quotations. Ending a quotation before a following sentence or source citation is not an inexact quotation.
- **Attribution:** Labels, surrounding prose, and quoted attribution were read together. Both methods distinguish the authors’ criticisms from the phonics-first/Course views they dispute. Reported Flesch, Ravitch, NRP, DOE, and neuroscience statements preserve the speakers required by the reference. There are no attribution failures, including among entries labeled disputing.
- **Totals:** Relevant-claim and used totals are sums of question–claim incidences, not deduplicated article claims. Total recall is total used divided by total relevant; total precision is all passing quotation passages divided by all quotation passages. Percentages are rounded to two decimals.

Method B beats Method A on recall in all three questions: +5 claims in Q1, +17 in Q2, and +12 in Q3, for 34 additional question–claim incidences overall (84.27% versus 78.81%, +5.46 percentage points). B does not beat A on precision: Q1 ties at 100%; B is lower in Q2 (94.95% versus 100%) and higher in Q3 (100% versus 98.04%), with lower pooled precision overall (98.83% versus 99.40%). Thus the null that B is no better is contradicted **for recall**, but stands **for precision**. Because the dimensions move in opposite directions and no combined superiority rule was specified, the article does not support an unqualified rejection of the overall null. This is a descriptive comparison for one article, not a statistical significance test. The methods’ different quotation lengths and the reference’s overlapping entries also limit a broad quality inference from the percentages alone.

Broadly relevant background and repeated reference entries increase the recall denominator; an entry may be missed even when an answer covers the general topic well. Passage-level precision also gives a longer quotation a different unit of assessment from several separately quoted excerpts. These properties warrant reporting both metrics and the full audit rather than treating either percentage as a general verdict on close reading.
