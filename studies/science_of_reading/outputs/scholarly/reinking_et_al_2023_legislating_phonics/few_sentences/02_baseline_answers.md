# Reinking, Hruby, and Risko (2023): baseline answers

Reinking, D., Hruby, G. G., & Risko, V. J. (2023). Legislating phonics: Settled science or political polemics? *Teachers College Record, 125*(1), 104–131. https://doi.org/10.1177/01614681231155688

Answered from the article alone. This scholarly commentary critiques a phonics-first ideology and its use of research to promote legislation, focusing on Tennessee’s Early Reading Training for Teachers and contrasting it with the preceding administration’s collaborative literacy initiative.

## 1. How is the science of reading defined, and how is it enacted?

### Advocates claim settled scientific authority for a phonics-first position

- **p. 110 · Phonics-first advocates’ view, reported and disputed by the authors.** "In recent years, those promoting a phonics-first ideology have taken the stance that their position is based on a settled science of reading, often drawing on esoteric neurological and psychological studies."
- **p. 106 · Phonics-first advocates’ view, characterized and disputed by the authors.** "Such legislation has been promoted by activists who are guided by what might be called a phonics-first ideology, with “first” denoting two related meanings fundamental to the ideology: phonics as being of foremost importance in learning to read, and phonics mastered before other aspects of reading are addressed."

### Legislation and incentivized teacher training promote a single phonics approach

- **p. 111 · Authors’ own account.** "The Tennessee DOE developed the Course in response to a provision in the Tennessee Literacy Success Act (2021) promoted by the current governor."
- **p. 111 · Authors’ own account.** "The Course is offered free online and was incentivized in the summer of 2021 with a $1,000 stipend for teachers who completed it (“TDOE Announces Stipends,” 2021)."
- **p. 117 · Authors’ own critique of the Course’s position.** "Yet, the Course promotes only one approach to phonics: a “synthetic” approach (teaching individual letters and their sounds that are then blended into words) without acknowledging that other approaches may be equally effective."

### The authors define reading research as multiple sciences with continuing questions

- **p. 122 · Authors’ own view.** "Thus, there is no one uppercase Science of Reading. Instead, there are multiple lowercase sciences of reading, each contributing interesting and relevant findings to our understanding."
- **p. 123 · Authors’ own view.** "Scientists aren’t satisfied with determining what works or not. They want to understand how, why, and in what circumstances different approaches may or may not be a good fit."

### The authors support phonics within comprehensive, adaptable instruction

- **p. 107 · Authors’ own view, endorsing research they cite.** "However, we are also convinced by research suggesting that phonics is more effective when embedded in a more comprehensive and balanced program of reading instruction; that multiple approaches to teaching phonics can be effective; and that there is a need to fine-tune all reading instruction to meet individual students’ needs (e.g., see Snow et al., 1998; Wixson et al., 2019)."

## 2. How is teachers' knowledge positioned?

### Teachers need broad reading knowledge, knowledge of individual students, and professional experience

- **p. 116 · Authors’ own view, endorsing research they cite.** "Arguably, and consistent with that research, the most important factors instructionally are teachers who are well informed, not just about phonics but about all aspects of reading development; who know their students’ individual needs and strengths and are prepared and supported in their response to those differences; who have a large repertoire of instructional options; and who supplement that knowledge with lessons learned from firsthand professional experience (Bondie et al., 2019)."
- **p. 122 · Authors’ own view.** "In practice, there is much room for professional judgment and variation, even to the point of rejecting findings from generalizations in light of the specific circumstances of unique cases."

### Political mandates diminish teachers’ professional authority and instructional judgment

- **p. 107 · Authors’ own critique.** "As teacher educators and professionals who work closely with teachers, we are dismayed to see their options to teach phonics limited, their flexibility to differentiate instruction in response to their students’ individual needs undermined, and their professionalism implicitly questioned and diminished by political decisions based on polemical arguments."
- **p. 122 · Authors’ own view of the limits of teachers’ and researchers’ specialist knowledge.** "Most teachers and researchers addressing issues of reading instruction are not qualified to assess the validity of findings, theories, and interpretations of brain research."

### Collaborative professional development offers an alternative to mandated practice

- **p. 112 · Authors’ own account of the earlier initiative.** "Further, unlike the top-down implementation of the current legislation, the DOE staff hired a literacy coach for each elementary school in the state and met regularly with those coaches."
- **p. 112 · Authors’ own account of the earlier initiative.** "The result was a jointly designed framework and professional development materials for early literacy instruction that accommodated multiple approaches to teaching phonics (e.g., supplementing decoding with making sense of meaningful texts) within a more comprehensive curriculum of foundational skills in the areas of language, vocabulary, background knowledge, and comprehension strategies."

## 3. How are students positioned?

### The criticized ideology treats children as following a uniform phonics sequence

- **p. 106 · Phonics-first advocates’ view, characterized and disputed by the authors.** "All children should move lockstep through a well-defined sequence of phonics skills. There is little, if any, need to differentiate instruction to meet the differing needs of individual students. Once children master the content of phonics, almost all of them will become successful readers."
- **p. 106 · Phonics-first advocates’ view, characterized and disputed by the authors.** "Virtually all difficulties in learning to read are attributed to and remedied by phonics."

### The authors position children as differing learners whose reading extends beyond decoding

- **p. 121 · Authors’ own view of balanced instruction.** "Another distinguishing aspect of a balanced approach is that it allows for flexibility of instructional approaches to address students’ needs in these areas."
- **p. 121 · National Reading Panel’s view, quoted and endorsed by the authors.** "It is important to evaluate children’s reading competence in many ways, not only their phonics skills but also their interest in books and their ability to understand information that is read to them."

### Students’ achievement is situated within social differences and institutional inequities

- **p. 116 · Authors’ own view.** "Social, cultural, and demographic factors that vary over time also undoubtedly influence education achievement in general, and reading achievement in particular."
- **p. 124 · Authors’ own view.** "Neither is there justification for suggesting that there is a settled science of reading that provides final answers about how reading should be taught to every child, particularly among those who live in poverty and who experience institutional inequities and injustice."
