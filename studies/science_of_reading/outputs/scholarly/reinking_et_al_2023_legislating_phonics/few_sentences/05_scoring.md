## Scope and counting rules

I used only `article.pdf`, `source.yaml`, `01_claims_reference.md`, `02_baseline_answers.md`, and `04_close_reading_answers.md`. I did not consult the close-reading file mentioned inside Method B or any external sources.

I extracted the PDF’s 28 pages locally with Apple PDFKit through JavaScript for Automation (`osascript -l JavaScript`). Each of the 42 answer quotation passages was checked by a tool against its cited PDF page. The article begins at journal p. 104 (PDF p. 1); subsequent printed pages follow in order. Matching preserves words, punctuation, and capitalization, while normalizing whitespace and joining words divided across typeset lines. For example, PDFKit extracts “fundamen- tal” and “under- standing”; these are layout divisions. The hyphen in “letter-sounds” remains a lexical hyphen even where the PDF wraps after it. Method A’s stipend quotation omits the superscript note-callout 7 after “online”; that bibliographic marker is not part of the sentence’s wording and is not an inexactness. No quotation changes the article’s lexical wording or punctuation.

Recall is scored within each numbered question’s section, including its headings and attribution labels. The shared introductory article summaries and citations are not additional question-specific answers. A relevant R entry is used when the answer expressly uses a substantive assertion from its numbered claim quotation, whether by quotation or a faithful paraphrase. Merely addressing the same topic, requiring an unstated inference about another finding, or reproducing a sentence only in an entry’s Evidence field does not earn credit. A reference entry can contain several assertions: using an identifiable assertion earns use credit, but does not mean that every assertion in that entry has been covered. Overlapping numbered entries remain separate fixed reference units. Thus the professional-judgment excerpt uses R247 and R248; the neuroscience-expertise sentence uses R251 and R252; and Method B’s testing sentence uses R263 and R264. All such overlap is counted equally for both methods.

Precision uses one unit per complete, separately presented quotation passage: 20 for A and 22 for B. Three sentences quoted together remain one passage; nested quotation marks, quoted terms, and citation titles inside a passage are not counted again. The bibliographic article title is not an evidentiary quotation. Repeated passages in different questions are checked and counted for each use. A passage passes only if it is exact, matches a numbered reference assertion, is relevant to its assigned question, and preserves the required attribution. Recall units and quotation units therefore differ. Totals sum question–claim pairs (623 per method), rather than deduplicating R numbers across questions; total recall and precision are pooled fractions.
