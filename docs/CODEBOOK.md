- **Corpus:** State Legislation (Corpus 2 of 3) — trial run: six texts from five states
- **Analytical framework:** Critical Discourse Analysis (Fairclough, 2003; Rogers, 2011)

## CDA dimensions

- **LEX:** Lexical Choices & Definitional Framing — how SoR is named, bounded, and defined in statutory language
- **PRE:** Presuppositions — claims taken for granted in legislative findings and intent sections
- **FOS:** Foregrounding & Silencing — what the legislation centres vs. omits
- **SUB:** Subject Positioning — how teachers, students, parents, and districts are constructed in law
- **INT:** Intertextuality — which research, texts, and authorities statutes cite, invoke, or exclude

## Excerpt fields

- **Excerpt ID:** [DIM]-[TEXT]-[##], where TEXT is CA, AR17, AR25, OH, GA, or MN
- **Observation:** the analysis's heading for the observation
- **Source:** state · year · document type · section and page
- **Code:** the code applied in the analysis, with its qualifier
- **Judge:** the judging session's finding on the code
- **Raw excerpt:** verbatim from the law
- **Analytic memo:** the analysis's reading of the passage
- **Certain or uncertain:** the analysis's statement of how sure the reading is

## Codes

Each definition keeps the original wording up to where the spreadsheet cut it
off; the rest was drafted from the excerpts coded with it and is marked
*(draft)* until reviewed. A code applies where the passage it cites shows what
the definition describes.

### LEX — Lexical Choices & Definitional Framing

- **LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor** — SoR defined by reference to NRP's five pillars (phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension): the five components are given as the definition or foundation of reading instruction, whether or not the National Reading Panel is named. *(draft)*
- **LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition** — SoR defined via IDA's Structured Literacy framework: instruction defined as "structured literacy", by its descriptors (explicit, systematic, sequential, cumulative), or by reference to the International Dyslexia Association's definition. *(draft)*
- **LEX-3: Simple View / Research Body** — SoR defined by reference to the Simple View of Reading (reading as the product of word recognition and language comprehension), or characterized as a body of research rather than a list of components or a program. *(draft)*
- **LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language** — 'Evidence-based,' 'scientifically-based,' or 'research-based' set as the standard for instruction or materials without a definition, a named source, or criteria for what qualifies. *(draft)*
- **LEX-5: Method Prohibition Language** — Explicit statutory naming and prohibition of specific instructional methods or programs (such as three-cueing or Reading Recovery), defining SoR by what it excludes. A general ban on materials that are "not evidence-based" is LEX-4. *(draft)*
- **LEX-6: Crisis / Urgency Framing** — Legislative findings sections deploy crisis, failure, or emergency language ("crisis", "emergency", "failing") to characterize current reading outcomes and justify the scope or speed of intervention. *(draft)*

### PRE — Presuppositions

- **PRE-1: Universal Learnability** — 'Every child can learn to read' taken for granted: the text assumes all students can reach proficiency given the right instruction, as background rather than as a claim it supports. *(draft)*
- **PRE-2: Instructional Deficit** — Current reading outcomes presupposed to result primarily from instruction (methods, curricula, or teacher preparation), so that low achievement is treated as preventable by changing instruction, and other causes go unstated. *(draft)*
- **PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap** — Teachers presupposed to lack knowledge of reading science, so that training, certification, or retraining requirements are presented as the remedy. *(draft)*
- **PRE-4: Research Consensus** — SoR's findings presupposed to be settled, unanimous, or conclusive ("settled", "conclusively established", "the science"), with no acknowledgment of debate, limits, or ongoing research. *(draft)*
- **PRE-5: Legislative Competence** — Legislatures presupposed to be appropriate arbiters of instructional method: choosing, mandating, or banning methods and materials is treated as a matter for the legislature or state agency rather than for professional or local judgment. *(draft)*
- **PRE-6: Mississippi Transferability** — Mississippi's multi-component reform presupposed to transfer: Mississippi's outcomes, or one component of its reform, invoked as evidence that the adopting state's provisions will work, without the conditions of Mississippi's reform. No excerpt is coded with it yet. *(draft)*

### FOS — Foregrounding & Silencing

- **FOS-1: Phonics / Decoding Foregrounded** — Phonics and decoding instruction centred as the primary or foundational element of reading instruction, in definitions, mandates, or the share of provisions devoted to it, with other components positioned as secondary or downstream. *(draft)*
- **FOS-2: Dyslexia Foregrounded** — Dyslexia screening, identification, and intervention given central place, so that the framework for struggling readers is organized around dyslexia. *(draft)*
- **FOS-3: Assessment / Screening Foregrounded** — Universal screening and diagnostic assessment mandates given central place: the screen, identify, and intervene sequence as a primary legislative mechanism. *(draft)*
- **FOS-4: Background Knowledge / Comprehension Silenced** — Background knowledge, knowledge-building curriculum, and comprehension instruction absent or minimal where the text defines or lists what reading instruction must include. *(draft)*
- **FOS-5: English Learners Silenced** — EL-specific research, translanguaging, and multilingual learners' needs absent where the text sets instruction, screening, or intervention requirements that apply to them. *(draft)*
- **FOS-6: Structural Causes Silenced** — Poverty, school funding, class size, and racial segregation absent as causes of reading outcomes where the text explains outcomes or sets remedies, leaving instruction or curriculum as the only lever. *(draft)*
- **FOS-7: Oral Language / Writing Silenced** — Oral language development and writing instruction absent where the text defines or lists what literacy instruction must include. *(draft)*

A silencing code (FOS-4 to FOS-7) applies only where the text defines, lists,
or explains the area the topic belongs to, so that its absence is a choice the
text makes. Cite that passage and say what it addresses instead. Where the
topic is present, use the code with *(counter-example)*. *(draft)*

### SUB — Subject Positioning

- **SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated** — Teachers constructed as lacking knowledge and therefore subject to regulation: required training, conditions on certification, prohibitions on their methods, or sanctions. *(draft)*
- **SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional** — Teachers constructed as professionals deserving support, whose judgment and learning are the means of implementation (professional learning, coaching, collaboration) rather than objects of compliance. *(draft)*
- **SUB-3: Student as Victim / Beneficiary** — Students — especially Black, low-income, and dyslexic students — constructed as harmed by current instruction and as beneficiaries of reform, or as the objects of accountability measures such as retention. *(draft)*
- **SUB-4: Parent as Rights-Holder** — Parents constructed as rights-holders with legal entitlements to information about, notice of, or a say in their child's reading instruction. *(draft)*
- **SUB-5: District as Compliance Target** — Local school districts and administrators constructed as compliance targets: required to adopt, purchase, or report as the state directs, with funding or approval contingent on compliance. *(draft)*
- **SUB-6: Publisher / Curriculum as Suspect** — Curriculum publishers and specific programs implicated in reading failure or treated as suspect until approved: approval lists, removal provisions, or programs named as inconsistent with the science of reading. *(draft)*
- **SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter** — The state legislature or education agency constructed as the authority that defines valid instruction, approves materials and training, and corrects local failure. *(draft)*

### INT — Intertextuality

- **INT-1: NRP / Federal Report Citation** — National Reading Panel (2000) cited as the foundation for the text's definition or requirements, by name, as is any other federal report cited that way. Listing the five components without naming the report is LEX-1 only. *(draft)*
- **INT-2: IDA / Professional Organisation Citation** — International Dyslexia Association cited as definitional authority, or another professional or advocacy organisation's standard adopted by reference, so that the organisation's definition governs the text's meaning. *(draft)*
- **INT-3: Interstate Intertextuality** — One state's legislation explicitly or implicitly cites or models another state's: naming it as a model, citing its outcomes as evidence, or reproducing its provisions. Within a single document, code only explicit references; implicit borrowing needs a comparison across the corpus. *(draft)*
- **INT-4: Research Literature Citation** — Peer-reviewed research, named researchers, or specific studies cited in the text or its legislative record as warrant for a provision. *(draft)*
- **INT-5: Media / Advocacy Intertextuality** — Emily Hanford's journalism or advocacy organisations' reports (such as ExcelinEd or NCTQ) cited or invoked as evidence in the text or its legislative record. *(draft)*
- **INT-6: Historical Intertextuality** — References to Reading First, NCLB, or other historical reading policies or reports, as precedent to follow or as a past approach the text corrects or moves beyond. *(draft)*
- **INT-7: Contested Intertextuality** — Texts or authorities cited by SoR advocates specifically to discredit opposing approaches, or programs and texts the statute names as inconsistent with the science of reading, including legal challenges that follow. *(draft)*

### Code qualifiers

A code can carry a qualifier in parentheses when the text relates to its
pattern in a particular way. *(draft)*

- **(counter-example)** — the text does the opposite of the code's pattern: a silencing code where the topic is foregrounded, as in FOS-5 for Minnesota, whose screening and instruction requirements name multilingual learners.
- **(absent — notable)** — the pattern is missing where comparable texts carry it, and the absence is meaningful, as in LEX-6 for Georgia, whose intent section uses constructive rather than crisis vocabulary. Cite the passage where the pattern would be expected.
- **(qualified)** — the text carries the pattern but limits it, as in PRE-5 for California, where state criteria for materials sit alongside a route for local selection.
- **(reframed)** — the text carries a revised version of the pattern, as in PRE-3 for California, which places the need in administrator preparation programs rather than in teachers.
- **(inverted)** — the text constructs the opposite subject position. The trial run didn't use it.
- **(partial)** — only part of the pattern is present, as in LEX-1 for California, whose component list says phonological rather than phonemic awareness and adds print concepts.

The examples for Minnesota, Georgia, and California come from the trial run;
see the [trial run matrix](#trial-run-matrix).

### Clarifications to existing codes *(draft)*

Where the trial's judges found a code didn't fit, the definition left the
case open. These notes close it one way; each is a choice to confirm.

- **LEX-3:** Arkansas 2025 defines the science of reading as "the study of the
  relationship between cognitive science and educational outcomes." The judge
  found this neither the Simple View nor a body of research, so LEX-3 didn't
  fit. Ohio's "interdisciplinary body of scientific evidence" did fit. Either
  widen LEX-3 to "a body of research or a field of study", or leave
  field-of-study definitions as their own case.
- **LEX-4:** where "evidence-based" is given criteria (components, a record of
  success), code LEX-4 *(counter-example)*, as the trial did for Minnesota's
  definition of evidence-based. California § 60205(b)(2) is the same case and
  was marked "no code fits".
- **PRE-2:** applies only where the text presupposes that instruction caused
  current outcomes. Prescribing an instructional remedy is not enough
  (Arkansas 2025, Minnesota).
- **PRE-4:** applies only where the text presupposes that the research is
  settled. A categorical statement that all students benefit from an approach
  is not enough (Ohio § 3313.6028(A)(1)(c)); it may be PRE-1.
- **PRE-5:** applies to choosing, mandating, or banning instructional methods
  and materials. General enforcement or implementation authority is SUB-7, not
  PRE-5 (Arkansas 2017 § 6-17-429(g)).

### Codes the trial didn't use

No text in the trial carried PRE-6, INT-1, INT-3, INT-4, INT-5, INT-6, or
INT-7, and LEX-6 appeared only as *(absent — notable)*, for Georgia. Apart
from Georgia's incorporation of the IDA definition (INT-2), none of the six
texts names a federal report, a study, a researcher, the media, or another
state. These codes may still apply to findings sections, legislative records,
or texts outside the trial.

### LEX — Lexical Choices & Definitional Framing

#### LEX-CA-01 — California AB 1454 (2025)

- **Observation:** Specified instructional content without a science-of-reading label
- **Source:** California · 2025 · Bill, chaptered · SECTION 1, Education Code §44265.1(a)(2), p. 2; repeated in SEC. 2, §44270.6(a)(2), p. 3, and, with a materials-specific opening and without “to all pupils,” in SEC. 3, §60205(b)(2), p. 3.
- **Code:** LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor (partial)
- **Judge:** the code fits.

**Raw excerpt:**

> “Evidence-based means of teaching foundational reading skills, which shall include explicit and systematic instruction in print concepts, phonological awareness, phonics and word recognition, and fluency to all pupils, and attending to oral and written language development, vocabulary and background knowledge, and comprehension, including tiered supports for pupils with reading difficulties, English learners, and pupils with exceptional needs.”

**Analytic memo:** The act never uses the label science of reading. Instead, it gives instructional content to evidence-based literacy through a repeated list spanning credential preparation and materials. The list resembles the five-component anchor but says phonological awareness rather than phonemic awareness, adds print concepts, language development, background knowledge, and tiered supports, and does not name the National Reading Panel. “Shall include” establishes required content without making the list exhaustive. This is neither an explicit five-pillars definition of reading nor an unelaborated appeal to evidence.

**Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain as a five-pillars coding: the shared components support the partial code, but the list can also be read as its own broader instructional specification. The lexical differences and absence of an NRP citation are certain; they preclude treating this as adoption of that report.

#### LEX-CA-02 — California AB 1454 (2025)

- **Observation:** Evidence as an authorizing adjective, with limits to its specification
- **Source:** California · 2025 · Bill, chaptered · SEC. 3, Education Code §60205(b)(2), p. 3.
- **Code:** No code fits
- **Judge:** no code fits; proposed: LEX-4 *(counter-example)*, per the LEX-4 clarification *(draft)*

**Raw excerpt:**

> “Align to evidence-based means of teaching foundational reading skills, which shall include explicit and systematic instruction in print concepts, phonological awareness, phonics and word recognition, and fluency, and attending to oral and written language development, vocabulary and background knowledge, and comprehension, including tiered supports for pupils with reading difficulties, English learners, and pupils with exceptional needs.”

**Analytic memo:** “Evidence-based” gives the enumerated approach epistemic authority, while “align” makes it a standard against which materials can be judged. The provision specifies instructional features but supplies no study, evidence threshold, or procedure for deciding which evidence qualifies. LEX-4 does not fully describe this combination because that code requires the absence of a definition, named source, or qualifying criteria: here there are substantive criteria and adjacent framework and guideline references. Nor do “explicit and systematic” alone establish adoption of a named structured-literacy framework. The act bounds acceptable instruction through positive alignment requirements; it names no prohibited method or program.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the wording and the absence of a named study or method prohibition. Uncertain about how fully the instructional list resolves evidence judgments: it may function as an adequate practical specification, or leave substantial interpretive work to the adopting authorities. There is no separate legislative findings section asserting a literacy crisis, universal proficiency, or a settled research consensus. The strongest presuppositions concern the institutions entitled to set standards and the preparation through which instruction is to be improved.

#### LEX-AR17-01 — Arkansas Act 1063 (2017)

- **Observation:** Scientific authority without a substantive definition
- **Source:** Arkansas · 2017 · Act · Section 1, § 6-17-429(b)(1)(A), introductory clause (page 1).
- **Code:** LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language (qualified)
- **Judge:** the code fits.

**Raw excerpt:**

> “No later than 2023, the following shall have proficient knowledge and skills to teach reading consistent with the best practices of scientific reading instruction:”

**Analytic memo:** “Scientific” authorizes the instructional standard, while “best practices” presents a preferred body of practice as already identifiable. Neither phrase supplies components, research-selection criteria, or a named research authority. This fits LEX-4's undefined scientific standard, although (b)(2) supplies an external assessment alignment point, so the act is not wholly without an institutional anchor. The wording does not itself establish a five-pillars, structured-literacy, or Simple View definition, and it names no prohibited method.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the absence of a substantive definition in this act and the authoritative work of the vocabulary. What the referenced assessment might specify cannot be established from this document.

#### LEX-AR17-02 — Arkansas Act 1063 (2017)

- **Observation:** Knowledge divided into credential levels
- **Source:** Arkansas · 2017 · Act · Section 1, § 6-17-429(d)(1)(A)-(B) (page 2).
- **Code:** no code fits
- **Judge:** no code fits; proposed: FOS-8: Educator Credentialing Foregrounded *(draft)*

**Raw excerpt:**

> “All teachers employed in a teaching position that requires an elementary education (K-6) license or special education (K-12) license shall demonstrate proficiency in knowledge and practices of scientific reading instruction; and” / “All other teachers shall demonstrate awareness in knowledge and practices of the scientific reading instruction.”

**Analytic memo:** “Proficiency” and “awareness” turn knowledge of reading instruction into differentiated levels of professional obligation. The division here follows the license required by the teaching position and expressly includes special education across K-12 in the proficiency group. This lexical hierarchy defines who must know how much more clearly than it defines what reading science contains. It should not be projected back as a complete resolution of (b)(1), where the broad program-completer proficiency category is followed by an awareness requirement for non-elementary program completers.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the differentiated wording and coverage here; the act does not specify the substantive threshold separating the two levels.

#### LEX-AR17-03 — Arkansas Act 1063 (2017)

- **Observation:** Reading named as a right
- **Source:** Arkansas · 2017 · Act · Section 1, § 6-17-429(a) (page 1).
- **Code:** no code fits
- **Judge:** no code fits; proposed: LEX-7: Rights Framing *(draft)*

**Raw excerpt:**

> “This section shall be known and may be cited as the "Right to Read Act".”

**Analytic memo:** The short title places the technical requirements under an entitlement-oriented name. Reading is framed as something owed, giving teacher preparation and credential regulation a moral purpose beyond administrative compliance. The title does not identify a right-holder or establish an individual remedy; the operative provisions organize institutional duties rather than elaborating the named right.

**Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain about how strongly the title should be read as an entitlement claim. It may function principally as a concise legislative label, although its rights vocabulary still frames the provisions that follow. There are no findings or purpose provisions explaining reading outcomes. The strongest presuppositional observations concern the machinery of preparation and state authority, rather than explicit claims of universal learnability, crisis, or research consensus.

#### LEX-AR25-01 — Arkansas Code § 6-17-429 (2025)

- **Observation:** A field of study becomes a governing term
- **Source:** Arkansas · 2025 · Code section · § 6-17-429(n)(1), p. 15.
- **Code:** LEX-3: Simple View / Research Body (partial)
- **Judge:** LEX-3 doesn't fit: the definition is neither the Simple View nor a body of research. No code fits.

**Raw excerpt:**

> ““Science of reading” means the study of the relationship between cognitive science and educational outcomes; and”

**Analytic memo:** The definition locates authority in a relationship between a scientific field and educational results. It defines an activity of study rather than a curriculum, a list of skills, or a particular finding. This fits the research orientation of LEX-3 only partly: neither a body of research nor the Simple View is expressly named. Its breadth leaves the earlier requirements for alignment dependent on administrative interpretation.

**Certain or uncertain:** Uncertain as a code fit. This could be read simply as a broad statutory definition of a field, without the accumulated-research meaning that LEX-3 describes.

#### LEX-AR25-02 — Arkansas Code § 6-17-429 (2025)

- **Observation:** Instruction defined through its manner
- **Source:** Arkansas · 2025 · Code section · § 6-17-429(n)(2), p. 16.
- **Code:** LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition
- **Judge:** the code fits.

**Raw excerpt:**

> ““Structured literacy” means an approach by which licensed personnel teach reading in an explicit, systematic, cumulative, and diagnostic manner.”

**Analytic memo:** Four descriptors make the manner of teaching central to the definition. The reference to licensed personnel also connects the approach to an authorized professional role. These same descriptors govern approved materials in (f), carrying the definition into purchasing criteria. The text does not attribute this definition to the International Dyslexia Association.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the procedural framing and code fit; the passage supplies no basis for attributing the wording to an unnamed organization.

#### LEX-AR25-03 — Arkansas Code § 6-17-429 (2025)

- **Observation:** Five components as intervention content
- **Source:** Arkansas · 2025 · Code section · § 6-17-429(k)(2)(B)(v), p. 12.
- **Code:** LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor (qualified)
- **Judge:** the code fits.

**Raw excerpt:**

> “The intensive, evidence-based literacy intervention program aligned to the science of reading the student's teacher will use to address the areas of phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension;”

**Analytic memo:** The five components make an otherwise broad alignment requirement operational: they specify what the planned intervention will address. Their inclusion anchors required intervention content without replacing the separate definition in (n)(1). The National Reading Panel is not named, and the list establishes no explicit hierarchy among its components.

**Certain or uncertain:** Certain about the five-component anchor; qualified because this is an intervention requirement rather than the definition of reading science for the whole section.

#### LEX-AR25-04 — Arkansas Code § 6-17-429 (2025)

- **Observation:** Evidence as an approval label
- **Source:** Arkansas · 2025 · Code section · § 6-17-429(f)(3) and (g)(2)(B), p. 4.
- **Code:** LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language (qualified)
- **Judge:** the code fits.
