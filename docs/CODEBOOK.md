- **Corpus:** State Legislation (Corpus 2 of 3) — 48 U.S. Jurisdictions
- **Analytical framework:** Critical Discourse Analysis (Fairclough, 2003; Rogers, 2011)

## Corpus sources

- **Primary texts:** Statutory language from enacted literacy legislation, legislative findings sections, state board rules, and executive orders referencing 'science of reading,' 'structured literacy,' or equivalent terms — 48 U.S. jurisdictions (40 states + D.C. + 7 states with board/executive policy only)
- **Cross-state surveys:** Shanker Institute (2023) — 223 bills in 45 states; EdWeek Tracker (Oct 2025); ExcelinEd in Action (2025); NCTQ State of the States (2024)
- **Supplementary:** RAND American Instructional Resources Survey (Spring 2024); CCSSO State Scan; Stateline / Pew (April 2025)

## Key states (exemplars per code)

- **Deep / pioneering:** Mississippi (2013+), Louisiana (2021–23), Tennessee (2023), Florida (2023), Arkansas (2023 LEARNS Act)
- **Equity-explicit:** Minnesota READ Act (2023), Minnesota most equity-explicit nationally; North Carolina (Leandro); Maryland Blueprint
- **Method bans:** 13+ states explicitly ban three-cueing by statute: MS, FL, TN, LA, AR, OH, IN, IA, VA, AL, SC, GA (2025)
- **Bipartisan exemplars:** Georgia (2025, 0 no votes); Delaware (Shanker curriculum-PD alignment model)
- **Contested / late:** California (AB 2222, 2024; CTA opposition; EL debate); Illinois (guidance only, weak)

## CDA dimensions

- **LEX:** Lexical Choices & Definitional Framing — how SoR is named, bounded, and defined in statutory language
- **PRE:** Presuppositions — claims taken for granted in legislative findings and intent sections
- **FOS:** Foregrounding & Silencing — what the legislation centres vs. omits
- **SUB:** Subject Positioning — how teachers, students, parents, and districts are constructed in law
- **INT:** Intertextuality — which research, texts, and authorities statutes cite, invoke, or exclude

## Codes

Each definition keeps the original wording up to where the spreadsheet cut it
off; the rest was drafted from the excerpts coded with it and is marked
*(draft)* until reviewed. A code applies where the passage it cites shows what
the definition describes.

### LEX — Lexical Choices & Definitional Framing

- **LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor** — SoR defined by reference to NRP's five pillars (phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension): the five components are given as the definition or foundation of reading instruction, whether or not the National Reading Panel is named. *(draft)*
- **LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition** — SoR defined via IDA's Structured Literacy framework: instruction defined as "structured literacy", by its descriptors (explicit, systematic, sequential, cumulative), or by reference to the International Dyslexia Association's definition. *(draft)*
- **LEX-3: Simple View / Research Body** — SoR defined by reference to the Simple View of Reading (reading as the product of word recognition and language comprehension), or characterized as a body of research rather than a list of components or a program. *(draft)*
- **LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language** — 'Evidence-based,' 'scientifically-based,' or 'research-based' set as the standard for instruction or materials without a definition, a named source, or criteria for what qualifies. *(draft)*
- **LEX-5: Method Prohibition Language** — Explicit statutory naming and prohibition of specific instructional methods or programs (such as three-cueing or Reading Recovery), defining SoR by what it excludes. A general ban on materials that are "not evidence-based" is LEX-4. *(draft)*
- **LEX-6: Crisis / Urgency Framing** — Legislative findings sections deploy crisis, failure, or emergency language ("crisis", "emergency", "failing") to characterize current reading outcomes and justify the scope or speed of intervention. *(draft)*

### PRE — Presuppositions

- **PRE-1: Universal Learnability** — 'Every child can learn to read' taken for granted: the text assumes all students can reach proficiency given the right instruction, as background rather than as a claim it supports. *(draft)*
- **PRE-2: Instructional Deficit** — Current reading outcomes presupposed to result primarily from instruction (methods, curricula, or teacher preparation), so that low achievement is treated as preventable by changing instruction, and other causes go unstated. *(draft)*
- **PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap** — Teachers presupposed to lack knowledge of reading science, so that training, certification, or retraining requirements are presented as the remedy. *(draft)*
- **PRE-4: Research Consensus** — SoR's findings presupposed to be settled, unanimous, or conclusive ("settled", "conclusively established", "the science"), with no acknowledgment of debate, limits, or ongoing research. *(draft)*
- **PRE-5: Legislative Competence** — Legislatures presupposed to be appropriate arbiters of instructional method: choosing, mandating, or banning methods and materials is treated as a matter for the legislature or state agency rather than for professional or local judgment. *(draft)*
- **PRE-6: Mississippi Transferability** — Mississippi's multi-component reform presupposed to transfer: Mississippi's outcomes, or one component of its reform, invoked as evidence that the adopting state's provisions will work, without the conditions of Mississippi's reform. No excerpt is coded with it yet. *(draft)*

### FOS — Foregrounding & Silencing

- **FOS-1: Phonics / Decoding Foregrounded** — Phonics and decoding instruction centred as the primary or foundational element of reading instruction, in definitions, mandates, or the share of provisions devoted to it, with other components positioned as secondary or downstream. *(draft)*
- **FOS-2: Dyslexia Foregrounded** — Dyslexia screening, identification, and intervention given central place, so that the framework for struggling readers is organized around dyslexia. *(draft)*
- **FOS-3: Assessment / Screening Foregrounded** — Universal screening and diagnostic assessment mandates given central place: the screen, identify, and intervene sequence as a primary legislative mechanism. *(draft)*
- **FOS-4: Background Knowledge / Comprehension Silenced** — Background knowledge, knowledge-building curriculum, and comprehension instruction absent or minimal where the text defines or lists what reading instruction must include. *(draft)*
- **FOS-5: English Learners Silenced** — EL-specific research, translanguaging, and multilingual learners' needs absent where the text sets instruction, screening, or intervention requirements that apply to them. *(draft)*
- **FOS-6: Structural Causes Silenced** — Poverty, school funding, class size, and racial segregation absent as causes of reading outcomes where the text explains outcomes or sets remedies, leaving instruction or curriculum as the only lever. *(draft)*
- **FOS-7: Oral Language / Writing Silenced** — Oral language development and writing instruction absent where the text defines or lists what literacy instruction must include. *(draft)*

A silencing code (FOS-4 to FOS-7) applies only where the text defines, lists,
or explains the area the topic belongs to, so that its absence is a choice the
text makes. Cite that passage and say what it addresses instead. Where the
topic is present, use the code with *(counter-example)*. *(draft)*

### SUB — Subject Positioning

- **SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated** — Teachers constructed as lacking knowledge and therefore subject to regulation: required training, conditions on certification, prohibitions on their methods, or sanctions. *(draft)*
- **SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional** — Teachers constructed as professionals deserving support, whose judgment and learning are the means of implementation (professional learning, coaching, collaboration) rather than objects of compliance. *(draft)*
- **SUB-3: Student as Victim / Beneficiary** — Students — especially Black, low-income, and dyslexic students — constructed as harmed by current instruction and as beneficiaries of reform, or as the objects of accountability measures such as retention. *(draft)*
- **SUB-4: Parent as Rights-Holder** — Parents constructed as rights-holders with legal entitlements to information about, notice of, or a say in their child's reading instruction. *(draft)*
- **SUB-5: District as Compliance Target** — Local school districts and administrators constructed as compliance targets: required to adopt, purchase, or report as the state directs, with funding or approval contingent on compliance. *(draft)*
- **SUB-6: Publisher / Curriculum as Suspect** — Curriculum publishers and specific programs implicated in reading failure or treated as suspect until approved: approval lists, removal provisions, or programs named as inconsistent with the science of reading. *(draft)*
- **SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter** — The state legislature or education agency constructed as the authority that defines valid instruction, approves materials and training, and corrects local failure. *(draft)*

### INT — Intertextuality

- **INT-1: NRP / Federal Report Citation** — National Reading Panel (2000) cited as the foundation for the text's definition or requirements, by name, as is any other federal report cited that way. Listing the five components without naming the report is LEX-1 only. *(draft)*
- **INT-2: IDA / Professional Organisation Citation** — International Dyslexia Association cited as definitional authority, or another professional or advocacy organisation's standard adopted by reference, so that the organisation's definition governs the text's meaning. *(draft)*
- **INT-3: Interstate Intertextuality** — One state's legislation explicitly or implicitly cites or models another state's: naming it as a model, citing its outcomes as evidence, or reproducing its provisions. Within a single document, code only explicit references; implicit borrowing needs a comparison across the corpus. *(draft)*
- **INT-4: Research Literature Citation** — Peer-reviewed research, named researchers, or specific studies cited in the text or its legislative record as warrant for a provision. *(draft)*
- **INT-5: Media / Advocacy Intertextuality** — Emily Hanford's journalism or advocacy organisations' reports (such as ExcelinEd or NCTQ) cited or invoked as evidence in the text or its legislative record. *(draft)*
- **INT-6: Historical Intertextuality** — References to Reading First, NCLB, or other historical reading policies or reports, as precedent to follow or as a past approach the text corrects or moves beyond. *(draft)*
- **INT-7: Contested Intertextuality** — Texts or authorities cited by SoR advocates specifically to discredit opposing approaches, or programs and texts the statute names as inconsistent with the science of reading, including legal challenges that follow. *(draft)*

### Code qualifiers

A code can carry a qualifier in parentheses when the text relates to its
pattern in a particular way. *(draft)*

- **(counter-example)** — the text does the opposite of the code's pattern: a silencing code where the topic is foregrounded, as in FOS-5 for Minnesota's EL provisions.
- **(absent — notable)** — the pattern is missing where comparable texts carry it, and the absence is meaningful, as in LEX-6 for Georgia's findings without crisis language. Cite the passage where the pattern would be expected.
- **(qualified)** — the text carries the pattern but limits it, as in PRE-4 for Minnesota's "no single approach" finding.
- **(reframed)** — the text carries a revised version of the pattern, as in PRE-3 for Georgia's preparation systems in place of teachers. The one use of *(modified)*, PRE-1 for Minnesota, reads the same way.
- **(inverted)** — the text constructs the opposite subject position, as in SUB-5 for Illinois's districts as autonomous agents.
- **(partial)** — only part of the pattern is present, as in LEX-1 for Minnesota.

## Coded excerpts

### LEX — Lexical Choices & Definitional Framing

#### LEX-MS-01 — Mississippi

- **Source:** MS · 2013/2019 · Statute · Miss. Code Ann. §37-177-1 et seq.
- **Dimension:** LEX
- **Code:** LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor
- **Code definition:** SoR defined by NRP's Five Pillars; 'every child can learn' as universalising presupposition
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → PRE-MS-01, FOS-MS-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The Legislature finds that every child can learn to read proficiently when provided with research-based reading instruction grounded in the five components identified by the National Reading Panel: phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and reading comprehension.

**Analytic memo:** Mississippi's 2013 Literacy-Based Promotion Act is the foundational text for the Five Pillars anchor adopted by 34 subsequent states. The NRP citation (2000) is doing definitional work: it delegates the question of what counts as reading science to a federal panel report, insulating the legislative definition from academic contest. 'Every child can learn' performs optimism while simultaneously constructing current outcomes as a result of instructional failure (→ PRE-2). Notably absent: background knowledge, oral language, writing.

#### LEX-TN-01 — Tennessee

- **Source:** TN · 2023 · Statute · Tenn. Code Ann. §49-6-2904
- **Dimension:** LEX
- **Code:** LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition
- **Code definition:** SoR defined via IDA's Structured Literacy framework — the most prescriptive statutory definition in the corpus
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → INT-TN-01, FOS-TN-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> As used in this part, 'structured literacy' means a comprehensive, evidence-based approach to reading instruction that is explicit, systematic, sequential, and cumulative, consistent with the science of reading, and aligned with the definition adopted by the International Dyslexia Association.

**Analytic memo:** Tennessee is one of five states that codifies IDA's Structured Literacy definition directly. 'Aligned with the definition adopted by the International Dyslexia Association' is a remarkable legislative move: it delegates definitional authority to a private professional organisation, insulating the statute from legislative revision. 'Explicit, systematic, sequential, and cumulative' encodes a specific pedagogical tradition — one that originated in dyslexia intervention research and is now applied universally. The definition is more restrictive than NRP's Five Pillars (→ LEX-1) and more operationally specific than the Simple View (→ LEX-3).

#### LEX-AZ-01 — Arizona

- **Source:** AZ · 2022 · Statute · A.R.S. §15-704
- **Dimension:** LEX
- **Code:** LEX-3: Simple View / Research Body
- **Code definition:** Arizona encodes the Simple View of Reading (Gough & Tunmer, 1986) as the statutory definition — the most theoretically complete formulation in the corpus
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → PRE-AZ-01, FOS-AZ-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Reading instruction shall be grounded in the science of reading, which includes the body of research establishing that reading ability is the product of language comprehension and word recognition, and that both components must be explicitly taught.

**Analytic memo:** The Simple View (Reading = Word Recognition × Language Comprehension) is the most complete theoretical anchor available in the corpus: it names both decoding and comprehension as independently necessary. Arizona's statute is therefore discursively more faithful to the research base than the NRP Five Pillars anchor (→ LEX-1), which centres five components but does not articulate their relationship. The phrase 'body of research establishing' positions SoR as an empirical accumulation rather than a single framework — preserving more definitional flexibility. 'Both components must be explicitly taught' is the operative mandate.

#### LEX-IL-01 — Illinois

- **Source:** IL · 2023 · State Board Guidance · ISBE Literacy Plan Guidance (non-statutory)
- **Dimension:** LEX
- **Code:** LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language
- **Code definition:** 'Evidence-based' and 'scientifically-based' used without definition — the compliance loophole
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → PRE-IL-01, FOS-IL-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Districts are encouraged to adopt evidence-based literacy instruction aligned with current research on how students learn to read. Programs should be selected from approved lists of scientifically-based reading programs.

**Analytic memo:** Illinois represents the weakest end of the statutory definition spectrum. 'Evidence-based' and 'scientifically-based' appear without definition, leaving the field open for curricula to self-certify compliance. 'Encouraged to adopt' (not mandated) further weakens enforcement. ExcelinEd (2025) specifically identifies this type of vague language as enabling non-compliant materials to remain in use. The absence of definitional content is itself a discursive act: it preserves local control while providing the appearance of SoR alignment — a political compromise encoded in language.

#### LEX-VA-01 — Virginia

- **Source:** VA · 2024 · Statute · Va. Code Ann. §22.1-253.13:1 (amended 2024)
- **Dimension:** LEX
- **Code:** LEX-5: Method Prohibition Language
- **Code definition:** Three-cueing named and prohibited by statute — the most direct legislative encoding of SoR's definitional boundary through exclusion
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → SUB-VA-01, PRE-VA-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> No teacher shall use or promote the use of the three-cueing system or any instructional approach that teaches students to use context clues, pictures, or sentence structure as the primary strategy for word identification in lieu of phonics-based decoding.

**Analytic memo:** Virginia's 2024 ban is among the most explicit in the corpus. The statute defines SoR's boundary through negation: what SoR is NOT (three-cueing) is codified before what it IS. 'In lieu of phonics-based decoding' is the operative qualifier — it targets displacement rather than any cueing use, preserving some teacher discretion while establishing phonics as the primary strategy. 'No teacher shall' is direct prohibitory language that shifts the subject position of teachers from professionals to regulated actors (→ SUB-VA-01). Note: Ohio (2023) and Georgia (2025) used similar prohibition language.

#### LEX-FL-01 — Florida

- **Source:** FL · 2023 · Statute · Fla. Stat. §1012.585 (amended 2023)
- **Dimension:** LEX
- **Code:** LEX-6: Crisis / Urgency Framing
- **Code definition:** 'Reading emergency' and 'crisis' in legislative findings — the strongest urgency lexis in the corpus
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → PRE-FL-01, SUB-FL-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The Legislature finds that Florida is in a state of reading emergency. Students are graduating from high school without the literacy skills needed to participate fully in civic and economic life. The current reading crisis demands immediate, evidence-based intervention grounded in the science of reading.

**Analytic memo:** Florida's legislative findings section deploys the most explicit crisis framing in the corpus — 'state of reading emergency' is extraordinary statutory language that borrows from disaster or public health emergency discourse. This lexical register justifies the scope of legislative intervention: if the situation is an emergency, normal deliberative caution is suspended. 'Graduating from high school without literacy skills' constructs the crisis as cumulative and systemic. Compare Georgia (2025): more measured findings language despite equally comprehensive legislation — suggesting urgency framing is a political choice, not a necessity.

#### LEX-MN-01 — Minnesota

- **Source:** MN · 2023 · Statute · Minn. Stat. §120B.12 (READ Act, 2023)
- **Dimension:** LEX
- **Code:** LEX-6: Crisis / Urgency Framing; LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor (partial)
- **Code definition:** Minnesota's findings section names equity dimensions — race, income, EL status, disability — most explicitly of any state; crisis framing directed at disparity rather than general failure
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → FOS-MN-01, PRE-MN-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The legislature finds that reading proficiency is foundational to all academic success and that persistent disparities in reading achievement by race, income, English learner status, and disability status represent an educational equity crisis requiring immediate, sustained, and culturally responsive intervention.

**Analytic memo:** Minnesota's READ Act is the most equity-explicit legislation in the corpus (Shanker, 2023). The findings language is structurally similar to Florida's crisis framing (LEX-6) but directed at specific populations rather than at 'every student.' 'Culturally responsive intervention' is the only instance in the corpus where statutory language acknowledges that SoR implementation must be adapted to cultural and linguistic contexts — a significant discursive departure from the universalising presuppositions dominating other states (→ PRE-MN-01, FOS-MN-01).

#### LEX-GA-01 — Georgia

- **Source:** GA · 2025 · Statute · O.C.G.A. §20-2-281.1 (signed March 2025)
- **Dimension:** LEX
- **Code:** LEX-3: Simple View / Research Body; LEX-6: Crisis / Urgency Framing (absent — notable)
- **Code definition:** Georgia explicitly rejects punitive framing in its findings language — the only state in the corpus to name and refuse the compliance paradigm
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → SUB-GA-01, PRE-GA-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The General Assembly finds that the science of reading reflects decades of converging evidence from cognitive science, linguistics, and educational research on how the brain learns to read, and that Georgia's educators deserve access to that evidence through high-quality professional learning rather than punitive compliance requirements.

**Analytic memo:** Georgia's 2025 findings section is distinctive in two ways. First, 'converging evidence from cognitive science, linguistics, and educational research' is the most epistemologically accurate statutory characterisation of the SoR research base — more faithful to the field's multi-disciplinary character than NRP Five Pillars (LEX-1) or IDA Structured Literacy (LEX-2). Second, 'deserve access... rather than punitive compliance requirements' explicitly names and refuses the accountability paradigm that Florida and Arkansas embed in their legislation. Passing with zero no votes, Georgia's framing shows that bipartisan consensus does not require crisis language.

### PRE — Presuppositions

#### PRE-MS-01 — Mississippi

- **Source:** MS · 2013/2019 · Statute · Miss. Code Ann. §37-177-3
- **Dimension:** PRE
- **Code:** PRE-1: Universal Learnability; PRE-2: Instructional Deficit
- **Code definition:** 'Every child can' as presupposition of neurological universality; failure framed as preventable — i.e., as a result of instruction
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-MS-01, FOS-MS-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Every child in Mississippi can learn to read at grade level when provided with effective, research-based reading instruction. Reading failure is not inevitable; it is a preventable outcome.

**Analytic memo:** 'Reading failure is not inevitable; it is a preventable outcome' is the most consequential presuppositional statement in the legislative corpus. It simultaneously encodes two claims as background truth: (1) all children have the neurological capacity to read (Universal Learnability) and (2) reading failure is caused by preventable — i.e., instructional — factors. Structural causes (poverty, school funding, class size, home language) are presupposed away as explanatory. This presupposition travels from Mississippi's statute into 34+ subsequent state laws and the popular press corpus virtually verbatim.

#### PRE-FL-01 — Florida

- **Source:** FL · 2023 · Statute · Fla. Stat. §1002.20 (parental rights provisions)
- **Dimension:** PRE
- **Code:** PRE-4: Research Consensus; PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap
- **Code definition:** 'Settled' and 'unambiguous' as presuppositional closures; teacher preparation framed as failure
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-FL-01, SUB-FL-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The science of reading is settled. The research base is unambiguous. Florida's teachers have not been adequately prepared to deliver research-based reading instruction, and this failure of preparation has resulted in preventable reading deficits across the state.

**Analytic memo:** Florida's statute uses 'settled' and 'unambiguous' as presuppositional operators — not arguments for consensus but declarations of it. This is the strongest version of PRE-4 in the corpus. 'Failure of preparation' explicitly presupposes that teacher education systems failed, constructing the knowledge gap as systemic (consistent with SUB-1 in the press corpus, where teachers are victims of their formation). Importantly, this presupposition justifies the scope of Florida's intervention: if the science is settled and teachers lack it, the state is obligated to act. No epistemic uncertainty is entertained.

#### PRE-AR-01 — Arkansas

- **Source:** AR · 2023 · Statute · Ark. Code Ann. §6-15-2901 (LEARNS Act)
- **Dimension:** PRE
- **Code:** PRE-4: Research Consensus; PRE-2: Instructional Deficit
- **Code definition:** 'Conclusively established' forecloses debate; 'denied access' frames reading failure as rights violation
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → SUB-AR-01, INT-AR-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Whereas decades of cognitive science research have conclusively established the most effective methods of reading instruction, and whereas Arkansas students have been denied access to this research through inadequate teacher preparation and the adoption of curricula inconsistent with the science of reading...

**Analytic memo:** Arkansas's LEARNS Act uses 'conclusively established' — the strongest epistemic closure language in the corpus — to presuppose research consensus. 'Denied access' is a rights-language presupposition: it constructs reading failure as a deprivation of something students were owed, activating a due-process rather than instructional-improvement frame. This language bridges PRE-4 (consensus) and PRE-2 (instructional deficit) by presupposing that the correct methods exist, are known, and have been withheld. The LEARNS Act also bundles SoR with school choice provisions — a political pairing that reveals the ideological valence of the accountability framing.

#### PRE-IL-01 — Illinois

- **Source:** IL · 2023 · State Board Guidance · ISBE Literacy Plan (2023)
- **Dimension:** PRE
- **Code:** PRE-5: Legislative Competence (absent — instructive)
- **Code definition:** Illinois guidance presupposes that local discretion is the appropriate mechanism — the counter-presupposition to legislative mandate
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-IL-01, SUB-IL-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Districts are expected to review their current reading programs and, where evidence-based alternatives are available and appropriate, consider transitioning to instructional approaches aligned with current research.

**Analytic memo:** Illinois's guidance is analytically valuable precisely for the presuppositions it does NOT carry. The modal verbs — 'expected to review,' 'consider transitioning' — presuppose local authority rather than legislative competence. 'Where... appropriate' presupposes that context matters — a counter to the universalising presuppositions of Mississippi, Florida, and Arkansas. Illinois guidance thus encodes a different theory of how change happens: through professional deliberation rather than statutory mandate. The weakness of this approach (identified by ExcelinEd as enabling non-compliance) is the inverse of its strength (respecting teacher and district expertise).

#### PRE-MN-01 — Minnesota

- **Source:** MN · 2023 · Statute · Minn. Stat. §120B.12(2) (READ Act)
- **Dimension:** PRE
- **Code:** PRE-1: Universal Learnability (modified); PRE-4: Research Consensus (qualified)
- **Code definition:** Minnesota qualifies the universalising presuppositions — the only state in the corpus to do so explicitly in statute
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-MN-01, FOS-MN-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The legislature further finds that no single approach to reading instruction is effective for all students, and that culturally and linguistically responsive implementation is necessary to achieve equitable outcomes for students of color, English learners, and students with disabilities.

**Analytic memo:** This is the most significant presuppositional departure in the legislative corpus. 'No single approach is effective for all students' explicitly contests PRE-1 (Universal Learnability through uniform instruction) and PRE-4 (settled consensus). Minnesota presupposes instead that: (a) student diversity is a material instructional variable, not a footnote; (b) culturally and linguistically responsive practice is empirically necessary, not optional; and (c) equitable outcomes require differentiated implementation. This presuppositional structure enables equity provisions that 34 other states lack — particularly for English Learners (Shanker, 2023: only 10 states address ELs in depth; Minnesota is the exemplar).

#### PRE-VA-01 — Virginia

- **Source:** VA · 2024 · Statute · Va. Code Ann. §22.1-253.13:1
- **Dimension:** PRE
- **Code:** PRE-2: Instructional Deficit; PRE-4: Research Consensus
- **Code definition:** Three-cueing named as the causal mechanism of failure — method as agent of harm in legislative findings
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-VA-01, INT-VA-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The Board finds that the three-cueing system contradicts the established science of how the brain processes print and has contributed to preventable reading difficulties in Virginia students.

**Analytic memo:** Virginia's findings locate causation with unusual specificity: not 'balanced literacy' generally, but 'the three-cueing system' specifically is identified as the agent of harm. 'Contradicts the established science of how the brain processes print' invokes neuroscience (orthographic mapping theory) as the warrant — a scientific claim that is accurate but represents a selective citation of the broader research base. 'Contributed to preventable reading difficulties' is a causal presupposition embedded in a legislative finding — establishing legal warrant for the prohibition that follows in the statute.

#### PRE-GA-01 — Georgia

- **Source:** GA · 2025 · Statute · O.C.G.A. §20-2-281.1
- **Dimension:** PRE
- **Code:** PRE-4: Research Consensus (qualified); PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap (reframed)
- **Code definition:** Georgia presupposes that SoR is a knowledge base requiring professional judgment — the most teacher-respecting presuppositional structure in the corpus
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-GA-01, SUB-GA-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The General Assembly further finds that the science of reading is not a single program or curriculum but a body of knowledge that requires educator understanding, professional judgment, and ongoing learning to implement effectively.

**Analytic memo:** Georgia's presuppositional structure is the most epistemologically sophisticated in the corpus. 'Not a single program or curriculum but a body of knowledge' directly contests the compliance-oriented presuppositions of Florida and Arkansas — presupposing that SoR cannot be delivered through approved program lists alone. 'Requires educator understanding, professional judgment, and ongoing learning' presupposes teacher expertise rather than teacher deficit (counter to PRE-3 dominant). This presuppositional frame enables Georgia's support-oriented rather than punitive-oriented policy instruments (→ SUB-GA-01).

### FOS — Foregrounding & Silencing

#### FOS-MS-01 — Mississippi

- **Source:** MS · 2013/2019 · Statute · Miss. Code Ann. §37-177-1
- **Dimension:** FOS
- **Code:** FOS-1: Phonics / Decoding Foregrounded; FOS-4: Background Knowledge Silenced
- **Code definition:** Phonological awareness, phonics, and decoding foregrounded as 'foundational'; other components implicitly derivative
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → PRE-MS-01, LEX-MS-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Reading instruction shall include systematic, explicit instruction in phonological awareness, phonics, and decoding as the foundational skills upon which all other literacy development depends.

**Analytic memo:** 'Foundational skills upon which all other literacy development depends' is a specific hierarchical claim: it encodes the Simple View's word recognition strand as the primary legislative object, positioning vocabulary, comprehension, and background knowledge as downstream. This is technically defensible for early grades — decoding is prerequisite — but the statute's framing has been interpreted as licensing a phonics-first approach across all grades, including middle school where comprehension and knowledge-building become the primary challenge (James Kim's argument). Background knowledge is entirely absent from the statutory definition.

#### FOS-MS-02 — Mississippi

- **Source:** MS · 2013/2019 · Statute · Miss. Code Ann. §37-177-5
- **Dimension:** FOS
- **Code:** FOS-3: Assessment / Screening Foregrounded
- **Code definition:** Universal screening and intervention foregrounded — Mississippi's assessment infrastructure is one reason its gains are considered multi-component, not phonics-only
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → INT-MS-01, PRE-MS-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Every school shall implement a universal screening program to identify students at risk of reading failure no later than the end of kindergarten. Students identified as at-risk shall receive additional targeted reading intervention services.

**Analytic memo:** Assessment and screening provisions are among the most extensively codified elements across the legislative corpus — present in 36+ states. Mississippi's early universal screening mandate (kindergarten) is cited by researchers as a key component of its reform that popular press coverage attributes solely to phonics instruction. The screening → identification → intervention pipeline is a genuine multi-component provision, yet statutory space devoted to comprehension supports, background knowledge curriculum, or oral language development is absent. Shanker (2023): assessment is prioritised; comprehensive student supports are not.

#### FOS-TN-01 — Tennessee

- **Source:** TN · 2023 · Statute · Tenn. Code Ann. §49-6-2906
- **Dimension:** FOS
- **Code:** FOS-2: Dyslexia Foregrounded; FOS-5: English Learners Silenced
- **Code definition:** Dyslexia and reading difficulties drive the intervention framework; EL provisions absent from this section
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → SUB-TN-01, INT-TN-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Students identified through universal screening as demonstrating risk for dyslexia or reading difficulties shall be entitled to structured literacy intervention, delivered by a trained interventionist, using an approved program from the state's structured literacy program list.

**Analytic memo:** Tennessee's intervention architecture centres dyslexia and reading difficulties — a pattern shared by 33 states (Shanker, 2023). The 'approved program list' mechanism forecloses teacher professional judgment in intervention selection, concentrating authority in state approval processes. English Learners do not appear in this provision, and Tennessee does not address ELs in depth elsewhere. The dyslexia-centred framing reflects the advocacy origins of SoR policy: dyslexia parent organisations were early and effective policy entrepreneurs, producing provisions that serve a specific diagnostic population while leaving EL, multilingual, and culturally diverse learners underaddressed.

#### FOS-MN-01 — Minnesota

- **Source:** MN · 2023 · Statute · Minn. Stat. §120B.12(3) (READ Act)
- **Dimension:** FOS
- **Code:** FOS-5: English Learners (Foregrounded — counter-example); FOS-7: Oral Language Foregrounded
- **Code definition:** Minnesota is the counter-example: EL provisions and oral language foregrounded — demonstrating that statutory silencing of these dimensions is a political choice, not an inevitability
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → PRE-MN-01, LEX-MN-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Reading instruction supported under this section shall be culturally and linguistically responsive, shall address oral language development and vocabulary as precursors to comprehension, and shall include provisions adapted for English learners at each stage of language acquisition.

**Analytic memo:** Minnesota's READ Act is the only state in the corpus to foreground all three silenced dimensions simultaneously: EL provisions at each stage of language acquisition, oral language development, and vocabulary as comprehension precursors. This is analytically significant because it demonstrates that the silences documented in FOS-5 and FOS-7 elsewhere are choices, not technical necessities. The provision is also the most research-consistent in the corpus: oral language and vocabulary are among the strongest predictors of reading comprehension. Minnesota's exceptionalism here reflects the political conditions of its enactment: a DFL majority with strong equity mandate.

#### FOS-KY-01 — Kentucky

- **Source:** KY · 2022 · Statute · KRS §158.792
- **Dimension:** FOS
- **Code:** FOS-7: Oral Language / Writing (Foregrounded — counter-example); FOS-1: Phonics Foregrounded
- **Code definition:** Kentucky is the only state in the corpus to explicitly integrate writing instruction — a significant departure from the decoding-only focus of most statutes
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → INT-KY-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Literacy instruction supported under this chapter shall integrate reading and writing as complementary processes and shall include explicit instruction in written composition alongside decoding and reading comprehension instruction.

**Analytic memo:** Kentucky's integration of writing instruction is unique in the corpus and is identified by the Shanker Institute as a model provision. Research on the reading-writing connection is strong (Graham & Hebert, 2011; Shanahan, 2016), yet 47 of 48 jurisdictions in this corpus either omit writing or mention it only in assessment contexts. Kentucky's provision demonstrates that the statutory silencing of writing is a political/drafting choice. The phrase 'complementary processes' encodes a research-consistent view of literacy as integrated — contrasting with statutes that treat reading as reducible to decoding.

#### FOS-IL-01 — Illinois

- **Source:** IL · 2023 · State Board Guidance · ISBE Literacy Plan
- **Dimension:** FOS
- **Code:** FOS-4: Background Knowledge / Comprehension Silenced; FOS-6: Structural Causes Silenced
- **Code definition:** Illinois's guidance omits background knowledge from the five components and reduces structural context to 'social-emotional and cultural considerations' — the softest possible framing
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-IL-01, PRE-IL-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Districts should ensure that reading instruction addresses the five components of reading. Districts should also consider the social-emotional and cultural contexts of their students when selecting instructional approaches.

**Analytic memo:** 'Social-emotional and cultural contexts' is the statutory displacement of structural analysis: it acknowledges that context matters but frames it as a consideration for implementation rather than as a causal factor in reading outcomes. Poverty, school funding, and structural racism are structurally absent. The five components reference omits background knowledge (following NRP's original omission) — perpetuating the research-to-policy gap that Shanker (2023) and Wexler (The Knowledge Gap) identify as consequential. Illinois guidance represents the minimal-intervention end of the corpus: acknowledging but not resolving the structural silences.

#### FOS-AR-01 — Arkansas

- **Source:** AR · 2023 · Statute · Ark. Code Ann. §6-15-2908 (LEARNS Act)
- **Dimension:** FOS
- **Code:** FOS-6: Structural Causes Silenced; FOS-1: Phonics Foregrounded (via approved list)
- **Code definition:** Arkansas's approved list mechanism centres curriculum compliance as the legislative object; structural conditions of reading failure are entirely absent
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → INT-AR-01, PRE-AR-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The Department shall maintain a list of approved reading curricula consistent with the science of reading. No school district shall use state funds to purchase or implement reading curricula not included on the approved list.

**Analytic memo:** The approved curriculum list is a governance mechanism that forecloses structural analysis by design: it makes the unit of intervention the curriculum product, not the conditions of schooling. 'No school district shall use state funds' is a fiscal enforcement mechanism that ties compliance to funding — concentrating legislative attention on what districts purchase rather than on poverty, class size, library access, or instructional time. Arkansas bundles this curriculum mandate with school choice provisions in the LEARNS Act, revealing that curriculum compliance and market expansion are co-present legislative objects.

### SUB — Subject Positioning

#### SUB-FL-01 — Florida

- **Source:** FL · 2023 · Statute · Fla. Stat. §1012.585
- **Dimension:** SUB
- **Code:** SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated
- **Code definition:** Certification denial constructs teachers as deficient until the state certifies their knowledge — the most punitive teacher subject position in the corpus
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-FL-01, PRE-FL-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Beginning July 1, 2024, no teacher shall receive a professional certificate renewal unless they have completed approved training in the science of reading as specified by the State Board of Education. Teachers who have not completed such training are prohibited from serving as the teacher of record for reading instruction.

**Analytic memo:** Florida's certification renewal requirement is the most consequential teacher subject-positioning act in the legislative corpus: it constructs teachers as deficient (lacking SoR knowledge) and conditionally competent (re-certified only after state-approved training). 'Prohibited from serving as teacher of record' is a regulatory exclusion — the legal enactment of the teacher deficit subject position. This contrasts sharply with Georgia's formulation (SUB-GA-01), which constructs teachers as professionals deserving access to knowledge. Both states enact SoR; the subject positions they construct for teachers are opposite.

#### SUB-GA-01 — Georgia

- **Source:** GA · 2025 · Statute · O.C.G.A. §20-2-281.1
- **Dimension:** SUB
- **Code:** SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional
- **Code definition:** Georgia explicitly names and refuses the SUB-1 deficit positioning — constructing teachers as professionals the system failed
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-GA-01, PRE-GA-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The General Assembly finds that Georgia's educators are not the problem; the systems that prepared and supported them are. Accordingly, this Act shall be implemented through high-quality professional learning, coaching, and collaborative inquiry rather than punitive compliance requirements.

**Analytic memo:** 'Georgia's educators are not the problem; the systems that prepared and supported them are' is the most explicit counter-subject-positioning statement in the corpus. It directly names the deficit positioning (teachers as problem) and refuses it, substituting a systemic accountability frame (teacher education systems as the problem). 'High-quality professional learning, coaching, and collaborative inquiry rather than punitive compliance' enacts the alternative: professional development as support, not regulation. Georgia's unanimity (0 no votes) suggests this framing commands broader coalition than the deficit model.

#### SUB-AR-01 — Arkansas

- **Source:** AR · 2023 · Statute · Ark. Code Ann. §6-15-2907 (LEARNS Act)
- **Dimension:** SUB
- **Code:** SUB-4: Parent as Rights-Holder
- **Code definition:** Arkansas constructs parental rights to information about reading instruction — bundling SoR with a transparency/parental rights discourse
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-AR-01, INT-AR-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Parents and guardians have the right to access information about their child's reading level, the instructional methods used in their child's classroom, and the research basis for those methods. Schools shall provide this information upon request.

**Analytic memo:** Arkansas's parental rights provision constructs parents as consumers with rights to product information (instructional method) rather than as partners in educational communities. This subject position is politically significant: it allies SoR reform with the parental rights discourse that animated school choice provisions in the LEARNS Act. The provision creates a lever for parents to challenge non-compliant curricula — a mechanism for market pressure on district decisions. Similar provisions appear in Florida, South Carolina, and Virginia, consistently co-present with school choice legislation.

#### SUB-MS-01 — Mississippi

- **Source:** MS · 2013/2019 · Statute · Miss. Code Ann. §37-177-9
- **Dimension:** SUB
- **Code:** SUB-3: Student as Victim / Beneficiary; SUB-5: District as Compliance Target
- **Code definition:** Third-grade retention constructs students as objects of accountability policy — and districts as compliance targets responsible for preventing it
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → FOS-MS-01, PRE-MS-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Students who are not reading proficiently at the end of third grade shall not be promoted to fourth grade, except under limited conditions specified by the State Board of Education.

**Analytic memo:** Third-grade retention is the most consequential student subject-positioning mechanism in the corpus: it makes students the enforcement locus of literacy policy. Students are constructed as beneficiaries (retention prevents a student from advancing before they're ready) and simultaneously as accountability objects (their scores determine district compliance). Research on the effectiveness and equity impacts of retention is mixed (RAND, 2024) — high-quality instruction and early intervention are more effective — but the provision persists because it makes the stakes of non-compliance visible and measurable. Eleven states have similar provisions.

#### SUB-TN-01 — Tennessee

- **Source:** TN · 2023 · Statute · Tenn. Code Ann. §49-6-2908
- **Dimension:** SUB
- **Code:** SUB-6: Publisher / Curriculum as Suspect; SUB-5: District as Compliance Target
- **Code definition:** Curriculum programs constructed as suspect until state-certified; 18-month removal timeline enacts gatekeeping urgency
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → INT-TN-01, FOS-TN-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> All approved literacy intervention programs shall demonstrate alignment with structured literacy principles as defined by the International Dyslexia Association. Programs not meeting this standard shall be removed from the state approved list within 18 months of this Act's effective date.

**Analytic memo:** Tennessee's program removal provision constructs curriculum publishers as presumptively suspect: any program not meeting IDA-aligned standards is to be removed from the approved list. This is an indirect but consequential subject-positioning of curriculum markets: publishers are constructed as actors whose products require state verification, not professional trust. The 18-month timeline creates urgency that disadvantages smaller or newer providers — potentially consolidating the market around large established SoR-aligned publishers. Reading Recovery's lawsuit against Ohio's similar provision (2023) illustrates the legal and market stakes.

#### SUB-IL-01 — Illinois

- **Source:** IL · 2023 · State Board Guidance · ISBE Literacy Plan
- **Dimension:** SUB
- **Code:** SUB-5: District as Compliance Target (inverted — district as autonomous agent)
- **Code definition:** Illinois constructs districts as autonomous decision-makers — the counter-subject-position to state-as-arbiter
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-IL-01, PRE-IL-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Districts are responsible for reviewing their reading programs and making locally-informed decisions about instructional approaches that best serve their student populations.

**Analytic memo:** Illinois's guidance constructs districts as locally-accountable agents rather than compliance targets. 'Locally-informed decisions' and 'best serve their student populations' presuppose local expertise and contextual judgment. This subject position is the structural opposite of Arkansas's approved-list mechanism (SUB-5 dominant) or Florida's certification denial (SUB-1 dominant). It reflects a theory of change that respects local professional capacity — but ExcelinEd (2025) identifies this as enabling non-compliance: without state arbitration, non-SoR-aligned programs persist unchallenged.

#### SUB-VA-01 — Virginia

- **Source:** VA · 2024 · Statute · Va. Code Ann. §22.1-253.13:1
- **Dimension:** SUB
- **Code:** SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated; SUB-7: State as Arbiter
- **Code definition:** Statutory prohibition on named method constructs teachers as regulated instructional actors; employment documentation as enforcement mechanism
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-VA-01, PRE-VA-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> No teacher shall use or promote the use of the three-cueing system. Violation of this section may be reported to the division superintendent and shall be documented in the teacher's employment file.

**Analytic memo:** Virginia's prohibition constructs teachers as regulatory targets whose instructional decisions are monitored and documented. 'Shall be documented in the teacher's employment file' is an employment sanction mechanism — it makes pedagogical choice a matter of personnel record. This is the most direct enactment of SUB-1 (teacher as regulated actor) in the corpus: not merely requiring training (Florida) or specifying approved programs (Tennessee), but prohibiting a named practice on pain of employment sanction. The subject position constructed here — teacher as licensed practitioner whose methods are legally regulated — has no parallel in other professions at this level of specificity.

### INT — Intertextuality

#### INT-MS-01 — Mississippi

- **Source:** MS · 2013/2019 · Statute · Miss. Code Ann. §37-177-1
- **Dimension:** INT
- **Code:** INT-1: NRP / Federal Report Citation
- **Code definition:** NRP (2000) cited by name as the legislative authority — foundational intertextual act that 34 subsequent states replicate
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-MS-01, PRE-MS-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Reading instruction shall be grounded in the five components identified by the National Reading Panel in its 2000 report, Teaching Children to Read, as the foundational elements of effective reading instruction.

**Analytic memo:** Mississippi's explicit citation of the NRP 2000 report is the most consequential intertextual act in the legislative corpus. By naming the NRP report as the statutory authority, Mississippi delegates the definition of reading science to a 25-year-old federal panel synthesis — one that was itself contested on methodological grounds at publication (Yatvin dissent). This citation travels into 34+ subsequent state statutes, often without the NRP being named again — its presence presupposed as background. The NRP's Five Pillars thus become not a research framework but a legal definition, acquiring a stability and finality the original report did not claim.

#### INT-TN-01 — Tennessee

- **Source:** TN · 2023 · Statute · Tenn. Code Ann. §49-6-2904
- **Dimension:** INT
- **Code:** INT-2: IDA / Professional Organisation Citation
- **Code definition:** IDA cited as definitional authority — delegating legislative meaning to a private organisation with a specific constituency
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-TN-01, FOS-TN-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> ..consistent with the science of reading, and aligned with the definition adopted by the International Dyslexia Association.

**Analytic memo:** Tennessee's citation of the IDA is an extraordinary intertextual act: it delegates the legal definition of structured literacy to a private professional organisation whose primary constituency is dyslexia researchers and practitioners. This means that IDA's future definitional revisions could alter Tennessee's statutory meaning without legislative action. The IDA's definition — 'explicit, systematic, sequential, cumulative, diagnostic' — is more restrictive than NRP's Five Pillars and is rooted in dyslexia intervention research, raising the question of whether it is appropriate as a universal pedagogical framework. Five states make this delegation; the others use NRP or the Simple View.

#### INT-AR-01 — Arkansas

- **Source:** AR · 2023 · Statute · LEARNS Act legislative record / committee testimony
- **Dimension:** INT
- **Code:** INT-5: Media / Advocacy Intertextuality
- **Code definition:** Hanford's journalism cited directly in legislative testimony — journalism entering statute as evidence
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → LEX-AR-01, PRE-AR-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Testimony before the Arkansas Education Committee cited Emily Hanford's Sold a Story podcast as evidence of the need for reform, with committee members referencing the podcast by name during floor debate.

**Analytic memo:** Arkansas's legislative record provides one of the clearest documented cases of journalism functioning as direct policy evidence. Committee members citing Sold a Story by name during floor debate constitutes an extraordinary intertextual event: audio journalism is imported into statutory process as empirical warrant. This mirrors the cross-citation pattern in the press corpus (INT-3) but here the destination is law, not another media outlet. ExcelinEd and NCTQ advocacy reports are also traceable in the LEARNS Act's structure — the advocacy-to-legislation pipeline is unusually compressed.

#### INT-OH-01 — Ohio

- **Source:** OH · 2023 · Statute / Legal Record · Ohio Rev. Code §3313.608; Reading Recovery v. Ohio Dept. of Ed. (2023)
- **Dimension:** INT
- **Code:** INT-7: Contested Intertextuality
- **Code definition:** Reading Recovery named and prohibited by statute — contested intertextuality with legal consequences; the lawsuit is itself an intertextual event
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → SUB-TN-01, LEX-VA-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> The Ohio legislature specifically named Reading Recovery as a program inconsistent with the science of reading and prohibited its use with state funds. Reading Recovery subsequently filed suit challenging the prohibition as a violation of due process and academic freedom.

**Analytic memo:** Ohio's explicit naming and prohibition of Reading Recovery is the most consequential instance of contested intertextuality in the corpus. By naming Reading Recovery in statute, Ohio transforms a professional and pedagogical debate into a legal one. Reading Recovery's lawsuit challenges the legislative power to define instructional validity — a question of constitutional as well as educational significance. The lawsuit itself becomes an intertextual event: it is cited in subsequent state legislative debates as a cautionary or validating precedent, depending on the interlocutor. ExcelinEd (2025) frames the lawsuit as evidence of 'resistance to evidence'; Reading Recovery frames it as defence of professional autonomy.

#### INT-VA-01 — Virginia

- **Source:** VA · 2024 · Legislative Record · Virginia House of Delegates Education Committee testimony (2024)
- **Dimension:** INT
- **Code:** INT-4: Research Literature Citation
- **Code definition:** Named researchers (Stanovich, Dehaene) cited in legislative record — specific intertextuality grounding the prohibition in named science
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → PRE-VA-01, LEX-VA-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Committee testimony cited the work of Stanovich (1986) on the Matthew Effect and Dehaene (2009) on the reading brain as scientific warrant for the three-cueing prohibition.

**Analytic memo:** Virginia's committee record is unusual in the corpus for citing named researchers — most state legislative records cite NRP generically or do not name researchers at all. Stanovich's Matthew Effect (1986) and Dehaene's Reading in the Brain (2009) are canonical SoR texts; their appearance in legislative testimony reflects advocacy organisations' capacity to translate research into policy language. Notably, neither Stanovich nor Dehaene specifically addressed the three-cueing system in the cited works — their research on the importance of early decoding is being extended to a policy prohibition that the original research did not directly support.

#### INT-DE-01 — Delaware

- **Source:** DE · 2022 · Statute / Policy · Delaware Reads (2022 Policy Framework)
- **Dimension:** INT
- **Code:** INT-3: Interstate Intertextuality
- **Code definition:** Delaware explicitly cites Louisiana as model — demonstrating interstate policy diffusion and the role of NAEP data as intertextual evidence
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → PRE-MS-01, FOS-MS-02

**Raw excerpt:**

> Delaware's framework explicitly models its curriculum-professional development alignment approach on Louisiana's HQIM+LETRS model, citing Louisiana's NAEP gains as evidence of the model's effectiveness.

**Analytic memo:** Delaware's explicit citation of Louisiana demonstrates the interstate intertextuality that characterises SoR policy diffusion. Louisiana's 2021–23 HQIM (High-Quality Instructional Materials) + LETRS model is cited across multiple states as the Mississippi analogue for curriculum-PD alignment. The Shanker Institute identifies Delaware as an exemplar for curriculum-PD alignment specifically because of this intertextual borrowing. NAEP data functions here as intertextual evidence — state-level test score trends are cited as policy warrants, transforming assessment data into interstate legislative argument.

#### INT-KY-01 — Kentucky

- **Source:** KY · 2022 · Statute · KRS §158.792 (legislative record)
- **Dimension:** INT
- **Code:** INT-4: Research Literature Citation; INT-6: Historical Intertextuality (NRP as outdated precedent)
- **Code definition:** Kentucky cites post-NRP research to justify going beyond the NRP Five Pillars — using research intertextuality to contest the dominant NRP anchor
- **Cross-dimension flag:** Y → FOS-KY-01, LEX-MS-01

**Raw excerpt:**

> Kentucky's integration of writing instruction is grounded in the research synthesis by Graham and Hebert (2011), Writing to Read, which demonstrated that writing instruction improves reading comprehension. This finding is not reflected in the NRP report and represents an update to the foundational research cited by most states.

**Analytic memo:** Kentucky's citation of Graham and Hebert (2011) is analytically significant: it uses named post-NRP research to justify a statutory provision that most states omit. This is an instance of research intertextuality being used to update the dominant legislative template rather than merely reproduce it. By implication, the NRP (2000) citation that anchors 34 states is constructed as an incomplete historical precedent rather than a settled authority — a subtle but important intertextual challenge to the dominant frame. Kentucky's writing provision is the only instance in the corpus where a state moves beyond the NRP framework by citing more recent research.
