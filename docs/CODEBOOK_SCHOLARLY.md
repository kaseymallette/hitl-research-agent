# CDA CODEBOOK — Scholarly Publications Corpus

DRAFT for review. The codes and definitions of the legislation codebook
([CODEBOOK.md](CODEBOOK.md)), without its examples. Examples from scholarly
articles are added after the pilot.

- **Study:** Literacy Science in Translation: A Cross-Domain Analysis of How Research, Legislation, and Popular Media Construct the Science of Reading
- **Corpus:** Scholarly Publications (Corpus 1 of 3) — pilot
- **Analytical framework:** Critical Discourse Analysis (Fairclough, 2003; Rogers, 2011)

## CDA dimensions

- **LEX:** Lexical Choices & Definitional Framing — how SoR is named, bounded, and defined in statutory language
- **PRE:** Presuppositions — claims taken for granted in legislative findings and intent sections
- **FOS:** Foregrounding & Silencing — what the legislation centres vs. omits
- **SUB:** Subject Positioning — how teachers, students, parents, and districts are constructed in law
- **INT:** Intertextuality — which research, texts, and authorities statutes cite, invoke, or exclude

## Codes

A code applies where the passage it cites shows what the definition describes.

### LEX — Lexical Choices & Definitional Framing

- **LEX-1: Five Pillars Anchor** — SoR defined by reference to NRP's five pillars (phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension): the five components are given as the definition or foundation of reading instruction, whether or not the National Reading Panel is named. *(draft)*
- **LEX-2: Structured Literacy / IDA Definition** — SoR defined via IDA's Structured Literacy framework: instruction defined as "structured literacy", by its descriptors (explicit, systematic, sequential, cumulative), or by reference to the International Dyslexia Association's definition. *(draft)*
- **LEX-3: Simple View / Research Body** — SoR defined by reference to the Simple View of Reading (reading as the product of word recognition and language comprehension), or characterized as a body of research rather than a list of components or a program. *(draft)*
- **LEX-4: Vague / Evidence-Based Language** — 'Evidence-based,' 'scientifically-based,' or 'research-based' set as the standard for instruction or materials without a definition, a named source, or criteria for what qualifies. *(draft)*
- **LEX-5: Method Prohibition Language** — Explicit statutory naming and prohibition of specific instructional methods or programs (such as three-cueing or Reading Recovery), defining SoR by what it excludes. A general ban on materials that are "not evidence-based" is LEX-4. *(draft)*
- **LEX-6: Crisis / Urgency Framing** — Legislative findings sections deploy crisis, failure, or emergency language ("crisis", "emergency", "failing") to characterize current reading outcomes and justify the scope or speed of intervention. *(draft)*

### PRE — Presuppositions

- **PRE-1: Universal Learnability** — 'Every child can learn to read' taken for granted: the text assumes all students can reach proficiency given the right instruction, as background rather than as a claim it supports. *(draft)*
- **PRE-2: Instructional Deficit** — Current reading outcomes presupposed to result primarily from instruction (methods, curricula, or teacher preparation), so that low achievement is treated as preventable by changing instruction, and other causes go unstated. *(draft)*
- **PRE-3: Teacher Knowledge Gap** — Teachers presupposed to lack knowledge of reading science, so that training, certification, or retraining requirements are presented as the remedy. *(draft)*
- **PRE-4: Research Consensus** — SoR's findings presupposed to be settled, unanimous, or conclusive ("settled", "conclusively established", "the science"), with no acknowledgment of debate, limits, or ongoing research. *(draft)*
- **PRE-5: Legislative Competence** — Legislatures presupposed to be appropriate arbiters of instructional method: choosing, mandating, or banning methods and materials is treated as a matter for the legislature or state agency rather than for professional or local judgment. *(draft)*
- **PRE-6: Mississippi Transferability** — Mississippi's multi-component reform presupposed to transfer: Mississippi's outcomes, or one component of its reform, invoked as evidence that the adopting state's provisions will work, without the conditions of Mississippi's reform. *(draft)*

### FOS — Foregrounding & Silencing

- **FOS-1: Phonics / Decoding Foregrounded** — Phonics and decoding instruction centred as the primary or foundational element of reading instruction, in definitions, mandates, or the share of provisions devoted to it, with other components positioned as secondary or downstream. *(draft)*
- **FOS-2: Dyslexia Foregrounded** — Dyslexia screening, identification, and intervention given central place, so that the framework for struggling readers is organized around dyslexia. *(draft)*
- **FOS-3: Assessment / Screening Foregrounded** — Universal screening and diagnostic assessment mandates given central place: the screen, identify, and intervene sequence as a primary legislative mechanism. *(draft)*
- **FOS-4: Background Knowledge / Comprehension Silenced** — Background knowledge, knowledge-building curriculum, and comprehension instruction absent or minimal where the text defines or lists what reading instruction must include. *(draft)*
- **FOS-5: English Learners Silenced** — EL-specific research, translanguaging, and multilingual learners' needs absent where the text sets instruction, screening, or intervention requirements that apply to them. *(draft)*
- **FOS-6: Structural Causes Silenced** — Poverty, school funding, class size, and racial segregation absent as causes of reading outcomes where the text explains outcomes or sets remedies, leaving instruction or curriculum as the only lever. *(draft)*
- **FOS-7: Oral Language / Writing Silenced** — Oral language development and writing instruction absent where the text defines or lists what literacy instruction must include. *(draft)*

A silencing code (FOS-4 to FOS-7) applies only where the text defines, lists,
or explains the area the topic belongs to, so that its absence is a choice the
text makes. Cite that passage and say what it addresses instead. Where the
topic is present, use the code with *(counter-example)*. *(draft)*

### SUB — Subject Positioning

- **SUB-1: Teacher as Deficient / Regulated** — Teachers constructed as lacking knowledge and therefore subject to regulation: required training, conditions on certification, prohibitions on their methods, or sanctions. *(draft)*
- **SUB-2: Teacher as Developing Professional** — Teachers constructed as professionals deserving support, whose judgment and learning are the means of implementation (professional learning, coaching, collaboration) rather than objects of compliance. *(draft)*
- **SUB-3: Student as Victim / Beneficiary** — Students — especially Black, low-income, and dyslexic students — constructed as harmed by current instruction and as beneficiaries of reform, or as the objects of accountability measures such as retention. *(draft)*
- **SUB-4: Parent as Rights-Holder** — Parents constructed as rights-holders with legal entitlements to information about, notice of, or a say in their child's reading instruction. *(draft)*
- **SUB-5: District as Compliance Target** — Local school districts and administrators constructed as compliance targets: required to adopt, purchase, or report as the state directs, with funding or approval contingent on compliance. *(draft)*
- **SUB-6: Publisher / Curriculum as Suspect** — Curriculum publishers and specific programs implicated in reading failure or treated as suspect until approved: approval lists, removal provisions, or programs named as inconsistent with the science of reading. *(draft)*
- **SUB-7: State as Rescuer / Arbiter** — The state legislature or education agency constructed as the authority that defines valid instruction, approves materials and training, and corrects local failure. *(draft)*

### INT — Intertextuality

- **INT-1: NRP / Federal Report Citation** — National Reading Panel (2000) cited as the foundation for the text's definition or requirements, by name, as is any other federal report cited that way. Listing the five components without naming the report is LEX-1 only. *(draft)*
- **INT-2: IDA / Professional Organisation Citation** — International Dyslexia Association cited as definitional authority, or another professional or advocacy organisation's standard adopted by reference, so that the organisation's definition governs the text's meaning. *(draft)*
- **INT-3: Interstate Intertextuality** — One state's legislation explicitly or implicitly cites or models another state's: naming it as a model, citing its outcomes as evidence, or reproducing its provisions. Within a single document, code only explicit references; implicit borrowing needs a comparison across the corpus. *(draft)*
- **INT-4: Research Literature Citation** — Peer-reviewed research, named researchers, or specific studies cited in the text or its legislative record as warrant for a provision. *(draft)*
- **INT-5: Media / Advocacy Intertextuality** — Emily Hanford's journalism or advocacy organisations' reports (such as ExcelinEd or NCTQ) cited or invoked as evidence in the text or its legislative record. *(draft)*
- **INT-6: Historical Intertextuality** — References to Reading First, NCLB, or other historical reading policies or reports, as precedent to follow or as a past approach the text corrects or moves beyond. *(draft)*
- **INT-7: Contested Intertextuality** — Texts or authorities cited by SoR advocates specifically to discredit opposing approaches, or programs and texts the statute names as inconsistent with the science of reading, including legal challenges that follow. *(draft)*

### Code qualifiers

A code can carry a qualifier in parentheses when the text relates to its
pattern in a particular way. *(draft)*

- **(counter-example)** — the text does the opposite of the code's pattern: a silencing code where the topic is foregrounded.
- **(absent — notable)** — the pattern is missing where comparable texts carry it, and the absence is meaningful. Cite the passage where the pattern would be expected.
- **(qualified)** — the text carries the pattern but limits it.
- **(reframed)** — the text carries a revised version of the pattern.
- **(inverted)** — the text constructs the opposite subject position.
- **(partial)** — only part of the pattern is present.
